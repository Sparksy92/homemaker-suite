# 12.1 The Big Three

These three plants are abundant across North America, easy to identify, and nutritionally dense. Master these before moving to anything else. They're your foundational wild foods.

---

## 1. Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale)

**Why it matters:** Every part edible. Grows everywhere. Bitter greens signal liver support.

### Identification (Foolproof Features)
- **Leaves:** Jagged "lion's teeth" edges, grow in a basal rosette (from the center, not a stem)
- **Flower:** Bright yellow, made of many tiny ray florets (not a single flower)
- **Seed head:** The iconic white "wish puff"
- **Stem:** Hollow, exudes white milky sap when broken
- **Root:** Long taproot, white inside

**No poisonous look-alikes.** If it has the milky sap + hollow stem + jagged leaves, it's dandelion.

### Harvest & Use

| Part | Best Season | Preparation | Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **Leaves** | Early spring (before flowers) | Raw in salad, or blanched 2 min to reduce bitterness | Rich in Vitamin A, K, calcium |
| **Flowers** | Spring/Summer | Remove green base (bitter), fry petals in batter, or make wine | Vitamin C, antioxidants |
| **Root** | Fall (after first frost) | Roast until dark, grind for coffee substitute | Prebiotic fiber, liver support |

**Bitterness hack:** Harvest in early morning (less bitter) or soak leaves in cold salt water for 30 minutes.

## 2. Plantain (Plantago major / P. lanceolata)

**Why it matters:** The "band-aid plant." Grows in compacted soil (driveways, trails). Medicinal powerhouse.

### Identification

**Broadleaf Plantain (P. major):**
- **Leaves:** Oval, ribbed with 5-7 parallel veins (distinctive—run your finger along them)
- **Grows in:** A basal rosette, low to ground
- **Flower stalk:** Thin spike with tiny greenish flowers

**Narrowleaf Plantain (P. lanceolata):**
- **Leaves:** Long and narrow (lance-shaped), same parallel veins
- **Flower head:** Compact cylindrical cluster on a long stalk

**Key feature both share:** The stringy veins. Tear a leaf—you'll see the veins stretch like strings. Nothing else does this.

### Harvest & Use

**Edible (young leaves only):**
- Harvest before flowering (they get fibrous later)
- Chop and add to stir-fries or soups
- Mild flavor, slightly mucilaginous (good for soothing gut)

**Medicinal (the main use):**
1. **"Spit poultice" for bites/stings:**
   - Chew fresh leaf into a pulp
   - Apply directly to bee sting, mosquito bite, or minor cut
   - The enzymes draw out toxins and reduce swelling (works in minutes)

2. **Infused oil for salves:**
   - Fill jar with chopped plantain
   - Cover with olive oil
   - Let sit in sun for 2 weeks
   - Strain, use for cuts, scrapes, rashes

**Why it works:** Contains allantoin (promotes cell growth) and aucubin (antimicrobial).

## 3. Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica)

**Why it matters:** One of the most nutrient-dense greens on the planet. Protein, iron, calcium, magnesium. The sting is temporary; the nutrition is forever.

### Identification (Respect Required)

- **Leaves:** Heart-shaped, serrated edges, opposite arrangement on square stem
- **Stem:** Square in cross-section (roll it between your fingers)
- **Height:** 3-7 feet tall when mature
- **The sting:** Covered in tiny hollow hairs that inject formic acid on contact (like a bee sting, but milder)

**Harvesting safely:**
- Wear **leather gloves** (cloth won't work—the hairs penetrate)
- Cut top 4-6 inches in spring (before flowering)
- Use scissors or knife, don't grab

**Disarming the sting:**
- **Cooking:** 30 seconds of boiling destroys the stinging hairs completely
- **Drying:** Dehydrating also neutralizes the sting
- **Blending:** Pureeing breaks the hairs

### Use

**As food:**
- **Nettle soup:** Boil in broth, blend smooth, add cream or coconut milk
- **Tea:** Steep dried leaves for mineral-rich infusion (tastes like mild green tea)
- **Sautéed:** Blanch 1 minute, then sauté like spinach with garlic

**Nutritional profile (per 100g cooked):**
- Protein: 6g (higher than most greens)
- Iron: 4mg (30% DV)
- Calcium: 481mg (48% DV)
- Magnesium: 71mg (17% DV)

**Medicinal:**
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Used for arthritis pain (freeze-dried capsules or tea)
- **Allergy relief:** Ironically, nettle tea reduces histamine response (take during allergy season)

## The 15-Minute Wild Salad

**Goal:** Make a complete meal from the Big Three.

**Harvest:**
- 2 cups young dandelion leaves
- 1 cup young plantain leaves
- 1 cup nettle tops (blanched and chopped)

**Preparation:**
1. Blanch nettle in boiling water for 1 minute, shock in cold water, chop
2. Soak dandelion in salt water for 10 minutes (reduces bitterness)
3. Chop plantain into ribbons

**Dressing:**
- 2 tbsp olive oil (or rendered fat)
- 1 tbsp vinegar (or lemon juice)
- 1 tsp honey
- Pinch of salt

**Nutritional yield:** ~15g protein, 60% daily calcium, 40% iron, Vitamins A, C, K.

## Safety Reminder

**Pollution awareness:**
- Don't harvest within 50 feet of roads (lead/oil contamination)
- Avoid chemically treated lawns (pesticides)
- Skip areas where dogs frequent (waste contamination)

**The 100% rule:**
- If you're not 100% certain of the ID, **don't eat it**
- Use multiple sources (books, apps, mentors) to confirm
- Start with small amounts even when certain (test for allergies)

**These three plants are impossible to confuse with anything dangerous.** Once you learn them, you'll see them everywhere. They're free, abundant, and waiting for you to notice.
