# 15.7 DIY Solar Dehydrator: Preserving the Harvest

Solar dehydration is the most energy-efficient way to preserve surplus garden yields, meat, and foraged goods without the need for electricity. Unlike traditional sun-drying, a dehydrator protects food from insects, sudden rain, and UV bleaching while accelerating the process through controlled convection.

## 1. Principles of Solar Dehydration
A successful solar dehydrator operates on three main physical principles:
1.  **Capturing Heat:** Solar radiation enters through a transparent cover (glancing) and is absorbed by dark surfaces, heating the air inside.
2.  **Convection (The Chimney Effect):** Heated air is less dense and rises naturally. By placing the air intake at the bottom and the exhaust at the top, we create a continuous stream of dry air.
3.  **Moisture Removal:** As the warm air passes over the food, it absorbs moisture. This moist air must be expelled quickly to prevent "case hardening" or mold.

## 2. Recommended Design: The Indirect Collector
We recommend the **Indirect Collector** design because it separates the heating area from the drying chamber. This allows you to dry sensitive items (like medicinal herbs or greens) without direct sunlight destroying their potency.

### Architecture:
*   **The Solar Collector:** A long, low-profile box tilted toward the sun.
*   **The Drying Cabinet:** A vertical box with multiple tray levels, attached to the top of the collector.

## 3. Construction Guide

### Materials List:
*   **Frame:** 1/2" or 3/4" Exterior-grade Plywood.
*   **Glazing:** Tempered glass or Polycarbonate (Twin-wall is best for insulation).
*   **Absorber Plate:** Aluminum flashing painted with high-heat matte black paint.
*   **Trays:** Food-grade stainless steel mesh or nylon "tulle" (avoid galvanized or fiberglass).
*   **Vents:** Fine aluminum window screen (to exclude flies and wasps).

### Assembly Steps:
1.  **Build the Collector:** Construct a box roughly 2ft wide and 4ft long. Line the bottom with thin insulation and place the black absorber plate on top. Seal with glazing.
2.  **Build the Cabinet:** Construct a vertical box (~3ft tall) with internal cleats to hold 5-8 trays. Cut a large opening at the bottom of the cabinet that matches the top of the collector.
3.  **Join the Units:** Attach the collector to the cabinet at a 30-45 degree angle. Ensure the seal is airtight.
4.  **Ventilation:** Cut a 2" wide intake at the bottom of the collector and a 3" wide exhaust vent at the very top of the cabinet.
5.  **Finish:** Coat the exterior with a light-colored, weather-resistant paint to reflect excess heat from the cabinet walls.

## 4. Optimal Drying Temperatures
*   **Herbs:** 95°F - 105°F
*   **Vegetables:** 125°F - 135°F
*   **Fruits:** 135°F - 145°F
*   **Meat (Jerky):** 145°F - 155°F (Requires careful monitoring for safety)

### Survival Tip:
If the temperature exceeds 150°F, you are "cooking" the food rather than drying it, which can seal moisture inside. Open the top vent further or partially shade the collector to regulate temp.

---
## 5. Maintenance & Safety
*   **Sanitation:** Scrub trays with vinegar and water after every use.
*   **Monitoring:** Use a simple probe thermometer inserted into the top vent to monitor internal cabinet temps.
*   **Storage:** Dehydrated food is shelf-stable only if kept in airtight containers (jars/mylar) in a cool, dark place.
