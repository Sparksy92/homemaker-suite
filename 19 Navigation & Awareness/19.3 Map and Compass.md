# 19.3 Map and Compass: The Lost Art

**Concept:** GPS satellites are fragile. Batteries die. The magnetic field of the earth is reliable. If you can read a map and shoot a bearing, you are never truly lost.

## 1. The Anatomy of a Compass
*   **Baseplate:** The clear plastic rectangle.
*   **Direction of Travel Arrow:** Points where you want to go.
*   **Bezel (Dial):** Rotates 360 degrees.
*   **Needle:** The red and white floating arrow. Red ALWAYS points North.
*   **Orienteering Lines:** Parallel lines inside the bezel housing.

## 2. Reading a Topographic Map
A normal map shows roads. A topo map shows the *shape* of the land.

*   **Contour Lines:** Lines that connect points of equal elevation.
    *   **Close together** = Steep terrain (cliff/hill).
    *   **Far apart** = Flat terrain (field/valley).
    *   **V-shape pointing upstream** = A river or draw.
    *   **Circles** = The peak of a hill.
*   **Scale:** Know your scale. 1:24,000 is standard (1 inch = 2000 feet).

## 3. Usage 1: Orienting the Map
Before you move, match the map to the real world.
1.  Turn the compass dial so "N" aligns with the Direction of Travel Arrow.
2.  Place the compass on the map. Align the edge with the side of the map (Grid North).
3.  **Rotate the entire map** (with the compass on it) until the Red Needle fits inside the "Shed" (the orienting arrow on the clear housing).
4.  Now, the map is aligned with reality. Left on the map is left in real life.

## 4. Usage 2: Taking a Bearing (Field to Map)
"I see a water tower. Where is it on the map?"
1.  Point the **Direction of Travel Arrow** at the object (water tower).
2.  Rotate the **Bezel** until the Red Needle is in the "Shed" (Red in the Shed).
3.  Read the number at the index line (e.g., 270 degrees).
4.  Place compass on map at your known location. Rotate it (without moving the dial) until N aligns with Map North.
5.  Draw a line along the compass edge. The tower is somewhere on that line.

## 5. Usage 3: Shooting an Azimuth (Map to Field)
"I want to go to this lake on the map. Which way do I walk?"
1.  Place compass on map. Connect "My Location" to "Destination" with the edge of the baseplate.
2.  Rotate the **Bezel** until `N` aligns with Map North (ignore the needle for now).
3.  Pick up the compass. Hold it flat in front of you.
4.  **Turn your body** until the Red Needle is in the "Shed".
5.  Look where the Direction of Travel Arrow points. Pick a landmark (tree, rock). Walk to it. Repeat.

## 6. Declination (The Hidden Trap)
*   **True North** (Map North) and **Magnetic North** (Compass North) are not the same.
*   The difference is called **Declination**.
*   **East Declination:** Subtract from map bearing.
*   **West Declination:** Add to map bearing.
*   *Mnemonic:* "West is Best (Add), East is Least (Subtract)."

## 7. The Pace Count
*   How far is 100 meters? You need to know.
*   **Measure it:** Walk 100m on flat ground. Count every time your *left* foot hits the ground.
*   **Average:** Usually 60-70 paces for an adult male.
*   **Use Beads:** Tie "Ranger Beads" to your pack. Slide one down every 100m. Slide a top bead every 1km.

**Universal Rule:** Trust the compass. When you feel like you're walking in circles, your brain is lying. The magnet is telling the truth.
