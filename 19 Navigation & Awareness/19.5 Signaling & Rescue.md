# 19.5 Signaling & Rescue: Being Found

In a survival situation, your primary goal is either finding your way out or being found. If you are immobilized or in a vast wilderness, signaling is your strongest tool.

## 1. The Rule of Threes (Signaling)
In the world of international rescue, **three of anything** represents a distress signal.
*   **Three Fires:** Arranged in a triangle or a straight line (100 feet apart).
*   **Three Whistle Blasts:** Short, sharp bursts.
*   **Three Mirror Flashes:** Rapidly repeating.
*   **Three Gunshots:** (Use only in extreme cases to conserve ammo).

## 2. Visual Ground-to-Air Signals
If a search plane or helicopter is overhead, use the international "V" and "X" codes. Make them as large as possible (at least 10ft x 10ft) using contrasting materials (logs on snow, rocks on sand, trampled grass).

*   **V:** I need assistance.
*   **X:** I need medical assistance.
*   **Arrow:** I am moving in this direction.
*   **SOS:** Help (Universally recognized).

## 3. The Signal Mirror
One of the most powerful tools in your kit. A mirror flash can be seen for 10 miles or more on a clear day.
*   **How to Aim:** 
    1. Hold the mirror in one hand.
    2. Extend your other arm toward the target (plane/rescue team) and make a "V" with your fingers.
    3. Flash the mirror across your fingers so the "light spot" hits the target through the V.
    4. Move the light back and forth.

## 4. Smoke and Fire
*   **Daytime:** Thick black smoke is best. Use rubber, grease-soaked rags, or green pine boughs.
*   **Nighttime:** Bright white/orange fire. Use dry wood.
*   **Preparation:** Always keep a "Ready Fire" piled with dry kindling and covered by a tarp or bark, ready to light the moment you hear an engine.

## 5. Audio Signaling
*   **The Survival Whistle:** Much louder and more sustainable than shouting. Use the SOS pattern: 3 short, 3 long, 3 short.
*   **Pausing:** Always stop and listen for 5 minutes for every 1 minute of signaling. You might be missing a distance response.

---
## 6. Survival Priority: The "Stay Put" Rule
If you have signaled successfully (e.g., built a large SOS out of rocks), **STAY PUT**. A moving target is significantly harder for search and rescue (SAR) teams to track than a stationary one.
