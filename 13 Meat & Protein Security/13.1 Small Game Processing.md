# 13.1 Small Game Processing

**Critical Principle:** Clean meat quickly. Bacteria multiply fast. From death to clean carcass in under 30 minutes is the goal.

---

## Pre-Processing: Field Dressing

**Do this immediately after harvest, before bringing the animal back:**

1. **Bleed the animal:** Cut throat or hang upside down (if already dead, skip this)
2. **Position:** Lay on its back, head uphill if on a slope
3. **Make the first cut:** Pinch skin at the belly, cut **shallow** from sternum to pelvis (avoid puncturing intestines)
4. **Remove entrails:** Reach in, pull out organs carefully (save heart/liver if desired)
5. **Cool the cavity:** Prop open with a stick if ambient temp is above 50°F

**Why:** Removes heat and bacteria source. A field-dressed carcass can hang for hours without spoiling.

---

## Rabbit Processing (Step-by-Step)

**Tools needed:** Sharp knife, cutting board, bowl for organs, water for rinsing.

### Step 1: Remove the Head and Feet
- Cut **between** the joints (not through bone—find the gap and slice through cartilage)
- Front feet: Cut at the "wrist" joint
- Rear feet: Cut at the ankle joint
- Head: Cut at the base of the skull

### Step 2: Skinning
**Rabbit skin peels off like a jacket—no knife needed after the initial cut.**

1. Pinch skin at the mid-back, make a small cut
2. Insert fingers under the skin, pull **down** toward the head and tail simultaneously (like peeling a banana)
3. Skin pulls off cleanly, leaving a clean carcass

**Tip:** Save the pelt (see section 13.3 Tanning Basics).

### Step 3: Gutting (If Not Field Dressed)
1. Make a shallow cut from sternum to pelvis
2. Carefully pull out intestines, stomach, lungs
3. **Save:** Heart, liver, kidneys (edible, nutrient-dense)

### Step 4: Rinse and Inspect
- Rinse carcass with clean water
- Check for abscesses, discoloration, or parasites (if found, discard the animal)
- Look for white spots on the liver (sign of tularemia—discard)

### Step 5: Butchering
**Rabbit yields 4 pieces + 2 small pieces:**

1. **Front legs:** Cut at the shoulder joint (twist and cut)
2. **Rear legs:** Cut at the hip joint (these are the largest, meatiest cuts)
3. **Saddle (back):** Cut into 2-3 pieces along the spine
4. **Rib cage:** Usually discarded (little meat) or used for stock

**Yield:** A 3-4 lb rabbit gives ~1.5 lbs of meat.

## Chicken Processing (Step-by-Step)

**Tools needed:** Sharp knife, cone or rope (for hanging), boiling water, plucking area.

### Step 1: Dispatch and Bleed
- Hang upside down by feet (use a cone or tie legs to a rope)
- Cut jugular veins on both sides of the neck (swift and humane)
- Let bleed for 2-3 minutes

### Step 2: Scald and Pluck
**Purpose:** Loosens feathers for easy removal.

1. Heat water to **145-150°F** (not boiling—too hot cooks the skin)
2. Dunk chicken for 30-60 seconds, agitate to ensure water penetrates feathers
3. Pull feathers out in the direction they grow (comes off easily when scalded properly)

**Tip:** Start with wing and tail feathers (hardest). If they pull easily, the rest will too.

### Step 3: Remove Head and Feet
- Cut head off at the base of the neck
- Cut feet off at the ankle joint (below the scaly part)

### Step 4: Gutting
1. Lay bird on its back
2. Make a horizontal cut just above the vent (anus), being careful not to puncture intestines
3. Insert hand into the cavity, scoop out all organs in one motion
4. **Save:** Heart, liver (cut away green gallbladder carefully—bile ruins meat), gizzard (cut open, remove gravel and inner lining)

### Step 5: Remove the Crop and Windpipe
- Reach into the neck cavity, pull out the crop (grain storage pouch) and trachea

### Step 6: Rinse
- Rinse inside and out with cold water
- Pat dry

### Step 7: Butchering (Optional)
**For stew/frying, cut into pieces:**

1. **Wings:** Cut at the shoulder joint
2. **Legs:** Cut through the hip joint, separate thigh and drumstick at the knee
3. **Breasts:** Cut along the breastbone, separate from the ribs
4. **Back:** Usually reserved for stock (little meat)

**For roasting:** Leave whole.

## Squirrel Processing (Similar to Rabbit)

**Difference:** Squirrel has a tougher hide and more fur.

**Skinning method:**
1. Cut a ring around the midsection (through skin only, not meat)
2. Pull skin toward the head and tail (like stretching a rubber band)
3. Cut head and tail off, skin comes free

**Butchering:** Same as rabbit (2 hind legs, 2 front legs, saddle).

**Note:** Squirrel is leaner than rabbit—needs slow cooking (stew, braise).

## Safety and Hygiene

### Preventing Cross-Contamination
- **Separate tools:** Use one knife for gutting, another for butchering (or clean thoroughly between)
- **Wash hands:** Before and after handling carcass
- **Clean workspace:** Scrub cutting board with hot soapy water after use

### Signs of Diseased Meat (Do Not Eat)
| Sign | What It Means | Action |
|------|---------------|--------|
| **White spots on liver** | Tularemia (rabbit fever) | Discard entire animal |
| **Swollen lymph nodes** | Infection | Discard entire animal |
| **Abscesses (pus pockets)** | Localized infection | Cut away affected area generously, cook remaining meat thoroughly |
| **Foul odor** | Spoilage | Discard entire animal |

### Proper Storage
- **Refrigeration:** Use within 2 days
- **Freezing:** Wrap tightly, use within 6 months
- **No refrigeration:** Cook immediately or preserve (see section 13.2 Salting and Smoking)

## Cooking Small Game

**Rabbit and chicken are interchangeable in recipes. Squirrel needs longer cooking.**

### Classic Rabbit Stew (One-Pot Meal)
**Ingredients:**
- 1 rabbit (cut into pieces)
- 2 onions, chopped
- 3 carrots, chopped
- 3 potatoes, cubed
- 2 cups broth (or water)
- Salt, pepper, herbs (thyme, rosemary)

**Instructions:**
1. Brown rabbit pieces in a pot with fat (lard, oil)
2. Add onions, cook until soft
3. Add carrots, potatoes, broth, herbs
4. Simmer 1.5-2 hours (until meat falls off the bone)
5. Season with salt and pepper

**Yield:** Feeds 4-6 people.

---

## Ethical Considerations

**If you harvest an animal, you owe it a clean, respectful process:**

- Use every part possible (meat, organs, bones for broth, hide for leather)
- Dispatch quickly and humanely
- Don't waste (if you can't use it all, share or preserve)

**Know your limits:** If you're not confident in your processing skills, practice on store-bought chicken first. The mechanics are identical.

---

## The 30-Minute Goal

**With practice, you should be able to:**

- Field dress a rabbit: 5 minutes
- Skin and butcher a rabbit: 10 minutes
- Rinse and store: 5 minutes
- **Total: 20 minutes**

**For chicken:**
- Scald and pluck: 10 minutes
- Gut and butcher: 10 minutes
- **Total: 20 minutes**

**Speed = freshness = safety.**
