# 13.2 Salting and Smoking

**Purpose:** Preserve meat for months without refrigeration. Both methods inhibit bacterial growth—salt through dehydration, smoke through antimicrobial compounds.

---

## The Science of Preservation

### Why Meat Spoils
- **Bacteria** multiply in moisture, warmth, and protein-rich environments
- **Enzymes** in the meat itself break down tissue (autolysis)

### How Preservation Works
- **Salt:** Draws water out of meat (bacteria can't survive without water)
- **Smoke:** Deposits creosote and phenols (antimicrobial chemicals)
- **Drying:** Reduces water activity below bacterial threshold

**Combined (salted + smoked):** Meat can last 6-12 months at room temperature.

---

## Method 1: Dry Salting (Simplest)

**Best for:** Pork, beef, fish. (Chicken and rabbit are too lean—better smoked.)

### Ingredients
- **Meat:** 5 lbs (pork belly, beef brisket, whole fish)
- **Salt:** 2 lbs (non-iodized—iodine causes off-flavors)
- **Optional:** 1/4 cup sugar (balances flavor, feeds beneficial bacteria)

### Process

#### Step 1: Prepare the Meat
- Trim off any damaged or bloody areas
- Cut into manageable slabs (2-3 inches thick)

#### Step 2: Pack in Salt
1. Rub salt **generously** over every surface (think of it like breading)
2. Layer in a non-reactive container (ceramic crock, food-grade plastic tub, wooden box)
3. Alternate layers: **salt → meat → salt → meat**
4. Top with a thick layer of salt (1 inch)

#### Step 3: Weight and Wait
- Place a weight on top (plate + rock, or water-filled jug)
- Store in a cool place (50-60°F ideal—basement, root cellar)
- **Timeline:**
  - **Thin cuts (1"):** 7 days per inch
  - **Thick cuts (3"):** 3 weeks per inch

#### Step 4: Check for Brine
- After 24-48 hours, liquid will pool at the bottom (this is brine—a good sign)
- If no brine forms, add more salt

#### Step 5: Rinse and Dry
- Remove from salt, rinse thoroughly under cold water
- Pat dry with a cloth
- Hang in a cool, ventilated space for 24 hours (called "equalization"—salt distributes evenly)

#### Step 6: Store
- Wrap in cheesecloth or butcher paper
- Hang in a cool, dark, dry place
- **Shelf life:** 6-12 months (longer if kept very cold)

### How to Use Salted Meat
**It's VERY salty—must be desalted before eating:**

1. Cut off the amount needed
2. Soak in cold water for 12-24 hours, changing water every 6 hours
3. Taste a small piece—if still too salty, soak longer
4. Cook as normal (boil, fry, add to stews)

## Method 2: Smoking (Best for Flavor + Preservation)

**Best for:** All meats, fish, poultry.

### Types of Smoking

| Type | Temperature | Time | Shelf Life | Use |
|------|-------------|------|------------|-----|
| **Cold smoke** | 70-90°F | 12-48 hours | Months (if also salted) | Salmon, bacon, sausage |
| **Hot smoke** | 180-220°F | 4-8 hours | 1-2 weeks (refrigerated) | Ribs, brisket, chicken |

**For preservation (no fridge), use cold smoke + salt.**

---

### Building a Simple Smoker

**Materials:**
- 2 metal barrels (55-gallon drums) or a large cardboard box (temporary)
- Firebox (offset, below the smoking chamber)
- Grates or hooks for hanging meat
- Hardwood chips (hickory, oak, apple, cherry)

**Design:**
```
[Firebox] ---(smoke travels)---> [Smoking Chamber]
   (coals + wood chips)              (meat hangs inside)
```

**Key feature:** Smoke travels horizontally or upward through a pipe, cooling before it reaches the meat (this is how you get <90°F temps).

**Simple Field Smoker:**
1. Dig a pit for the fire (2 feet deep)
2. Dig a trench leading uphill (3-4 feet long) to a second pit
3. Place grate over second pit, hang meat above it
4. Cover with canvas or bark (to trap smoke)
5. Build small fire in first pit, add green wood for smoke

---

### Cold Smoking Process (Preservation Method)

#### Step 1: Brine the Meat First
**Why:** Pre-salting is required for cold smoking (flavor + preservation).

**Basic Brine (1 gallon):**
- 1 gallon water
- 1 cup salt (non-iodized)
- 1/2 cup brown sugar
- 1 tbsp black pepper
- Optional: garlic, bay leaves, juniper berries

**Process:**
1. Dissolve salt and sugar in water
2. Submerge meat completely
3. Refrigerate (or keep in a cool place) for:
   - Fish: 6-12 hours
   - Chicken: 12-24 hours
   - Pork/beef: 24-48 hours

#### Step 2: Dry the Meat (Critical)
- Remove from brine, rinse briefly
- Pat dry
- Hang in a cool, breezy place for 2-4 hours until surface is **tacky** (called forming a "pellicle")
- **Why:** The pellicle helps smoke adhere and creates a barrier against bacteria

#### Step 3: Smoke
- Hang meat in the smoker
- Start fire with hardwood **only** (no pine, no lighter fluid)
- Add damp wood chips to create smoke (not flames)
- Maintain temp below 90°F (if it feels warm inside the chamber, reduce fire)
- **Timeline:**
  - Fish: 12-24 hours
  - Chicken: 24-36 hours
  - Pork/beef: 36-48 hours

**Goal:** Meat should be dry to the touch, dark, and deeply smoky.

#### Step 4: Store
- Let cool completely
- Wrap in cloth or butcher paper
- Hang in a cool, dry place
- **Shelf life:** 2-6 months (depending on salt content and storage conditions)

---

### Hot Smoking (For Eating Soon)

**Best for:** Immediate consumption or short-term storage (1-2 weeks).

**Process:** Same as cold smoking, but maintain 180-220°F in the chamber.

**Timeline:**
- Fish: 2-3 hours
- Chicken: 4-5 hours
- Pork ribs: 5-6 hours
- Brisket: 8-12 hours

**Doneness:** Use a thermometer:
- Chicken: 165°F internal
- Pork: 145°F internal
- Fish: 145°F internal

**This is cooking + smoking, not preservation.** Eat within days (or freeze).

---

## Wood Selection

**Never use:**
- Pine, fir, cedar (resinous—toxic smoke)
- Treated lumber (chemicals)
- Moldy wood (imparts off flavors)

**Best woods:**
| Wood | Flavor | Best For |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Hickory** | Strong, bacon-like | Pork, beef |
| **Oak** | Medium, balanced | All meats |
| **Apple** | Sweet, mild | Chicken, fish |
| **Cherry** | Fruity, subtle | Poultry, pork |
| **Mesquite** | Intense, earthy | Beef (use sparingly—can be bitter) |

## Jerky (The Portable Protein)

**Best for:** Lean meat (venison, beef, turkey). No fat (fat goes rancid).

### Process
1. **Slice:** Cut meat into 1/4" strips (with the grain for chewy, against for tender)
2. **Marinate (optional):**
   - 1/4 cup soy sauce
   - 2 tbsp Worcestershire
   - 1 tsp black pepper
   - 1 tsp garlic powder
   - Marinate 6-12 hours
3. **Dry:**
   - Lay strips on racks (don't overlap)
   - Smoke at 160°F for 4-6 hours (or dehydrate at 160°F)
   - **Done when:** Bends without breaking, no moisture when squeezed
4. **Store:**
   - Cool completely
   - Store in airtight container
   - **Shelf life:** 1-2 months (longer if vacuum-sealed)

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| **Meat too salty** | Too much salt or not rinsed | Soak longer before eating |
| **Meat spoiled (slimy, smells off)** | Too warm during curing | Cure in cooler environment (below 60°F) |
| **Smoke flavor too strong** | Over-smoked | Reduce smoke time, use milder wood |
| **Meat too dry** | Over-dried | Monitor time, remove when still slightly pliable |

## The Emergency Salt Pork Recipe

**Purpose:** Turn fresh pork into 6-month shelf-stable protein in 48 hours.

**Ingredients:**
- 5 lbs pork belly (or any fatty pork cut)
- 2 lbs salt
- 1/4 cup brown sugar

**Process:**
1. Cut pork into 2" thick slabs
2. Pack in salt (layer method)
3. Weight down, leave 2 days
4. Rinse, pat dry, hang in cool place
5. Use within 6 months

**To cook:** Slice thin, soak 30 minutes, fry crispy (tastes like thick bacon).

---

## Why This Matters

**In a grid-down scenario, you can't freeze surplus meat.** Hunting, butchering, and preserving becomes a cycle:

1. Harvest animal
2. Process immediately
3. Smoke or salt within 24 hours
4. Store for lean months

**Master this, and protein scarcity becomes protein security.**
