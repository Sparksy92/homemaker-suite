# 13.4 Fishing Techniques

**The Advantage of Fish:** Unlike hunting, fishing is legal almost everywhere (with basic regulations), and fish populations rebound quickly. In survival scenarios, fish are often the most accessible protein source.

**Fish don't run away.** They're in that water, right now. You just need to get them out.

---

## Fishing Without Modern Gear

### Method 1: Hand Line (Simplest)

**Materials:**
- Line (any strong string, fishing line, dental floss, or natural cordage)
- Hook (store-bought, improvised wire, bone, or thorn—see section 16.2)
- Weight (sinker, small rock, or metal nut)
- Bait

**Setup:**
1. Tie hook to end of line
2. Add weight 6-12 inches above hook (helps it sink)
3. Wrap other end of line around a stick (for grip) or tie to a stationary object

**Technique:**
- Cast or drop line into water
- Let it sink to desired depth
- Feel for bites (tug on the line)
- Pull up when you feel resistance

**Best for:** Dock fishing, shore fishing, ice fishing

---

### Method 2: Trotline (Passive, Multi-Hook)

**The ultimate set-it-and-forget-it method.**

**Materials:**
- Long line (50-100 feet of strong cord)
- 10-20 shorter lines (12-18 inches each, called "droppers")
- 10-20 hooks
- Weights (to sink the line)
- Floats (optional, for surface trotlines)

**Setup:**
1. String main line across a body of water (bank to bank, or anchor both ends)
2. Tie dropper lines every 4-6 feet along main line
3. Attach hook to each dropper
4. Bait all hooks
5. Submerge line (weight it down or let it sink naturally)

**Check frequency:** Every 4-12 hours (more often in warm weather—fish spoil fast)

**Advantages:**
- Passive (works while you sleep)
- Multiple hooks = higher catch rate
- Can leave for days (check regularly)

**Legal note:** Some jurisdictions restrict trotlines. In survival, legality is secondary to food.

---

### Method 3: Fish Trap (Completely Passive)

**The ancient method—used for thousands of years.**

#### Woven Basket Trap

**Materials:**
- Flexible branches (willow, grapevine) or wire
- Cordage

**Design:**
- Cone-shaped basket with a narrow entrance
- Fish swim in, can't find the exit

**Construction:**
1. Weave a cylinder (12-18" diameter, 2-3 feet long)
2. Weave a funnel cone that fits inside one end (narrow opening points inward)
3. Fish enter through the cone, get trapped in the cylinder

**Placement:**
- Submerge in shallow water (streams, ponds)
- Anchor with rocks or stakes
- Bait inside (bread, fish guts, dog food)

**Check:** Daily

---

#### Bottle Trap (Modern Version)

**Materials:**
- 2-liter soda bottle
- Knife

**Construction:**
1. Cut bottle 1/3 from the top
2. Invert the top, insert into bottom (creates funnel—see section 16.2)
3. Poke holes in cap and sides (water flow)
4. Tie sections together with string/wire
5. Add bait, submerge

**This works.** It's simple, effective, and can be set in minutes.

---

### Method 4: Spearfishing (Active)

**Best for:** Clear, shallow water where you can see fish.

**Materials:**
- Long, straight stick (6-8 feet)
- Sharp point (carved, fire-hardened, or lashed knife/bone)

**Technique:**
1. Wade slowly into water or stand on shore/dock
2. Wait for fish to approach (stillness is key)
3. **Aim below the fish** (water refraction makes fish appear higher than they are)
4. Thrust downward with force
5. Pin fish against bottom or bank

**Skill level:** High (requires practice, patience, and good aim)

**Tip:** Fish in the morning or evening (fish are more active, lighting is better)

---

### Method 5: Noodling (Hand Fishing)

**Warning:** This is dangerous. Fish can bite, and you can encounter snapping turtles, snakes, or get stuck underwater.

**How it works:**
1. Find underwater holes (logs, rock crevices, undercut banks)
2. Reach in slowly
3. Feel for fish (catfish, bass often hide in holes)
4. Grab fish by the mouth or gills
5. Pull out quickly

**Only attempt this if:**
- You're a strong swimmer
- Water is warm (hypothermia risk in cold water)
- You know local species (some fish have venomous spines)

**Not recommended for beginners.**

## Bait Selection

### Natural Bait (In Order of Effectiveness)

| Bait | Best For | How to Get It |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Worms** | All freshwater fish | Dig in moist soil, under logs |
| **Grasshoppers/crickets** | Panfish, trout | Catch in grass, under boards |
| **Minnows** | Bass, pike, catfish | Catch with net or trap |
| **Fish guts** | Catfish, carp | Save from previous catch |
| **Bread dough** | Carp, panfish | Mix bread with water, form balls |
| **Grubs/larvae** | All fish | Found under bark, in rotting wood |

**General rule:** Use what fish eat naturally in that water body.

---

### Artificial Lures (If Available)

**Shiny lures (spoons, spinners):** Mimic small fish, attract predatory fish (bass, pike)

**Improvised lures:**
- Bottle cap on a hook (wobbles like a wounded fish)
- Strip of cloth (white or red, tied to hook)
- Feather + hook (resembles insect)

**Movement = attraction.** Even crude lures work if they move erratically.

---

## Fishing Locations (Where Fish Hide)

Fish don't swim randomly—they cluster in specific spots.

### Prime Fishing Zones

1. **Structure:** Logs, rocks, docks (fish hide from predators)
2. **Drop-offs:** Where shallow water meets deep water (fish ambush prey here)
3. **Shade:** Under trees, bridges, overhangs (cooler water, insects fall in)
4. **Inlets/outlets:** Where streams enter/exit ponds (oxygenated water, moving food)
5. **Weed beds:** Edges of vegetation (cover + food source)

**Avoid:** Open, deep water in the middle of lakes (fish on the edges, not the center)

---

## Time of Day (When to Fish)

**Best times:**
- **Dawn** (30 min before sunrise to 2 hours after)
- **Dusk** (2 hours before sunset to 30 min after dark)

**Why:** Fish feed most actively in low light (they feel safer from predators).

**Worst time:** Midday (hot, bright—fish go deep or hide)

**Exception:** Overcast days = all-day fishing (low light even at noon)

---

## Seasonal Fishing Patterns

| Season | Fish Behavior | Best Technique |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| **Spring** | Shallow (spawning, warming water) | Shore fishing, shallow traps |
| **Summer** | Deep (seeking cool water) | Deeper trotlines, early morning |
| **Fall** | Feeding heavily (preparing for winter) | All methods work well |
| **Winter** | Slow, deep (cold-blooded metabolism slows) | Ice fishing, deeper bait |

## Preserving Your Catch

### Immediate Field Care
1. **Kill humanely:** Strike head with rock (brain death is instant)
2. **Gut immediately:** Slit belly, remove organs (prevents spoilage)
3. **Keep cool:** Submerge in cold water (in a mesh bag) or pack in ice/snow

**Timeline:** Fish spoil faster than mammals. Process within 2-4 hours in warm weather.

### Preservation Methods

**Short-term (1-2 days):**
- Keep on ice or in cold water
- Gut and clean

**Long-term:**
- **Smoking:** See section 13.2 (fish cold-smokes beautifully)
- **Salting:** Pack in salt (same method as meat)
- **Drying:** Fillet thin, hang in sun/smoke (makes jerky)

**Fish jerky recipe:**
1. Fillet fish into 1/4" thick strips
2. Soak in brine (1 cup salt per gallon water) for 30 minutes
3. Hang in smoker or sun for 6-12 hours (until dry and leathery)

**Shelf life:** 1-3 months if dried properly.

## Ice Fishing (Winter Protein)

**If you live where water freezes:**

### Equipment
- **Ice auger or chisel** (to drill holes)
- **Hand line** (rod is optional)
- **Tip-ups** (flags that signal a bite—can fish multiple holes)

### Technique
1. Drill 6-10 inch hole through ice
2. Drop baited line to bottom, raise 6-12 inches
3. Wait (ice fishing = patience)
4. Pull up when you feel a bite

**Safety:** Ice must be 4+ inches thick to walk on safely. Test with an auger before venturing out.

---

## Net Fishing (If You Can Make/Find a Net)

### Gill Net (Passive, Highly Effective)

**How it works:** Fish swim into net, gills get caught in mesh (can't back out).

**Setup:**
- Stretch net across a narrow section of water (stream, channel)
- Anchor both ends with stakes or rocks
- Check every 6-12 hours

**Legal note:** Gill nets are illegal in many places (they're too effective and can overfish). Use only in survival.

---

### Cast Net (Active)

**How it works:** Throw circular net over a school of fish, weights sink it, pull in by rope.

**Skill level:** High (requires practice to throw properly)

**Best for:** Shallow, clear water with visible fish

---

## Fishing Line from Scratch

**If you have no modern line:**

### Natural Cordage (revisit section 16.2)
- **Nettle fiber** (strongest)
- **Dogbane** (very strong, smooth)
- **Sinew** (from animal tendons—incredibly strong)

**Test:** Line should support 5-10 lbs for most fish (more for large catfish/pike).

### Modern Scavenged Line
- **Dental floss** (surprisingly strong, 20-30 lb test)
- **Thread from clothing** (weave multiple strands)
- **Wire** (from fences, appliances—use for leaders to prevent bite-offs)

---

## Fish Cleaning (Quick Method)

1. **Scale:** Scrape from tail to head with knife or spoon (removes scales)
2. **Gut:** Slit belly from anus to gills, scoop out organs
3. **Remove head and fins** (optional—conserves meat if you leave them on)
4. **Rinse in clean water**

**Total time:** 2-3 minutes per fish with practice.

**For small panfish:** Cook whole (organs and all) over fire—bones get crispy and edible.

---

## Realistic Expectations

**Good fishing spot + proper technique:**
- 3-10 fish per day (hand line or trotline)
- 10-30 fish per day (trap or gill net)

**Average fish size:** 1/2 - 2 lbs (panfish, trout, small bass)

**Protein yield:** 1 lb fish = ~60-80g protein = 1 person's daily protein need.

**Translation:** Catch 2-3 medium fish = fed for the day.

---

## The Fishing Daily Routine

**Morning:**
1. Check trotlines/traps (harvest overnight catches)
2. Re-bait and reset
3. Hand-line fish for 1-2 hours (dawn is prime time)

**Midday:**
- Process catches (clean, preserve)
- Gather bait for next session
- Repair gear

**Evening:**
1. Fish for 1-2 hours (dusk is prime time)
2. Check trotlines/traps before dark
3. Set overnight lines

**Time investment:** 4-5 hours per day = consistent protein.

---

## When Fish Aren't Biting

**Troubleshooting:**
1. **Change bait:** What works yesterday might not work today
2. **Change location:** Move to structure, shade, or different depth
3. **Change depth:** Fish go deeper when hot, shallower when cool
4. **Fish at different times:** Try dawn/dusk if midday fails

**Sometimes fish just don't bite.** Don't waste all day on one method—diversify (traps + lines + nets).

---

## The Bottom Line

**Fishing is the most reliable protein source in survival:**
- Easier than hunting (fish don't run away)
- More predictable than trapping (you can see the fish)
- Season-independent (fish year-round, even under ice)

**Master 3 techniques:**
1. **Hand line** (active, immediate)
2. **Trotline** (passive, efficient)
3. **Trap** (ultra-passive, set-and-forget)

**With these three, you'll never go hungry near water.**
