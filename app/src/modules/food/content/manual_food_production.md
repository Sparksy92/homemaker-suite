## 1. Soil Science: The Foundation of Life

YOU CANNOT GROW FOOD WITHOUT ALIVE SOIL. In a survival scenario, you cannot buy fertilizer. You must build soil.

### 1.1 Soil Composition & texture
Soil is composed of three particles: **Sand, Silt, and Clay**.
*   **Sand:** Large particles. Drains fast, holds few nutrients. (Gritty).
*   **Clay:** Tiny particles. Holds water/nutrients well, but compacts and drowns roots. (Sticky).
*   **Silt:** Medium size. (Floury).
*   **Loam:** The Holy Grail. 40% Sand, 40% Silt, 20% Clay.

**Field Test (The Jar Method):**
1.  Fill a clear jar half-full of soil.
2.  Fill rest with water + 1 tsp salt (dispersant).
3.  Shake violently for 2 mins.
4.  Let sit for 24 hours.
5.  **Result:**
    *   Bottom Layer: Sand (settles in 1 min).
    *   Middle Layer: Silt (settles in 1 hour).
    *   Top Layer: Clay (settles in 24 hours).
    *   Floating Stuff: Organic Matter (Gold).

### 1.2 Soil pH (Acidity)
Most vegetables need pH **6.0 - 7.0** (Slightly Acidic to Neutral).
*   **Acidic Soil (pH < 6):** Nutrients are locked up. Common in forests/rainy areas.
    *   *Correction Reference:* Add **Wood Ash** or **Lime** (crushed shells/limestone).
*   **Alkaline Soil (pH > 7.5):** Common in deserts/arid areas.
    *   *Correction Reference:* Add **Pine Needles**, **Oak Leaves**, or **Sulfur**.

**Improvised pH Test:**
1.  **Test for Acid:** Moist soil + Baking Soda. Bubbles? = Acidic.
2.  **Test for Alkali:** Moist soil + Vinegar. Bubbles? = Alkaline.
3.  **No Reaction?** = Neutral (Good).

### 1.3 N-P-K (The Big Three)
*   **Nitrogen (N):** Leaf growth.
    *   *Deficiency:* Pale/yellow older leaves. Stunted growth.
    *   *Survival Sources:* Human urine (diluted 10:1 - aged is better), Manure (Chicken/Rabbit), Coffee grounds, Legumes (Beans/Peas fix N from air).
*   **Phosphorus (P):** Root & Fruit development.
    *   *Deficiency:* Purple tints on leaves. Poor fruiting.
    *   *Survival Sources:* Bone meal (burn bones then crush), Wood ash (small amounts), Bird guano.
*   **Potassium (K):** General health & disease resistance.
    *   *Deficiency:* Brown scorching on leaf edges. Weak stems.
    *   *Survival Sources:* Wood Ash (Potash), Banana peels, Kelp/Seaweed.

## 2. Advanced Composting Systems

Turn waste into gold. In survival, **nothing** organic is thrown away.

### 2.1 The Hot Compost Pile (Berkeley Method)
Produces ready compost in **18 days**. High labor.
*   **Ratio:** 30:1 (Carbon:Nitrogen).
    *   *Browns (C):* Dry leaves, straw, cardboard, sawdust, paper.
    *   *Greens (N):* Kitchen scraps, grass clippings, manure, urine.
*   **Size:** Minimum 3ft x 3ft x 3ft (1 cubic yard). Crucial for heat.
*   **Procedure:**
    1.  Build pile all at once. chop materials small (1-2 inches).
    2.  Water until it feels like a wrung-out sponge.
    3.  **Day 4:** Turn pile (outside to inside). Temp should be 120-140°F (Hot to touch).
    4.  **Day 6, 8, 10, etc:** Turn every 2 days.
    5.  **Day 18:** Dark, crumbly, earthy smell. Done.

### 2.2 Humanure (The Composting Toilet)
SAFE sewage management is critical to prevent Cholera/Typhoid.
*   **The System:** 5-Gallon Bucket + Toilet Seat + Cover Material (Sawdust).
*   **Rule 1:** Cover every deposit completely with sawdust/peat moss. NO SMELL = NO FLIES.
*   **The Pile:**
    1.  Designate a "Humanure Bin" far from water sources.
    2.  Dump buckets here. Cover with straw.
    3.  **Time:** MUST sit for **1-2 YEARS** undisturbed. Thermophilic bacteria kill pathogens.
    4.  **Use:** Only use on fruit trees/bushes. Avoid root vegetables just to be safe.

### 2.3 Vermiculture (Worm Farming)
Indoor, stealth composting.
*   **Worms:** Red Wigglers (*Eisenia fetida*). Not earthworms.
*   **Bedding:** Shredded damp newspaper/cardboard.
*   **Food:** Veggie scraps (No meat/dairy/citrus).
*   **Harvest:** Worm Castings (Poop) are the strongest fertilizer known to man. Use sparingly as top dressing.

## 3. Land Assessment & Garden Planning

### 3.1 Sun Mapping
Observe land for 1 year if possible.
*   **Full Sun:** 6-8+ hours directly. (Tomatoes, Peppers, Corn, Squash).
*   **Part Shade:** 3-6 hours. (Root veg: Carrots, Beets, Potatoes).
*   **Shade:** < 3 hours. (Leafy Greens: Lettuce, Spinach, Kale).

### 3.2 Bio-Indicators
Plants tell you about the soil.
*   **Dandelions/Dock:** Compacted soil (Taproots trying to break it).
*   **Nettles:** High Nitrogen soil (Rich).
*   **Moss:** Acidic, damp, shady soil.

### 3.3 The Three Sisters (Survival Polyculture)
Ancient, proven, resilient.
1.  **Corn:** Provides the structure (trellis).
2.  **Beans:** Climbing pole beans use the corn. They fix Nitrogen into soil for the corn.
3.  **Squash:** Grows along ground. Big leaves shade soil (retain water, suppress weeds). prickly vines deter pests.

## 4. Seed Saving Protocols

If you can't save seed, you are not sustainable.

### 4.1 Hybrid (F1) vs. Heirloom
*   **Heirloom (Open-Pollinated):** Seeds produce identical offspring. **REQUIRED FOR SURVIVAL.**
*   **Hybrid (F1):** Seeds produce unpredictable/sterile mutants. Avoid.

### 4.2 How to Save Seeds (Specifics)
*   **Beans/Peas:** Let pods dry BROWN and crispy on the vine. rattle test. Shell and store.
*   **Tomatoes/Cucumbers (Wet Seeds):**
    1.  Scoop seeds + gel into jar.
    2.  Add water. Let sit 3 days (Ferment). Mold will form (destroys diseases).
    3.  Good seeds sink. Bad seeds float.
    4.  Rinse sinkers and dry on screen.
*   **Corn:** Must grow huge population (50+ plants) to prevent inbreeding depression. Let dry on stalk.
*   **Biennials (Carrots/Beets/Onions):** Do not seed in Year 1. Must overwinter. Collect seeds in Year 2 summer.

## 5. Crop Profiles (Survival Calories)

Focus on Calorie Density, not Salad. You cannot survive on lettuce.

### 5.1 Potatoes (The King)
*   **Technique:** "Chitting" (sprouting eyes). Plant 4" deep. "Hill up" soil as they grow to increase yield.
*   **Harvest:** When tops die back.
*   **Calories:** Very High.
*   **Storage:** 6+ months in root cellar.

### 5.2 Dry Beans (Protein)
*   **Types:** Pinto, Black, Kidney.
*   **Harvest:** Fully dry on vine.
*   **Storage:** Years if kept dry.
*   **Cooking:** Soaking required.

### 5.3 Winter Squash (Pumpkin/Butternut)
*   **Value:** High vitamin A. Stores at room temp/cool area for 6 months.
*   **Harvest:** Cure stems in sun for 1 week to harden skin.

### 5.4 Jerusalem Artichoke (Sunchoke)
*   **Nature:** Invasive weed. Unkillable.
*   **Part:** Tubers.
*   **Warning:** Contains Inulin (causes gas). Roast slowly/ferment to breakdown.
*   **Role:** Emergency famine food.

## 6. Pest Management (No Chemicals)

### 6.1 Physical Barriers
*   **Floating Row Covers:** Lightweight fabric. Lets light/rain in, keeps beetles/moths out.
*   **Collars:** Cardboard tube around seedling stem blocks Cutworms.

### 6.2 Biological Warfare
*   **Ladybugs:** Eat Aphids.
*   **Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt):** Natural bacteria. Kills caterpillars (Cabbage loopers/Tomato worm). Safe for humans.
*   **Diatomaceous Earth (DE):** Microscopic fossils. Like razor wire for bugs. Sprinkle on dry plants. Reapply after rain.

### 6.3 Homemade Sprays
*   **Soap Spray:** 1 tsp Castile soap + 1 quart water. Kills soft-bodied bugs (Aphids/Mites) on contact.
*   **Pepper/Garlic Spray:** Blend hot peppers + garlic + water. Strain. Repels mammals/bugs.
*   **Nicotine Tea:** Boil tobacco. **EXTREMELY TOXIC**. Use only as last resort nuclear option. Can kill you too.

## 7. Indoor & Hydroponic Systems

### 7.1 Dutch Bucket System (Tomatoes/Peppers)
*   **Setup:** 5-gallon buckets with drain at bottom. Perlite medium.
*   **Flow:** Pump drips nutrient water onto top. Drains back to reservoir. Recirculates.
*   **Pro:** High yield.
*   **Con:** Dependent on electricity (pump).

### 7.2 Kratky Method (Lettuce/Leafy)
*   **Tech Level:** Zero (Passive).
*   **Setup:** Net cup with plant suspended in lid of jar/tote.
*   **Fluid:** Water + Nutrients. Air gap grows as water level drops. Roots breathe humid air.
*   **Maintenance:** None until harvest.

### 7.3 Aquaponics (Fish + Plants)
*   **Cycle:** Fish Poop (Ammonia) -> Bacteria convert to Nitrates -> Plants eat Nitrates -> Clean water returns to fish.
*   **Fish:** Tilapia (warm) or Trout (cold).
*   **Balance:** Hard to maintain. If pump fails, fish die in 4 hours.
*   **Redundancy:** Battery backup for aerator is mandatory.

## 8. Foraging Encyclopedia (Region: Temperate NA)
*Note: See wildlifeData.json for full schema, this is the manual.*

### 8.1 The Rule of Threes (Foraging)
1.  **Identify 3 times:** Flower, Leaf, Habit.
2.  **3 Sources:** Confirm with 3 separate guides.
3.  **Try a little:** Rub on skin (wait). Touch to lip (wait). Chew spit out (wait). Swallow small bit (wait).

### 8.2 Top Mushrooms (Safe-ish)
*   **Morels:** Sponge cap. Hollow stem. Spring.
*   **Chanterelles:** False ridges (not gills). Smells like apricots.
*   **Chicken of the Woods:** Bright orange. No gills (pores). Grows on wood.
*   **Warning:** "Little Brown Mushrooms" (LBMs) - Ignore them. Too many lookalikes. Galerina destroys liver.

### 8.3 Trees
*   **Oak (Acorns):** Crack shell. Grind meat. Leach tannins in running water (or jar changes) until water runs clear. Dry -> Flour.
*   **Maple:** Sap runs when days > 32°F and nights < 32°F. Boil 40 gallons sap = 1 gallon syrup.
*   **Pine:** Cambium (inner bark) is edible (fry like bacon). Needle tea prevents Scurvy.

## 9. Failure Points & Redundancies

*   **Failure:** Drought.
    *   *Redundancy:* Greywater diversion (laundry/bath water to garden). Mulching heavily (woodchips) retains moisture.
*   **Failure:** Pest Swarm (Locusts/Beetles).
    *   *Redundancy:* Diversity. Don't plant monocultures. Underground crops (potatoes) survive surface swarms.
*   **Failure:** Theft.
    *   *Redundancy:* "Guerrilla Gardening". Plant crops that don't look like food (Sunchokes looks like sunflowers, Amaranth looks like weeds).
