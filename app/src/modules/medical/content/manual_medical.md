# Survival Manual: Survival Medicine
**Version:** 1.0 (Survival Edition)
**Domain:** Medicine / Pharmacology / Anatomy
**Complexity:** Critical (Life/Death)
**Dependency:** Water, Sanitation, Shelter

---

## 1. The Rule of Triage (Mass Casualty)

When resources are scarce, you cannot save everyone.
1.  **Immediate (Red):** Life-threatening but treatable (Airway, Bleeding). *Treat FIRST.*
2.  **Delayed (Yellow):** Serious but stable (Broken bone, large wound). *Wait.*
3.  **Minimal (Green):** Walking wounded (Cuts, scrapes). *Self-Aid.*
4.  **Expectant (Black):** Dying. Injuries incompatible with survival given current resources (Head trauma with brain matter exposed, full body burns). *Palliative Care Only.*

## 2. Trauma: Stop the Bleed (MARCH Protocol)

**M - Massive Hemorrhage:**
*   **Direct Pressure:** Push HARD. Don't let up.
*   **Tourniquet (TQ):** Use ONLY for limb arterial bleeds (bright red spurting).
    *   *Placement:* "High and Tight" (not over a joint).
    *   *Improvised:* Belt is bad (too wide/loose). Use heavy stick (windlass) + triangular bandage/t-shirt. Twist until bleeding stops. LOCK IT.
    *   *Time:* Mark time on forehead. TQ can stay on 2-6 hours safely. Saving life > Saving limb.
*   **Wound Packing:** For junctions (groin/armpit) where TQ fails. Stuff gauze/shirt INTO the wound aggressively. Contact the bone. Hold pressure 3-5 mins.

**A - Airway:**
*   **Chin Lift / Jaw Thrust:** Open the pipe.
*   **NPA (Nasopharyngeal Airway):** Tube in nose to keep tongue off back of throat. (Don't use if facial trauma).
*   **Recovery Position:** Roll on side if unconscious (prevents choking on vomit).

**R - Respiration:**
*   **Sucking Chest Wound:** Hole in lung. Air sucked IN hole instead of mouth.
*   **Seal:** Plastic Bag + Tape on 3 sides (valve). Air goes out, not in.

**C - Circulation:**
*   **Shock:** Pale, Clammy, Fast Pulse, Confusion = Not enough blood.
*   **Treat:** Stop bleeding. Keep Warm. Lay flat. No water by mouth.

**H - Hypothermia:** Blood doesn't clot when cold. Keep patient warm at all costs.

## 3. Infection Recognition & Management

Infection kills more than bullets.
*   **Signs:** Redness (erythema), Swelling (edema), Heat, Pain, Pus (purulence).
    *   *Danger Sign:* Red streaks tracking up the limb (Sepsis).
*   **Cleaning:**
    *   **Irrigation:** "The solution to pollution is dilution." Flush with LITERS of boiled (cool) water. Pressure (syringe) helps.
    *   **Debridement:** Cut away dead/black tissue. It feeds bacteria.
*   **Dressing:**
    *   **Wet-to-Dry:** Damp gauze inside wound, dry on top. When pulled out, it cleans dead tissue. (Painful but effective).
    *   **Honey:** Medical Manuka (or raw local honey). Antibacterial barrier.
    *   **Sugar:** Granulated sugar packed into a wound creates hypertonic environment -> bacteria dehydrate and die. (Old field trick).

## 4. Antibiotics (The stash)

*WARNING: Use only when necessary. Resistance is real.*
*   **Amoxicillin (Fish Mox):** Broad spectrum. Sinus, respiratory, dental.
*   **Ciprofloxacin (Fish Flox):** Anthrax, Urinary Tract (UTI), abdominal.
*   **Doxycycline (Fish Doxy):** Tick diseases (Lyme), Pneumonia.
*   **Metronidazole (Fish Zole):** Gut bugs (Giardia), Anaerobic infections.
*   **Azithromycin (Fish Cin):** Respiratory, Strep.

## 5. Field Procedures (Civilian Safe)

### 5.1 Suturing vs. Gluing vs. Steri-Strips
*   **Steri-Strips / Butterfly:** Safest. Less infection risk suitable for 90% of cuts.
*   **Super Glue (Cyanoacrylate):** Good for clean, shallow straight cuts. Seal top only. DO NOT glue deep dirty wounds (traps bacteria = abscess).
*   **Suturing:** DANGEROUS field skill. Only if you know how.
    *   *Risk:* Closing a dirty wound = Gangrene.
    *   *Rule:* If > 6 hours old, do NOT close. Leave open ("healing by secondary intention").

### 5.2 Dental Emergencies
*   **Lost Filling:** Plug with temporary filling material (Zinc Oxide + Clove Oil) or melted candle wax (last resort).
*   **Abscess:** DANGEROUS. Can spread to brain. Antibiotics (Amox/Clindamycin) + Pain med + Drain pus if visible bubble. extract tooth if antibiotics fail.
*   **Extraction:** Grab tooth, NOT bone. Rock back/forth. Pull. Pack with gauze. Bite down. Suture gum if needed.

### 5.3 Blisters / Burns
*   **Blisters:** Do not pop unless painful/weight bearing. Thread with needle/thread to drain but leave skin flap (natural bandage).
*   **Burns:**
    *   *1st (Red):* Aloe / Cool water.
    *   *2nd (Blister):* Do not burst. Silvadene cream (Silver Sulfadiazine).
    *   *3rd (Charred/White):* High infection risk. Keep clean. Hydrate patient (fluid loss).

## 6. Herbal Medicine (The Forest Pharmacy)

*   **Willow Bark:** Salicin (Aspirin). Pain/Fever. (Chew twig or Tea).
*   **Yarrow:** Styptic. Stop bleeding.
*   **Plantain:** Poultice for drawing out infection/venom.
*   **Mullein:** Respiratory tea.
*   **Ginger:** Nausea.
*   **Cloves:** Toothache (Eugenol numbs nerve).
*   **Activated Charcoal:** Poison ingestion. Adsorbs toxins. (Burnt wood/toast scraped).

---

## 7. Medical Storage & Hygiene

*   **Sterilization:** Boiling (Tools) for 20 mins. Alcohol (70% is better than 91% - water helps penetration). flame.
*   **Needles:** Single use only. If forced reuse, boil + alcohol + flame.
*   **Handwashing:** The #1 Survival Skill. Ash + Fat = Soap. 20 seconds.

---

## 8. Failure Points

*   **Running out of Meds:**
    *   *Redundancy:* Stockpile 1 year supply. Learn herbal alternatives.
*   **Doctor Death:**
    *   *Redundancy:* 3 people in group cross-trained in TCCC (Tactical Combat Casualty Care).
*   **Epidemic:**
    *   *Redundancy:* Quarantine protocols. Isolation room. N95 masks.
