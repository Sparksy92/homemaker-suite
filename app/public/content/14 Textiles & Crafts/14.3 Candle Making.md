# Candle Making: Light in the Darkness

Candles are more than decor; they are stored light.

## Wax Types
1.  **Beeswax**: The homestead standard. Sweet smelling, hard, long-burning. Expensive if you don't have bees.
2.  **Tallow**: Rendered beef fat. Free if you butcher steers. Can smell "meaty" if not rendered 3+ times.
3.  **Soy**: Agricultural byproduct. Easy to clean up (soap and water).

## Wicks
*   The wick size matters. Too small = "tunneling" (wax remains on sides). Too large = smoking and rapid burn.
*   **Rule of Thumb**: Cotton braided wicks for beeswax. Wood wicks for soy.

## Basic Beeswax Process
1.  **Melt**: Double boiler. Beeswax melts at 144°F. Do not overheat (it can discolor).
2.  **Prep Container**: Glue wick to bottom of jar. Keep it centered with a pencil/clothespin across the top.
3.  **Pour**: Pour slowly to avoid air bubbles.
4.  **Cool**: Let cool slowly in a warm room. Cooling too fast cracks the wax.

## Tallow Candles ("Emergency Candles")
*   Render fat until pure liquid gold.
*   Add essential oils (optional) to mask scent.
*   Tallow is softer than beeswax, usually better in jars than as pillars.

> [!WARNING]
> **Fire Hazard**: Never leave melting wax unattended. If it catches fire, smother it. Do NOT use water.
