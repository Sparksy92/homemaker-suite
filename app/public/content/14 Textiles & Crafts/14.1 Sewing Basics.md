# Sewing Basics: The Art of Mending

In a disposable world, fixing your clothes is a radical act of self-reliance.

## The Survival Kit
You don't need a machine to start.
*   **Needles**: Assorted sizes (Sharps for regular fabric, Darners for wool).
*   **Thread**: Polyester (stronger) or Cotton (dyeable).
*   **Scissors**: Fabric shears (NEVER cut paper with them) and embroidery snips.
*   **Thimble**: Protects your finger when pushing through denim/canvas.

## The 3 Essential Stitches

### 1. The Running Stitch (Basting)
*   **Use**: Temporarily holding fabric together, or visible mending (Sashiko).
*   **How**: Up, down, up, down. Even spacing.

### 2. The Backstitch (Strength)
*   **Use**: Seams that need to hold weight (pants, bags). The strongest hand stitch.
*   **How**: Bring needle up at A, go back down at B (behind A), come up at C (ahead of A). You are constantly looping back.
*   **Result**: Looks like a solid line of thread.

### 3. The Whip Stitch (Edges)
*   **Use**: Finishing edges so they don't fray, or joining two edges flat (fur, felt).
*   **How**: Loop the thread *over* the edge of the fabric repeatedly.

## Mending 101
*   **Patches**: Cut a patch 1 inch larger than the hole. Pin it in place. Whip stitch the edges of the patch to the garment.
*   **Darning**: Weaving new thread across a hole (usually socks). Create a grid of vertical threads, then weave horizontal threads over-under-over-under.

> [!TIP]
> **Button Repair**: When sewing a button, place a toothpick on top of the button and sew over it. Remove the toothpick at the end. This creates a "shank" so the button isn't too tight against the fabric.
