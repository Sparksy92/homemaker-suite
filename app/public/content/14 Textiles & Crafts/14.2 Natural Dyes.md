# Natural Dyes: Color from the Land

Synthetic dyes are predictable. Natural dyes are alive. They shift with the season, the soil, and the water.

## The Process

### 1. Scouring (Cleaning)
Fabric must be clean. Oil/dirt prevents dye from sticking.
*   **Cotton**: Simmer with soda ash.
*   **Wool**: Wash gently with pH neutral soap (Dawn).

### 2. Mordanting (The Glue)
Most natural dyes won't stick without a "mordant" (binder).
*   **Alum (Pickling Alum)**: The gold standard. Safe and bright.
    *   *Recipe*: Dissolve alum (15% weight of fiber) in warm water. Simmer fabric in it for 1 hour. Let cool in pot overnight.

### 3. The Dyestuff (Foraging)
*   **Yellows**: Goldenrod (flowers), Onion Skins (skins), Dandelion.
*   **Oranges**: Coreopsis, Rhizome roots.
*   **Pinks/Reds**: Pokeberry (berry juice - vinegar modifier), Madder Root.
*   **Browns**: Black Walnut Hulls (No mordant needed! Rich in tannins).

### 4. Dyeing
*   Make a "tea" with your plant material. Strain the chunks out.
*   Add damp, mordanted fabric.
*   Simmer (never boil wool!) for 1 hour.
*   Let sit overnight for deeper color.

### 5. Modifiers (Magic)
*   **Iron**: Dipping dyed fabric in rusty water "saddens" the color (makes it darker/grayer). Yellow turns Olive. Pink turns Purple.
*   **Acid (Vinegar)**: Shifts colors brighter/warmer.

> [!NOTE]
> **Fugitive Dyes**: Some things look great (Beets, Red Cabbage) but fade to brown in weeks. We use *lightfast* dyes for clothing.
