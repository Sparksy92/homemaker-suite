# 17.5 Simple Off-Grid Structures: Building Your Foothold

When you move beyond emergency tarps, you need a structure that can handle snow loads, keep out predators, and stay dry for years.

---

## 1. The Survival A-Frame (Fastest Permanent Structure)
An A-frame is essentially a roof that goes all the way to the ground. It is incredibly strong in wind and sheds snow easily.

### Step 1: The Foundation
*   Don't dig holes (they rot the wood).
*   Place four large, flat stones at the corners of your structure.
*   Place your "Sill Logs" (bottom frame) on these stones.

### Step 2: The Main Triangles
1.  Cut two logs of equal length (e.g., 10 feet).
2.  Notch them at the top to overlap (a "half-lap" joint).
3.  Secure the top with a wooden peg or a heavy bolt.
4.  Stand the triangle up and secure the bottom ends to your sill logs.
5.  Repeat for the back of the structure.

### Step 3: The Ridgepole
1.  Connect the peaks of the two triangles with a long, straight ridgepole.
2.  Lash or bolt it securely. This is the spine of your house.

### Step 4: Sheathing & Roofing
*   **Primitive:** Vertical poles leaned against the ridgepole, covered in 18 inches of bark or boughs.
*   **Modern:** 2x4 rafters, plywood, and metal roofing.

---

## 2. The One-Man Log Cabin (12' x 12')
A classic for a reason. If you have trees, you have a house.

### The "Saddle Notch" Technique
This is the simplest way for one person to build a cabin. The weight of the logs locks the corners together.

1.  **Prep:** Peel the bark off all logs (bark holds moisture and bugs).
2.  **Level One:** Lay two logs parallel on your stone foundation.
3.  **The Notch:** Lay two cross-logs on top. Carve a U-shaped "saddle" into the top of the bottom logs so the top logs sit halfway into them.
4.  **The "Cup":** Carve a slight concave "cup" along the entire length of the bottom of each log. This allows it to sit snugly on the log below it.
5.  **Chinking:** Fill the gaps with a mix of clay, sand, and straw (cob).

---

## 3. The Floor: Keeping Out the Damp
*   **Dirt Floor:** Hard-pack the earth. Coat with linseed oil or wood ash to create a "leather" finish.
*   **Raised Wood Floor:** Requires more lumber but keeps you much warmer. Ensure there is 12 inches of airflow *under* the floor to prevent rot.

---

## 4. Heating: The "Through-Wall" Exit
*   **Never** just poke a stovepipe through a wooden wall.
*   Build a "Thimble": A hole twice the diameter of the pipe, lined with stone or metal, to prevent your house from burning down.

---

## Tool List for One-Man Construction
*   **Felling Axe:** For cutting trees.
*   **Drawknife:** For peeling bark (essential for longevity).
*   **Adze:** For flattening the top of logs for flooring.
*   **Auger (1-inch):** For drilling holes for wooden pegs (trenails).
*   **Crosscut Saw:** For squaring ends.

---

## The Golden Rule of Building
**"Water is the enemy."**
1.  Keep the roof wide (large overhangs) to keep rain off the walls.
2.  Keep the bottom logs off the dirt (stone foundation).
3.  Ventilate. A sealed cabin is a moldy cabin.
