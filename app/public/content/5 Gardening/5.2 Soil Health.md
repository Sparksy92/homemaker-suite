# 5.2 Soil Health: Feed the Soil, Not the Plant

## The Web of Life
Dirt is dead; Soil is alive. A teaspoon of healthy soil has more microbes than humans on earth. These microbes break down nutrients for plants.

## N-P-K (The Big Three)
*   **N - Nitrogen (Green):** Leaf growth.
    *   *Source:* Blood meal, Fish emulsion, Coffee grounds, Manure.
*   **P - Phosphorus (Root/Flower):** Fruit development.
    *   *Source:* Bone meal, Rock phosphate.
*   **K - Potassium (General Health):** Disease resistance, sturdy stems.
    *   *Source:* Wood ash (sparingly), Kelp meal.

## Amendments
*   **Compost:** The universal fixer. Adds organic matter, improves drainage in clay, improves water retention in sand.
*   **Mulch:** Cover bare soil with straw, leaves, or grass clippings.
    *   Retains water.
    *   Suppresses weeds.
    *   Rot down into food.

## No-Till Gardening
Tilling destroys fungal networks and brings weed seeds to the surface.
*   **Method:** Layer compost/cardboard *on top* of the ground (Lasagna gardening). Plant directly into the top layer.

## Cover Crops: Living Armor
Never leave soil bare. When a bed is empty, plant cover crops to prevent erosion and add nutrients.
*   **Winter Rye/Vetch:** Plant in autumn. In spring, cut it down and let it rot (green manure). It adds massive amounts of organic matter.
*   **Clover:** A "living mulch" that fixes nitrogen while you grow other crops.

## pH Adjustment
Most vegetables prefer a pH of 6.0 to 7.0 (slightly acidic).
*   **Too Acidic:** Add crushed eggshells or wood ash (sparingly) to raise pH.
*   **Too Alkaline (Rare):** Add pine needles or coffee grounds to lower pH.

## Homesteader Soil Tips
*   **The Worm Audit:** Dig a 1ft cube of soil. If you find at least 5-10 earthworms, your soil biology is healthy.
*   **Liquid Gold (Compost Tea):** Soak a bag of finished compost in a bucket of water for 24 hours. Use the liquid to water heavy feeders like tomatoes for an instant nutrient boost.
