# 5.9 Season Extension & High Tunnels

In a survival or homestead scenario, the "Hungry Gap" (late winter/early spring) is the most dangerous time. Extending your season ensures a continuous food supply when the ground is frozen.

## High Tunnels (Hoop Houses)
A high tunnel is a large, unheated greenhouse made of PVC or metal hoops and 6-mil greenhouse plastic.

### DIY Low-Cost Build
1.  **Ground Stakes:** Drive 2ft lengths of rebar into the ground every 4ft along your garden edges.
2.  **Hoops:** Slide 20ft lengths of UV-resistant PVC pipe over the rebar to create arches.
3.  **Ridge Pole:** Zip-tie a single pipe along the top of all arches for stability.
4.  **Plastic:** Pull clear plastic over the frame and weigh down the edges with rocks or heavy lumber.

## Cold Frames: The "Mini-Greenhouse"
A cold frame is essentially a bottomless box with a transparent lid (an old window or clear plastic). 
*   **Placement:** Face the glass South to catch maximum winter sun.
*   **Thermal Mass:** Place 2-gallon jugs of water (painted black) inside the cold frame. They absorb heat during the day and release it slowly at night, keeping the temperature above freezing.
*   **Venting:** On sunny days, even in winter, a closed box can hit 100°F. Propping the lid open a few inches is critical to prevent "cooking" your seedlings.

## Mulching for Heat
*   **Black Plastic:** Lays directly on the soil to absorb sun and warm the ground 5-10°F faster in spring.
*   **Straw/Leaves:** Acts as insulation. A 6-inch layer of straw can protect root crops (carrots, parsnips, leeks) so they can be harvested even when the topsoil is frozen.

## Best Cold-Weather Crops
These crops can survive a light frost and even thrive in the cooler temperatures of a high tunnel:
*   **Extreme Cold:** Spinach, Kale, Mache (Corn Salad), Claytomia.
*   **Moderate Cold:** Carrots, Onions, Bok Choy, Radishes.
