# 5.11 Vertical Gardening for Small Spaces

When you run out of ground, go up. Vertical gardening can quadruple your yield per square foot while improving airflow and making harvest easier.

## Vertical Supports
*   **Cattle Panel Trellis:** 16-foot heavy-duty wire panels bent into an arch between two beds. Excellent for squash, melons, and cucumbers.
*   **The Florida Weave:** A technique for tomatoes using twine and T-posts to create a wall of fruit that is easy to prune.
*   **Living Walls:** Using "pocket" planters or felt bags mounted to a fence. Perfect for herbs, strawberries, and leafy greens.

## Climbing Crops
Not all plants can climb. Choose varieties specifically bred for vertical growth:
*   **Pole Beans:** Will naturally spiral up any pole or string.
*   **Vining Cucumbers:** Look for "slicing" or "pickling" varieties; avoid "bush" types if you want to go vertical.
*   **Indeterminate Tomatoes:** These grow indefinitely and **must** be supported. Determinate (bush) tomatoes will not climb.

## Managing the "Understory"
The space under your trellises is valuable real estate. 
*   **Shade Lovers:** Plant lettuce, spinach, and cilantro under the shadow of your climbing cucumbers or beans. The vertical crop provides the shade needed to prevent these greens from "bolting" (going to seed) in the heat of summer.
*   **Mulch Effect:** The canopy of leaves from a trellis acts as a living mulch, keeping the soil below cool and moist.

## Harvesting at Eye Level
One of the greatest benefits of vertical gardening is ergonomic. 
*   **Ease of Audit:** Pests and diseases are easier to spot when they are at eye level.
*   **Clean Fruit:** Squashes and cucumbers grown vertically remain clean, straight, and less prone to rot than those resting on the soil.
