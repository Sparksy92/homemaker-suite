# Seed Saving: Closing the Loop

Buying seeds every year is a dependency. Saving seeds adapts plants to your specific micro-climate over generations.

## Open Pollinated vs. Hybrid
*   **Open Pollinated (OP) / Heirloom**: Offspring will look like the parent. **Save these.**
*   **Hybrid (F1)**: Offspring will be a random mix or sterile. **Do not save.**

## Easy Seeds (Self-Pollinators)
These flowers are closed and pollinate themselves before opening. Low risk of crossing.
*   **Beans/Peas**: Let pods dry brown and crispy on the vine. Shell and store.
*   **Lettuce**: Let it bolt (flower). Wait for fluff (like dandelions). Shake into a bag.
*   **Tomatoes**: See "Wet Processing" below.

## Wet Processing (Tomatoes/Cucumbers)
Seeds inside wet fruit have a gel inhibitor preventing sprouting.
1.  **Squeeze**: Squeeze seeds and pulp into a jar.
2.  **Ferment**: Let sit for 3 days until a mold skin forms on top. This eats the gel.
3.  **Wash**: Add water. Good seeds sink. Bad seeds float. Pour off the top.
4.  **Dry**: Dry sinking seeds on a coffee filter or screen for 2 weeks.

## Difficult Seeds (Cross-Pollinators)
*   **Squash/Pumpkins**: Bees carry pollen for miles. If you grow a Zucchini near a Pumpkin, the seed will be a weird hybrid next year. You must hand-pollinate and tape the flower shut to save pure seed.
*   **Corn**: Wind pollinated. Needs huge isolation distances.

## Storage
*   **Enemy**: Moisture and Heat.
*   **Ideal**: Air-tight jar in the fridge or freezer.
*   **Test**: Germination test. Put 10 seeds in a damp paper towel. If 8 sprout, you have 80% germ rate.
