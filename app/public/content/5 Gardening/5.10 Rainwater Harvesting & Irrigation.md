# 5.10 Rainwater Harvesting & Irrigation

Water is the lifeblood of the garden. In a drought or grid-down scenario, you must be able to capture and distribute water efficiently.

## Rainwater Harvesting
A 1,000 sq ft roof can shed **600 gallons** of water during just 1 inch of rain.

### Setting Up a Rain Barrel
1.  **Positioning:** Place barrels on high ground (or cider blocks) relative to the garden. Gravity is your pump.
2.  **Screening:** Use fine mesh over the intake to prevent mosquitoes and debris.
3.  **Overflow:** Always install an overflow hose that leads away from your home's foundation.
4.  **Linking:** You can link multiple barrels together using 1-inch bulkhead fittings and PVC pipe to create a "bank" of water reserve.

## DIY Drip Irrigation
Drip irrigation reduces water waste by 50% compared to overhead spraying and keeps leaves dry, preventing fungus.

### The Low-Pressure Setup
*   **Header Line:** A 1/2-inch poly tube running along the head of the garden beds.
*   **Emitters:** Small stakes or holes that drip water directly at the base of each plant.
*   **Timer:** A simple battery-operated hose timer can automate watering even if you are away.

## The Olla: Ancient Efficiency
An "Olla" is an unglazed clay pot buried in the garden with only the neck exposed. 
1.  Fill the pot with water and cap it.
2.  Water slowly seeps through the porous clay directly into the surrounding soil.
3.  Plant roots will actually wrap around the pot to take exactly what they need. This is the most water-efficient method for desert or high-heat environments.
