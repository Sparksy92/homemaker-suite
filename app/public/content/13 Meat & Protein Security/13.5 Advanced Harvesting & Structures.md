# 13.5 Advanced Harvesting & Structures

**Purpose:** Move beyond simple field tools to semi-permanent infrastructure that scales your survival capacity. These projects require more time but provide massive returns in food security.

---

## 1. The Log Smoke House
A semi-permanent structure designed for long-term large-batch cold smoking.

### Specifications
- **Dimensions:** 4' x 4' base, 6' height.
- **Materials:** Non-resinous logs (cedar for structure is okay, but use hardwood for smoke), clay/mud for chinking, flat rocks for base.
- **Key Feature:** Remote firebox (prevents fat-drip fires and keeps temperatures low for cold smoking).

### Construction Steps
1. **The Foundation:** Level a 4x4 area. Place flat rocks at the corners and along the perimeter to keep the bottom logs off the wet ground.
2. **The Firebox Trench:** Dig a trench 1' wide, 1' deep, and at least 6' long, leading into the center of the smokehouse. 
   - *Why 6 feet?* This distance allows the smoke to cool below 90°F before it hits the meat.
3. **The Chimney/Flue:** Cover the trench with flat rocks and seal gaps with mud or clay. The end inside the smokehouse should have a vertical opening.
4. **Log Walls:** Build simple notched-log walls. Leave a small gap near the top for a "vent" on the opposite side of the flue entrance.
5. **The Roof:** A simple pitch roof made of bark or split logs. It doesn't need to be perfectly waterproof, but it must trap smoke.
6. **Hanging Racks:** Install horizontal sapling poles across the top interior. Use stainless hooks or S-hooks carved from wood to hang meat slabs.

## 2. The Traditional Fish Wheel
A current-driven machine that automatically "scoops" fish from a river and deposits them into a harvest box.

### Requirements
- **Location:** A river with a steady, swift current and a known fish run (salmon, whitefish, etc.).
- **Materials:** Logs for the axle/raft, flexible willow/spruce for baskets, wire or cordage.

### Design
```
    [Current --->]
      /   \
     | (X) | <-- Axle
      \   /
    [Baskets] --> [Catch Box]
```

### Construction Steps
1. **The Raft:** Build a floating platform using two large logs connected by cross-beams. This allows the wheel to adjust to changing water levels.
2. **The Axle:** A long, straight log spanning the two raft logs. Carve the ends into "pins" that sit in notched bearings on the raft.
3. **The Spokes:** Attach 3 or 4 sets of spokes (long saplings) to the axle at 90° or 120° intervals.
4. **The Baskets:** Weave large, funnel-shaped baskets (like oversized lacrosse heads) onto the end of the spokes. 
   - **Critical:** The baskets must be angled so that as the wheel turns, the fish slides down the spoke toward the axle.
5. **The Slide & Box:** Build a slanted wooden slide next to the axle. When a basket reaches the top of the rotation, the fish falls out, hits the slide, and drops into a submerged mesh box (the "live box").

## 3. The Atlatl (Spear Thrower)
A primitive tool that adds an extra "joint" to your arm, doubling the leverage and force of a thrown spear.

### Materials
- **Thrower:** 2-foot straight piece of hardwood (hickory, ash).
- **Dart (Spear):** 5-6 foot flexible sapling, feathered for stability.
- **Weight:** A "bannerstone" (smooth rock) lashed to the back of the thrower can increase momentum.

### Construction
1. **Handle:** Carve a comfortable grip at one end.
2. **The Spur:** At the far end, carve or lash a small "hook" (spur) that will fit into a dimple in the back of the dart.
3. **The Dart:** The dart must be flexible. When it hits the target, it should flex slightly to dump all its kinetic energy into the point.
4. **Fletching:** Add large feathers to the tail to ensure straight flight at higher velocities.

## 4. Specialized Harvesting Tools

### Basket Traps (Eels & Crayfish)
- **Design:** Double-funnel entrance.
- **Bait:** Fish guts or smelly meat.
- **Placement:** Rocky crevices or under banks where current slows.

### Walk-In Bird Trap
- **Design:** A large cage made of sticks and netting with a "low-entry" tunnel.
- **Mechanism:** Birds enter through a hole in the ground or a narrow tunnel to reach grain. Once inside, they look *up* for an exit and fail to find the ground-level hole.

---

## Troubleshooting Primitive Gear
- **Smokehouse too hot?** Lengthen the flue trench or use smaller coals.
- **Fish wheel won't turn?** Increase basket size (more drag) or move closer to the main current.
- **Atlatl dart wobbling?** Your shaft is too stiff; thin it down until it has a slight "bounce" when flexed.

> [!TIP]
> Semi-permanent structures require maintenance. Check your smokehouse for rodent holes (they love cured meat) and your fish wheel for debris/logs that can break the axle during high-water events.
