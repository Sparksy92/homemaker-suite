# 13.3 Hunting & Trapping

**Ethics First:** Hunting and trapping are for **sustenance, not sport**. Take only what you need, use every part of the animal, and kill humanely. Wasting meat is both wasteful and disrespectful.

**Legal Note:** In non-emergency situations, always follow local hunting/trapping regulations. This section assumes a survival scenario.

---

## Hunting vs. Trapping: When to Use Each

| Method | Energy Cost | Success Rate | Time Investment | Best For |
|--------|------------| -------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Hunting** | High | Low-Medium | Active (hours) | Immediate need, skilled hunter |
| **Trapping** | Low | Medium-High | Passive (check 1-2x/day) | Long-term, conserving energy |

**In survival:** Traps are force multipliers. Set 10 traps, check them twice daily = 20 chances to catch food while you focus on other tasks.

## Small Game Traps (No License Required in Survival)

### Trap 1: Figure-4 Deadfall (Squirrels, Rabbits)

**Already covered in section 16.2 Improvised Tools.** Quick recap:

- 3 notched sticks arranged in a "4" shape
- Heavy rock or log balanced on top
- Bait stick triggers collapse
- **Kill zone:** Under the rock

**Ethics:** Check daily (twice if possible). Injured animals suffer if left.

---

### Trap 2: Simple Snare (Rabbits, Squirrels)

**Most effective passive trap.**

#### Materials
- 24" of wire (picture-hanging wire, guitar string, or braided fishing line)
- Small stick for stake
- Knife

#### Construction

**Step 1: Make the loop**
1. Tie a small fixed loop at one end of the wire (slip it through itself)
2. Thread the other end through this loop to create a noose
3. Size: 4-5" diameter for rabbits, 2-3" for squirrels

**Step 2: Set the snare**
1. Find a game trail (look for droppings, tracks, runs through grass)
2. Place the noose across the trail at head height of the animal:
   - Rabbit: 4-6" off the ground
   - Squirrel: varies (often along fallen logs)
3. Secure the wire to a stake, tree, or **drag stick** (heavy branch that animal will drag if caught—prevents escape)

**Step 3: Funnel the animal (optional but effective)**
- Place sticks or brush on either side of the snare (narrows the path)
- Animal is more likely to walk through the snare

#### How It Works
- Animal's head enters loop
- As it struggles forward, loop tightens around the neck
- Quick, humane (if checked promptly)

**Check frequency:** Every 12 hours minimum (more often in warm weather—meat spoils fast).

---

### Trap 3: Twitch-Up Snare (Self-Lifting)

**Advanced version:** Uses a bent sapling to lift the animal off the ground (faster kill, prevents escape).

#### Materials
- Snare wire
- Bent sapling or spring pole
- Trigger mechanism (carved sticks or a peg)

#### Construction

**Step 1: Find or create a spring pole**
- Bend a young sapling down to the ground
- Tie snare wire to the tip

**Step 2: Create the trigger**
- **Simple toggle:** Tie the spring pole to a stake with a slip knot
  - When animal pulls the snare, the knot releases, pole springs up
- **Carved trigger:** Notch two sticks to "catch" each other
  - Disturbance releases the catch

**Step 3: Set the snare on a trail**
- Same placement as simple snare (head height)

**Advantage:** Animal is lifted, dies faster (constriction + gravity).

## Hunting Small Game (Active Pursuit)

### The Stillness Method (No Firearm)

**Best for:** Squirrels, rabbits (if you have a slingshot, throwing stick, or primitive bow).

**Technique:**
1. **Move slowly:** 1-2 steps per minute (no joke)
2. **Freeze often:** Stop for 2-3 minutes, scan for movement
3. **Look for:**
   - Twitching leaves (feeding squirrel)
   - Ears in grass (rabbit)
   - Movement in branches
4. **Approach:** Within 10-20 feet (throwing range)

**Throwing stick technique:**
- Use a heavy, straight stick (2 feet long, wrist-thick)
- Aim for the head or body
- Throw sidearm (like skipping a stone)
- **Hit rate:** Low, but improves with practice

---

### The Blind Sit (Patience Method)

**Best for:** Dawn/dusk hunting when animals are active.

**Setup:**
1. Find a game trail, feeding area, or water source
2. Position downwind (animals smell you before they see you)
3. Sit still (against a tree or in cover) for 30-60 minutes

**Goal:** Let animals come to you instead of pursuing them.

## Primitive Weapons (If Firearms Are Unavailable)

### Slingshot (DIY)
**Materials:** Y-shaped branch, rubber tubing or elastic strip, leather pouch

**Construction:**
1. Cut a Y-shaped fork from a tree (hardwood preferred)
2. Attach rubber tubing to each arm of the Y (surgical tubing works best)
3. Connect tubing to a leather or cloth pouch (holds the projectile)

**Ammunition:** Small stones (marble-sized)

**Range:** 30-50 feet effective  
**Skill level:** Practice required (aim for head shots on small game)

---

### Throwing Stick (Rabbit Stick)

**Materials:** Hardwood branch, 18-24" long, wrist-thick

**How to use:**
1. Hold one end, throw sidearm (like a frisbee)
2. Aim for small game in open areas
3. **Curve the stick slightly** (boomerang effect increases hit zone)

**Effective range:** 15-30 feet

---

### Primitive Bow (Advanced)

**Materials:** Flexible sapling (hickory, yew, ash), cordage, straight arrow shafts

**This is a multi-day project.** See historical archery guides for full instructions.

**Quick version:**
1. Cut a 5-foot sapling (thumb-thick)
2. Carve notches at each end
3. String with strong cordage (reverse-wrap plant fiber)
4. Arrows: straight sticks, sharpened or fire-hardened tips

**Skill requirement:** High (practice for weeks before relying on this)

## Trap Placement Strategy (Multiplying Success)

**Don't set one trap—set ten.**

**Game trails to target:**
1. **Water sources:** Animals must drink (set traps on approach paths)
2. **Feeding areas:** Nut trees, berry bushes, crop fields
3. **Den entrances:** Rabbit warrens, hollow logs
4. **Travel corridors:** Fences, hedgerows, fallen logs

**The rule of 10:** Set 10 traps, check twice daily = 20 opportunities per day.

## Bait Selection

| Animal | Best Bait | Why |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| **Squirrel** | Peanut butter, nuts, seeds | High-fat, strong smell |
| **Rabbit** | Carrots, apples, greens | Herbivore diet |
| **Raccoon** | Fish, cat food, marshmallows | Omnivore, loves sweets |
| **Opossum** | Overripe fruit, cat food | Scavenger tendencies |

**None available?** Use scent markers (urine from a previous catch, fish guts) instead of food.

## Humane Dispatch (If Animal is Still Alive)

**Never let an animal suffer.**

**Method 1: Blunt trauma (Immediate)**
- Strike the back of the head with a heavy rock or stick
- One swift, hard blow

**Method 2: Dislocation**
- For rabbits/chickens: Hold hind legs, pull head back sharply (breaks neck instantly)

**This is not pleasant, but it's necessary.** A quick death is your responsibility.

## Processing the Catch

**Refer to section 13.1 Small Game Processing** for full instructions.

**Quick field dressing (if you're miles from home):**
1. Gut immediately (prevents spoilage)
2. Hang in shade to cool
3. Process fully within 2-4 hours

## Trap Checking Schedule

**Minimum:** Every 12 hours (morning and evening)

**Ideal:** Every 6-8 hours

**Why:** 
- Suffering minimized
- Predators won't steal your catch
- Meat doesn't spoil

**Mark your trap locations** (GPS, landmarks, or blaze marks on trees). Losing a trap = losing food.

---

## Legal and Ethical Boundaries

### In NON-survival situations:
- **Get licensed:** Most jurisdictions require hunting/trapping licenses
- **Follow seasons:** Off-season hunting harms breeding populations
- **Respect bag limits:** These exist for species conservation

### In TRUE survival emergencies:
- Laws are superseded by immediate survival need
- **Still:** Don't overkill (take only what you can use)
- **Avoid:** Endangered species (if alternatives exist)

---

## Trap Maintenance

**After each catch:**
1. Reset the trap (animals avoid traps that smell like death)
2. Clean wire snares (remove blood/fur)
3. Re-bait if using bait

**Long-term:**
- Replace worn wire (breakage = lost animal)
- Sharpen deadfall triggers (tight fit = reliable trigger)

---

## The Trap Line System

**If you're stationary for weeks/months:**

1. **Establish a circuit:** 10-20 traps along a 1-2 mile loop
2. **Check daily:** Walk the circuit once in the morning, once at dusk
3. **Harvest:** Remove catches, reset traps
4. **Time investment:** 2-3 hours per day yields consistent protein

**This is how trappers historically fed themselves through the winter.**

---

## Realistic Expectations

**Success rate per trap:** 5-15% per night (varies by season, location, animal density)

**With 10 traps:** 0-2 catches per day is normal  
**Translation:** 1-2 rabbits = 1.5-3 lbs of meat = enough protein for 1-2 people for a day

**Trapping is supplemental protein, not guaranteed abundance.** Combine with foraging, fishing, and gardening.

---

## The Bottom Line

**Hunting:** High effort, uncertain reward.  
**Trapping:** Low effort, passive accumulation.

**In survival, set traps first thing in the morning. Then forage, fish, build, or rest while the traps work for you.**

**Master trapping, and you've unlocked the most energy-efficient protein source available.**
