# 13.7 Cold Water & Ice Fishing

**Purpose:** Access the high-protein yields of winter water systems. Winter fishing requires specialized safety knowledge and a shift in biology-based strategy.

---

## 1. Ice Safety (The Golden Rules)

Never step onto ice without testing it first.

### Ice Thickness Chart
| Thickness | Safety Level |
| :--- | :--- |
| **2" or less** | **STAY OFF.** Extremely dangerous. |
| **4"** | Safe for walking / ice fishing. |
| **5" - 7"** | Safe for snowmobiles or ATVs. |
| **8" - 12"** | Safe for small cars or light trucks. |
| **12" - 15"** | Safe for medium-sized trucks. |

### Visual Cues
*   **Clear Blue/Black Ice:** The strongest ice. Formed in cold, calm weather.
*   **White "Snow" Ice:** Formed from wet snow freezing on top. Only half as strong as clear ice.
*   **Grey/Dark Ice:** Indication of rot or melting. **AVOID.**

### Self-Rescue Gear
1.  **Ice Picks:** Always wear a pair around your neck. If you fall through, use them to grip the slippery edge and pull yourself out.
2.  **Spud Bar:** A heavy metal rod used to "thump" the ice ahead of you. If it goes through in one hit, the ice is too thin.
3.  **Floatation Suit:** In extreme environments, wear a suit that provides buoyancy and thermal insulation.

---

## 2. Winter Gear & Tools

### The Ice Auger
*   **Manual:** A large drill bit with a T-handle. Requires physical effort but is silent and reliable.
*   **Gas/Electric:** Faster, but heavy and requires fuel/batteries.
*   **Chisel (Spud Bar):** Use for small holes or clearing ice that froze over.

### Tip-Ups
*   **Design:** A device that sits over the hole with a spool of line submerged. A flag "tips up" when a fish pulls the line.
*   **Benefit:** Allows you to fish 5-10 holes simultaneously across a wide area.

### Jigging Rods
*   **Design:** Short, sensitive rods (24-30 inches). 
*   **Technique:** Use tiny "ice jigs" (size 10-14 hooks) tipped with wax worms or spikes. Move vertically in 2-inch increments.

---

## 3. Cold Water Biology

Fish are cold-blooded; their metabolism slows significantly in winter.

*   **Lethargy:** Fish will not chase fast-moving lures. Use slow, subtle movements.
*   **Small Portions:** Downsize your bait. A fish's stomach is smaller in winter.
*   **The Thermal Layer:** In frozen lakes, the warmest water (39°F) is at the **bottom**. Most fish will congregate in the bottom 2-3 feet of the water column.

---

## 4. Target Species (Winter)

### Lake Trout
*   **Depth:** Deep water (40-100 feet).
*   **Lure:** Heavy silver spoons or white tubes.

### Walleye
*   **Time:** 4 PM to 8 PM is the "Golden Hour."
*   **Method:** Set tip-ups near drop-offs where they move up from deep water to feed.

### Yellow Perch
*   **Habitat:** Basin flats with muddy bottoms.
*   **Method:** "Pound" the bottom with a jig to kick up dust, attracting the school.

---

## 5. Hypothermia Triage

If you or a partner get wet:
1.  **Get Out:** Use ice picks to crawl back the way you came (the ice you walked in on is known to be strong).
2.  **Roll in Snow:** Dry snow acts as a sponge, pulling moisture out of clothes.
3.  **Remove Wet Clothes:** Strip and replace with dry layers immediately.
4.  **Shelter:** Get to a vehicle or start a fire. Do NOT "tough it out."

> [!WARNING]
> Ice is never 100% safe. Stay away from areas with moving current (inlets/outlets), as the current thins the ice from below.
