# 13.8 Large Game Processing: Deer & Beyond

Harvesting a deer or elk provides hundreds of pounds of protein, but if you don't process it correctly, half of it will rot before it hits the freezer.

---

## 1. The Golden Hour (Field Dressing)
The clock starts the moment the animal dies. Heat is your enemy.

### Step 1: The Incision
1.  Place the animal on its back, head uphill.
2.  Cut around the anus (the "coring" cut) to free it from the pelvic bone. Tie it off with a piece of string to prevent leakage.
3.  Make a shallow cut from the pelvis to the sternum. Use two fingers to lift the skin away from the guts as you cut (to avoid puncturing the stomach).

### Step 2: The "Dump"
1.  Reach up into the neck and cut the windpipe (trachea) and esophagus.
2.  Pull downward. The lungs, heart, stomach, and intestines should all pull out in one large mass.
3.  Cut the diaphragm (the thin muscle separating the chest from the belly) to free the heart/lungs.

### Step 3: Cooling
1.  Wipe out the interior with a dry cloth (avoid water if possible, as it promotes bacterial growth).
2.  Prop the chest cavity open with a stick to allow airflow.
3.  **Transport:** Do not tie the deer to the hood of a car (engine heat). Get it into the shade or a cool garage immediately.

---

## 2. Skinning (The Clean Shield)
Skin the animal while it is still warm if possible; the hide comes off much easier.

1.  **Hang it:** Use a "gambrel" through the rear leg tendons to hang the animal head-down.
2.  **Ring the legs:** Cut the skin around the "knees" of all four legs.
3.  **The "Y" Cut:** Cut from the belly incision down the inside of each leg to the "rings" you just made.
4.  **The Pull:** Grasp the skin at the neck/shoulders and pull downward. Use your knife only to "nick" the white connective tissue.

---

## 3. Butchering (The Quartering Method)
If you don't have a walk-in cooler, you must "quarter" the animal to fit it into regular coolers or a fridge.

*   **Front Shoulders:** No bone attachment. Simply lift the leg and cut the muscle between the shoulder blade and the ribs.
*   **Hind Quarters:** Follow the pelvic bone until you hit the ball-and-socket hip joint. Cut the ligaments to free the leg.
*   **Backstraps (The Prize):** Long muscles running along either side of the spine from the neck to the hips. Slice along the spine and peel them out.
*   **Tenderloins:** Found *inside* the cavity, under the spine near the hips. Reach in and pull them out by hand.

---

## 4. Preservation Without a Freezer
If you are truly off-grid:

*   **Biltong/Jerky:** Slice meat 1/4" thick *against the grain*. Salt heavily and hang in a breezy, bug-proof area (See 13.2 Salting and Smoking).
*   **Canning:** Pressure canning is the only safe way to preserve meat in jars (See 4.2 Pressure Canning).
*   **Confis:** Cook meat slowly in its own fat (lard) and store in a cool crock covered by a 2-inch layer of solid fat. This keeps for months in a root cellar.

---

## 5. Waste Not
*   **Bones:** Boil for 24 hours to make bone broth (essential minerals).
*   **Tallow:** Render the fat (especially from elk/moose) for soap or candles. Note: Deer fat (suet) has a waxy taste and is better for birds or soap than for cooking.
*   **Hide:** Salt it immediately if you plan to tan it later. It will rot in 24 hours without salt.

**One deer = 50,000 calories. Respect the life taken by using every ounce.**
