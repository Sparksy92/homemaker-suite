# 16.6 Textile & Fiber Arts Tools

**Concept:** If you can't make cloth, you will eventually be naked. Clothes wear out faster than steel. This module covers the tools needed to turn raw fluff (wool, cotton, milkweed) into fabric.

---

## 1. The Drop Spindle (The Spinner's Tool)

**Function:** Twists loose fibers into strong yarn/thread.
**Benefit:** Highly portable. One ounce of wood creates miles of thread.

### Construction
1.  **The Shaft:** A straight wooden dowel or stick (12" long, pencil thick).
2.  **The Whorl (Flywheel):** A round disk (clay, wood, stone) with a hole in the center.
    *   *Weight matters:* 2-3 oz for wool, 1 oz for cotton/fine fibers.
3.  **The Hook:** A small brass hook screw or bent wire at the top of the shaft.

### Assembly
1.  Push the shaft through the whorl (mount it 2" from the bottom).
2.  Screw the hook into the top center.

### Technique: Park and Draft
1.  **Hook:** Tie a leader string to the shaft, spiral it up, and loop it on the hook.
2.  **Spin:** Twirl the spindle clockwise like a top. Let it hang.
3.  **Park:** When it stops spinning, trap the shaft between your knees.
4.  **Draft:** Pinch a tuft of wool, join it to the spinning leader. Pull back gently—spin enters the fiber, turning it into yarn.
5.  **Repeat:** Wind the new yarn onto the shaft and repeat.

## 2. Carding Combs (Fiber Prep)

**Function:** Untangles and aligns fibers before spinning. Without this, your yarn is lumpy and weak.

### Construction
1.  **Paddles:** Two rectangular wooden boards with handles (like ping pong paddles).
2.  **Teeth:** Hundreds of small finish nails driven through the board face.
    *   *Grid:* Space nails 1/8" apart.
    *   *Angle:* Bend all nails slightly (15°) towards the handle.

### Operation
1.  Load raw wool onto one comb.
2.  Brush the other comb through it (teeth opposing).
3.  The fibers are teased apart and aligned parallel ("Rolag").
4.  Remove the fluff roll—it is now ready to spin.

## 3. The Warp-Weighted Loom (The Viking Engine)

**Function:** Weaves yarn into wide sheets of cloth.
**History:** This tool clothed Europe for 3,000 years. It leans against a wall and uses gravity for tension.

### Components
1.  **Frame:** Two tall uprights (7') leaning against a wall, with a crossbeam (cloth beam) at the top.
2.  **Weights:** 20-30 heavy stones or clay donuts (1-2 lbs each).
3.  **Heddle Rod:** A horizontal bar that pulls specific threads forward.

### The Setup (Warping)
1.  **The Warp:** Tie hundreds of vertical threads to the top beam. Let them hang down.
2.  **Tension:** Tie bunches of these threads to the **Loom Weights** at the bottom. Gravity keeps them tight.
3.  **The Shed:** Separate threads into Front and Back layers.

### Weaving
1.  **Pass the Shuttle:** Throw a spool of horizontal thread (Weft) between the front/back layers.
2.  **Beat:** Use a wooden sword/stick to beat the new thread up tight against the cloth beam.
3.  **Switch:** Move the Heddle Rod to switch the front/back threads.
4.  **Repeat.**

## 4. Dyeing Tools (Chemistry)

**Mordants:** Chemicals that make dye stick.
*   **Iron Water:** Put rusty nails in vinegar. result: Saddens colors (Greys/Blacks).
*   **Alum:** Aluminum sulfate (natural mineral). Result: Brightens colors.
*   **Urine:** Stale urine (Ammonia) is a traditional mordant for Indigo.

**Dye Pots:**
*   Use Stainless Steel or Enamel. Reactive metals (Iron/Aluminum/Copper) will change the dye color.
