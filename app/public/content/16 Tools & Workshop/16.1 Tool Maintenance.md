# 16.1 Tool Maintenance

**Core Principle:** A dull tool is dangerous and inefficient. In a grid-down scenario, your tools are irreplaceable. Maintenance isn't optional—it's survival.

---

## The Tool Hierarchy: What to Prioritize

If you can only maintain 5 tools, choose these:

1. **Knife** (food prep, defense, fire starting)
2. **Axe** (firewood, shelter building)
3. **Saw** (precision cutting, less energy than axe)
4. **Shovel** (sanitation, gardening, earthworks)
5. **File/Sharpening stone** (maintains all the others)

**Everything else is secondary.**

---

## Sharpening Fundamentals

### The Angle Rule
- **Knives:** 15-20° per side (kitchen knives: 15°, bushcraft knives: 20°)
- **Axes:** 25-30° per side (more durability, less likely to chip)
- **Saws:** Specialized (requires a saw set tool—see below)

**How to find the angle:** 
- 15° = matchbook thickness under the spine
- 20° = two stacked coins under the spine
- Practice until muscle memory takes over

---

## Knife Sharpening (Step-by-Step)

### Tools Needed
- **Whetstone** (dual-grit: 1000/6000 is ideal, or 400/1000 for field use)
- **Water or oil** (depends on stone type—read the label)
- **Leather strop** (optional, for final polish)

### Process

#### Step 1: Inspect the Edge
- Run your thumb **perpendicular** to the edge (don't slide along it)
- Feel for rolled sections (edge curled over) or chips (missing metal)

#### Step 2: Coarse Grit (Repair)
**Use 400-1000 grit for damaged blades.**

1. Soak stone in water for 10 minutes (or apply oil if oil stone)
2. Hold knife at 15-20° angle
3. Push the blade **away** from you, into the stone (like you're shaving a thin layer off the stone)
4. 10 strokes on one side, flip, 10 strokes on the other
5. Repeat until you feel a **burr** (tiny raised edge on the opposite side)

**The burr tells you you've reached the edge.** If no burr forms, keep going.

#### Step 3: Fine Grit (Refine)
**Use 3000-6000 grit for polishing.**

1. Same angle, same motion
2. 5 strokes per side, alternating
3. Goal: remove the burr created in Step 2

#### Step 4: Strop (Optional, but Recommended)
1. Use a leather belt or dedicated strop
2. Pull the blade **backward** (spine leading, edge trailing) across the leather
3. 10 passes per side
4. **Effect:** Aligns microscopic teeth, makes edge shaving-sharp

### Testing Sharpness
- **Paper test:** Blade should slice through paper cleanly with no tearing
- **Thumbnail test:** Gently place edge on thumbnail (perpendicular)—sharp blade grips, dull blade slides
- **Shaving test:** Should shave arm hair cleanly (don't do this with axes)

## Axe Sharpening

**Key difference from knives:** Axes see more abuse (hitting knots, dirt), so they need a stronger, thicker edge.

### Tools Needed
- **Coarse file** (bastard or mill file)
- **Whetstone** (medium grit, 400-1000)
- **Leather strop** (optional)

### Process

#### Step 1: Secure the Axe
- Clamp the head in a vise, or wedge the handle between your knees (blade facing away)

#### Step 2: File the Edge
1. Hold file at 25-30° angle
2. Push file **away** from you (files only cut on the push stroke)
3. File from the center of the edge outward (heel to toe)
4. 10-15 strokes per side
5. **Goal:** Remove nicks, establish consistent bevel

#### Step 3: Stone the Edge
1. Use circular motions with the whetstone (unlike the straight strokes for knives)
2. Medium pressure, 20-30 circles per side
3. Feel for burr, flip when you find it

#### Step 4: Test
- **Thumbnail test** (should grip, not slide)
- **Chop test:** Sharp axe bites into wood, dull axe bounces off

**Don't over-sharpen:** An axe doesn't need to be razor-sharp. A shaving-sharp axe is fragile and will chip.

## Saw Maintenance

### Cleaning
- **After every use:** Wipe blade with an oily rag (prevents rust)
- **Monthly:** Remove sap with mineral spirits or rubbing alcohol

### Sharpening (Crosscut Saws)
**This is advanced—practice on a disposable saw first.**

**Tools needed:**
- **Triangular file** (sized to match your saw's teeth)
- **Saw set tool** (bends teeth outward for clearance)

**Process:**
1. Secure saw in a vise (teeth facing up)
2. File every other tooth (skip one, file one) at a 60° angle
3. Flip saw, file the teeth you skipped
4. Use saw set tool to bend teeth slightly outward (alternating left/right)

**Easier option:** Carry a spare saw blade. Sharpening saws is a specialized skill that takes practice.

## Handle Replacement

**Wooden handles break eventually.** Know how to replace them.

### Axe Handle Replacement

**Tools needed:**
- New handle (hickory is strongest, ash is acceptable)
- Saw or knife
- Wooden wedge (usually comes with the handle)
- Metal wedge (optional, adds security)

**Process:**
1. **Remove old handle:** Saw off flush with the axe head, drill out the remaining wood, or burn it out over coals (carefully)
2. **Fit new handle:** Slide handle into the eye (top of the axe head)
3. **Wedge it:** Drive the wooden wedge into the slot at the top of the handle (splits the wood, locks it in place)
4. **Add metal wedge (optional):** Drive perpendicular to the wooden wedge for extra security
5. **Trim excess:** Saw off protruding handle, sand smooth

**Test:** The head should not wiggle. If it does, the handle isn't seated properly—remove and retry.

### Shovel Handle Replacement
**Same principle as axes:**
1. Remove rivet or screw holding old handle
2. Slide new handle into socket
3. Drill new hole, insert bolt/rivet

## Rust Prevention and Removal

### Prevention
- **Daily:** Wipe tools clean after use
- **Weekly:** Apply thin coat of oil (motor oil, linseed oil, or cooking oil in a pinch)
- **Storage:** Hang tools off the ground, in a dry area

### Removal (Light Surface Rust)
**Method 1: Vinegar soak**
1. Submerge rusty tool in white vinegar for 3-24 hours (depends on rust severity)
2. Scrub with steel wool or wire brush
3. Rinse, dry thoroughly
4. Oil immediately (bare metal rusts fast)

**Method 2: Abrasive scrubbing**
1. Use steel wool, sandpaper (80-120 grit), or a wire brush
2. Scrub until rust is gone
3. Oil immediately

**For deep rust (pitting):** File or grind it out, then oil. Deep rust weakens metal—consider replacing the tool if structural.

---

## Field Repairs (Improvisation)

### Broken Knife Tip
**Regrind the tip:**
1. Use a file or stone to reshape the blade into a new point
2. You'll lose 1/2" - 1" of length, but the knife is still functional

### Cracked Wooden Handle
**Wrap with cordage:**
1. Tightly wrap the crack with paracord, wire, or cloth strips
2. Secure with knots or wire wrapping
3. **Temporary fix only**—replace handle when possible

### Dull Blade, No Stone
**Improvised sharpening:**
- **Concrete block:** Use the flat, rough surface as a sharpening stone (not ideal, but works)
- **River rock:** Find a smooth, flat stone, wet it, use as a whetstone
- **Leather belt:** Strop only (won't sharpen, but maintains a working edge)

---

## Tool Storage (Preventing Damage)

### Hanging vs. Stacking
- **Hang:** Axes, saws, shovels (prevents dulling, rust, and handle rot)
- **Stack:** Never. Tools touching = faster rust and damage

### Edge Protection
- **Knives:** Store in sheaths (prevent dulling and injury)
- **Axes:** Use edge guards (split garden hose works) or bury blade in a stump
- **Saws:** Hang on wall; if stored flat, protect teeth with cardboard

---

## The 10-Minute Daily Ritual

**At the end of each day:**
1. **Wipe clean:** Remove dirt, sap, moisture (30 seconds per tool)
2. **Inspect:** Check for damage, loose handles, rust (1 minute)
3. **Oil:** Light coat on all metal surfaces (1 minute)
4. **Hang:** Put tools away properly (1 minute)

**Total time:** ~5 minutes for 5-10 tools.

**Payoff:** Tools last decades instead of months.

---

## When to Replace (Not Repair)

**Replace if:**
- **Cracks in metal** (blade could snap under stress)
- **Deep pitting** (rust has eaten through >50% of blade thickness)
- **Handle splits at eye** (axe head could fly off—extremely dangerous)

**Everything else is repairable.**

---

## Emergency Tool Kit

**If you can only carry 3 maintenance items:**
1. **Small whetstone** (400/1000 grit, pocket-sized)
2. **Metal file** (sharpens axes, can shape metal)
3. **Oil (small bottle)** (rust prevention)

**With these three, you can maintain every cutting tool you own.**

## The Long-Term Mindset

**In the old days, a farmer's grandfather's axe was still in use.** The same axe. 100+ years.

**How?**
- Daily maintenance (5 minutes)
- Weekly sharpening (10 minutes)
- Yearly handle replacement (30 minutes)

**Modern culture treats tools as disposable. Survival culture treats tools as heirlooms.**

**Your mission:** Make every tool in your shed last a lifetime.
