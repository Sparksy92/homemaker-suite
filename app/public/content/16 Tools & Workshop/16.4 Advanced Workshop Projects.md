# 16.4 Advanced Workshop Projects

**Concept:** Once you have basic tools, you use them to build **machines**. These project multipliers increase your efficiency, reduce calorie expenditure, and improve quality of life.

---

## Project 1: The Shaving Horse (Woodworking Engine)

**The single most useful woodworking appliance for the off-grid homestead.** It holds wood for you so you can shape it with a drawknife. Essential for making: Axe handles, bow staves, chair legs, shingles.

### Components
1.  **The Bench (Body):** A heavy plank or half-log (6-8 feet long).
2.  **The Legs:** 3 or 4 sturdy legs splayed for stability.
3.  **The Dumbhead (Clamp):** A pivoting arm that clamps the workpiece down when you push a foot pedal.

### Construction Steps (Bodger Style)

#### Step 1: The Body
*   Find a 2x10 plank or split a 8" log, about 6 feet long.
*   Drill 2 holes at the rear (seat end) and 1 or 2 at the front (working end).
*   Insert legs (20" long) into holes. Wedge them tight.

#### Step 2: The Ramp
*   Attach a secondary plank (the "ramp") at the front, sloping up at a 20° angle. This is where the work sits.

#### Step 3: The Dumbhead Frame
*   Cut a slot in the body (or mount pivot blocks to the sides) right where the ramp ends.
*   The Dumbhead is an H-frame or T-frame that pivots on a bolt/wooden pin through this slot.
*   **Action:** When the bottom of the H (pedal) is pushed forward (by your feet), the top of the H (head) rocks back and clamps down on the ramp.

### Operation
1.  Sit on the bench.
2.  Place wood on the ramp.
3.  Push the foot pedal. The head clamps the wood.
4.  Pull the drawknife towards you. The limitless grip strength of your legs holds it steady.

## Project 2: The Brick Rocket Stove (High-Efficiency Cooking)

**Efficiency:** Burns twigs and sticks with zero smoke and high heat. Uses 10% of the fuel of an open fire.

### Materials
*   **Bricks:** ~20-25 standard red clay bricks.
*   **Mud/Clay:** To seal gaps (optional but better).
*   **Grate:** Stiff wire mesh (optional).

### Design: The "L" Chamber
The core concept is an insulated, L-shaped combustion chamber.
*   **Feed Tube:** Horizontal tube where sticks go in.
*   **Burn Tunnel:** Horizontal burning area.
*   **Heat Riser:** Vertical chimney where fire shoots up.

### Construction Steps
1.  **Level the Ground:** Clear space for a 4x4 brick foundation.
2.  **Layer 1 (The Base):** Lay bricks to creating the horizontal "L" channel (about 4" wide).
3.  **The Fuel Shelf:** Insert a thin metal plate or wire mesh horizontally in the feed tube. Sticks go *on top*, air flows *underneath*. This is critical for efficiency.
4.  **Layer 2-4 (The Riser):** Build the vertical chimney up. It should be at least 3x taller than the burn tunnel is wide (approx 12-16").
5.  **The Pot Stand:** Place two loose bricks or metal rebar on top of the chimney to hold the pot. **Gap is crucial:** Air must exit under the pot.

### Operation
1.  Light a small fire at the bottom of the chimney.
2.  Once drafting (roaring sound), push sticks into the feed tube.
3.  The insulated riser gets superheated, burning all smoke.

## Project 3: Solar Dehydrator (Passive Preservation)

**Concept:** Move warm, dry air over food using only the sun. No electricity.

### Design: The Updraft Collector
*   **Collector Box (Bottom):** A black, glass-covered box angled at the sun (45°). Heats the air.
*   **Drying Cabinet (Top):** A vented box with screened trays where food sits.
*   **Chimney Effect:** Hot air rises from the collector, passes through food, and exits top vents, carrying moisture away.

### Materials
*   Plywood or scrap lumber.
*   Black paint (non-toxic BBQ paint).
*   Window screen (fiberglass or aluminum).
*   Glass or clear polycarbonate sheet (old window sash works great).

### Construction Steps

#### Step 1: The Collector
1.  Build a shallow box (2' wide x 3' long x 4" deep).
2.  Paint inside **Matte Black**.
3.  Cut an air intake vent at the very bottom (cover with screen).
4.  Cover top with glass/plastic.
5.  Leave the top end open (this leads to the cabinet).

#### Step 2: The Cabinet
1.  Build a box (2' wide x 2' high x 1' deep) with a door.
2.  Install shelf runners (strips of wood) inside.
3.  Build rectangular frames with window screen stretched over them (Food Trays).
4.  Cut a hole in the **bottom** (connects to Collector) and **top** (exhaust vent).

#### Step 3: Assembly
1.  Mount the cabinet on legs (waist high).
2.  Attach the collector to the bottom hole of the cabinet, angling it down towards the sun.
3.  Seal all joints with caulk/mud.

### Performance
*   **Capacity:** 5-10 lbs of produce per batch.
*   **Time:** 1 sunny day for herbs/greens, 2 days for apples/tomatoes.
*   **Advantage:** Keeps UV light OFF the food (preserves vitamins) while using solar heat.

## Project 4: The Spring Pole Lathe (Reciprocating Engine)

**The Ancient Power Tool.** Before electric motors, this is how table legs, bowls, and tool handles were made.

### Concept
*   **Power Source:** A flexible sapling (pole) overhead connected to a foot pedal by a rope.
*   **Action:** Push pedal down -> Rope spins wood -> Cut with chisel. Release pedal -> Pole pulls rope up -> Wood spins back (reset).
*   **Cutting:** You only cut on the downstroke (towards you).

### Construction
1.  **Bed:** Two heavy rails (2x6) mounted on legs, waist high.
2.  **Poppets (Headstocks):** Two upright wooden blocks that slide between the rails. They hold the metal spikes (centers) that grip the wood.
3.  **Tool Rest:** A horizontal bar in front of the wood to steady your chisel.
4.  **The Pole:** A green sapling (10-15 ft long) anchored nearby with the tip hanging over the lathe. Or, a bungee cord system in a garage.

### Operation
1.  Wrap the drive rope *around* the wood piece twice.
2.  Push pedal: Wood spins *towards* you. **CUT NOW.**
3.  Lift foot: Wood spins *away*. **Relax.**
4.  Rhythm: Push-Cut, Lift-Rest. Push-Cut, Lift-Rest.

## Project 5: The Cedar Smokehouse (Meat Preservation)

**Cold Smoking:** The only way to preserve meat in wet climates where sun-drying fails.

### Design: The "Outhouse" Style
A tall, narrow wooden shed (3'x3'x6').
*   **Firebox:** Located 3-5 feet *away* from the shed (connected by a pipe) OR a very small smoldering fire at the very bottom.
*   **Racks:** Removable wooden dowels near the top.
*   **Vents:** Adjustable roof vent to control draft.

### Materials
*   **Untreated Wood:** Cedar or Pine (for structure, not fuel!). **NEVER use pressure-treated wood.**
*   **Pipe:** 6" Stove pipe (if using offset firebox).
*   **Screening:** To keep flies out (Crucial).

### The Process (Cold Smoke)
1.  **Cure:** Salt/sugar cure the meat first (draws out moisture).
2.  **Hang:** Place meat on racks.
3.  **Smoke:** Generate smoke *without heat* (target temp < 100°F).
4.  **Time:** 1-2 weeks for hams, 2-3 days for jerky.

## Project 6: The Apple Cider Press (Mechanical Advantage)

**Harvest Efficiency:** Turn 100 lbs of apples into 8 gallons of cider (vinegar/alcohol potential) in one hour.

### Components
1.  **The Frame:** Massive 4x4 timber bolted together. Must withstand 2+ tons of pressure.
2.  **The Screw:** A large acme-thread screw (from an old workbench vise or car jack).
3.  **The Cheeses:** Apples are wrapped in cloth (cheeses) and stacked with wooden "slats" between them.

### Construction
1.  Build a rectangle frame.
2.  Mount the screw pointing down through the top beam.
3.  Build a slatted wooden tub (bucket with gaps) at the bottom.

### Operation
1.  **Scratting (Crushing):** You MUST crush apples into pulp first (use a bucket and a 4x4 post pounder).
2.  **Building the Cheese:** Place cloth in tub, fill with pulp, fold cloth over. Place wooden board on top. Repeat.
3.  **Pressing:** Turn the screw. Juice flows through the slats into a collection bucket.
