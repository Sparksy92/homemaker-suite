# 16.5 Metalworking & Forging

**Concept:** The Iron Age. Wood rots, stone breaks, but steel endures. With heat and force, you can recycle the ruins of the old world (car springs, rebar, spikes) into tools for the new one.

---

## 1. The Brake Drum Forge (The Standard Scrap Build)

You need to heat steel to orange-hot (1800°F). A campfire won't cut it. You need **forced air**.

### Components
1.  **The Pot:** A cast iron brake drum (rear drum from a truck is best). It withstands intense heat.
2.  **The Tuyere (Air Pipe):** 2" black iron pipe (T-fitting + nipple). Connected to the bottom of the drum.
3.  **The Air Source:** Hair dryer, leaf blower, or mattress pump.
4.  **The Stand:** Steel legs or a cinder block tower to hold the drum waist-high.

### Assembly
1.  Flip brake drum concave side up.
2.  Bolt a "floor flange" over the center hole.
3.  Screw the 2" pipe nipple into the flange (pointing down).
4.  Screw a **T-fitting** onto the nipple.
5.  **The Blast:** Connect air source to the *side* of the T.
6.  **The Ash Gate:** Screw a cap onto the *bottom* of the T. (Unscrew this to dump ash).

### Fuel
*   **Coal:** Bituminous (soft) coal is best.
*   **Charcoal:** Lump charcoal (not briquettes!) works perfectly but burns fast.

## 2. The Improvised Anvil

You need a surface harder than the hot steel you are hitting.

### Options (Ranked)
1.  **Real Anvil:** Gold standard. (London Pattern).
2.  **Railroad Track:** The classic substitute. Mount it vertically (on end) for more mass under the hammer.
3.  **Sledgehammer Head:** A 10-20lb sledge head embedded in a log.
4.  **Forklift Tine:** Massive, hardened steel chunk.
5.  **Flat Rock:** A huge granite boulder. (Will spark and eventually crack, but works).

**Mounting:** Whatever you use, secure it to a heavy stump. The face should be at knuckle height when your arm is hanging loose.

## 3. Basic Metallurgy (The Colors of Heat)

Forging is painting with temperature.

| Color | Temp (°F) | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yellow-White** | 2300° | **Welding.** Steel melts/burns soon after. Danger zone. |
| **Bright Yellow** | 2100° | **Heavy Forging.** Moving lots of metal effortlessly. |
| **Orange** | 1800° | **Normal Forging.** Shape, bend, taper. |
| **Cherry Red** | 1500° | **Finishing.** Smooth out hammer marks. Stop hitting soon. |
| **Dull Red** | 1200° | **Critical Temp.** Metal is magnetic again. Do not hit! (Stress cracks). |
| **Black** | <900° | **Cold.** Hitting now does nothing but dent your anvil. |

### Annealing vs. Hardening

*   **Annealing (Softening):** Making steel soft to file/drill.
    *   Heat to Cherry Red (non-magnetic).
    *   Bury in bucket of wood ash / vermiculite.
    *   Let cool overnight (very slow).

*   **Hardening (Strengthening):** Making the edge hard.
    *   Heat to Cherry Red.
    *   Quench quickly (Water or Oil, depending on steel).
    *   *Note: Steel is now brittle like glass. You MUST temper it.*

*   **Tempering (Toughness):** Removing brittleness.
    *   Clean the steel.
    *   Heat gently to ~400°F (Straw/Wheat color).
    *   Air cool.

## 4. Project 1: Making Tongs

**The Paradox:** You need tongs to hold hot steel, but you need hot steel to make tongs.
**Solution:** Start with long stock (rebar) that you can hold by hand.

### Step-by-Step (Bolt Tongs)
**Material:** 2 pieces of 1/2" Rebar (18" long each).

1.  **The Jaw:** Heat the tip (2"). Flatten it on the anvil.
2.  **The Boss (Pivot):** Heat behind the jaw. Rotate 90°. Flatten this section. (This creates a step).
3.  **The Reins (Handles):** Heat behind the boss. Draw out (taper) the handles to be comfortable.
4.  **Drill:** Punch or drill a hole through the center of the Boss on both pieces.
5.  **Rivet:** Insert a steel pin/nail. Heat the pin. Hammer it flat on both sides to lock the halves together.
6.  **Adjust:** Heat the jaws and clamp them onto the stock size you want to hold. Let cool.

**Now you can hold short hot metal. You have unlocked the rest of the forge.**
