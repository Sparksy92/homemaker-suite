# 14.1 Herbal Medicine

**Critical Disclaimer:** This is educational information for emergency/grid-down scenarios. Always consult a medical professional when available. Some herbs interact with medications or have contraindications.

---

## The Backyard Pharmacy: 6 Essential Medicinal Herbs

These plants are easy to grow, identify, and prepare. Most are already in your garden or grow wild nearby.

---

## 1. Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) - "The Soldier's Herb"

![Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)](/images/botany/yarrow.jpg)

**Primary Use:** Hemostatic (stops bleeding), antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory.

> [!NOTE]
> **Scientific Evidence:** Clinical studies have shown that Yarrow ointment significantly reduces pain and edema in episiotomy wounds and exhibits potent inhibitory effects against *Staphylococcus* species. See [Section 14.5: Evidence-Based Botanical Studies](file:///c:/Users/Blair/Downloads/Homemaker%20Suite/app/public/content/14%20Health%20&%20First%20Aid/14.5%20Evidence-Based%20Botanical%20Studies.md) for research details.

### Identification
- **Leaves:** Feathery, fern-like (looks like a "thousand leaves")
- **Flowers:** Flat-topped clusters of tiny white or pink flowers
- **Stem:** Single upright stem, 1-3 feet tall
- **Smell:** Pungent, slightly sweet

**Where it grows:** Roadsides, meadows, disturbed soil (very common).

### Medicinal Uses

#### External (Wounds, Bleeding)
**Fresh leaf poultice:**
1. Crush or chew fresh leaves to a pulp
2. Apply directly to cuts, scrapes, nosebleeds
3. Secure with a bandage
4. **Effect:** Stops bleeding in minutes (contains astringent tannins)

**Infused oil (for bruises, sprains):**
1. Fill jar halfway with dried yarrow flowers
2. Cover with olive oil
3. Let sit in a sunny window for 2 weeks, shake daily
4. Strain, store in dark bottle
5. **Use:** Massage into sore muscles, bruises

#### Internal (Fever, Cold/Flu)
**Yarrow tea:**
1. 1-2 tsp dried flowers per cup of boiling water
2. Steep 10 minutes, strain
3. Drink 2-3 cups per day
4. **Effect:** Induces sweating (breaks fevers), antimicrobial

**Caution:** Avoid during pregnancy (stimulates uterus). Rare allergic reactions in people allergic to ragweed.

## 2. Comfrey (Symphytum officinale) - "Knitbone"

![Comfrey (Symphytum officinale)](/images/botany/comfrey.jpg)

**Primary Use:** Accelerates healing of bruises, sprains, fractures.

### Identification
- **Leaves:** Large, lance-shaped, rough/hairy texture
- **Flowers:** Bell-shaped, purple or cream colored, hang in clusters
- **Height:** 2-3 feet
- **Root:** Deep taproot, black outside, white/cream inside

**Where it grows:** Moist soil, near streams, shade-tolerant.

### Medicinal Uses

#### External Only (Do Not Ingest)
**Modern research shows comfrey contains pyrrolizidine alkaloids (PAs) that can damage the liver when ingested. Use externally only.**

**Comfrey poultice (sprains, bruises, fractures):**
1. Mash fresh leaves or grate fresh root into a paste
2. Apply to affected area (not on open wounds—only intact skin)
3. Cover with cloth, leave for 2-4 hours
4. Repeat 2-3 times daily
5. **Effect:** Contains allantoin (promotes cell growth), reduces swelling

**Comfrey salve:**
1. Make infused oil (same method as yarrow)
2. Melt 1 oz beeswax per cup of oil
3. Pour into tins, let cool
4. **Use:** Rub on bruises, sore joints, old scars

**Why it works:** Allantoin stimulates tissue regeneration. Historically used on broken bones under casts.

## 4. Calendula (Calendula officinalis) - "Pot Marigold"

![Calendula (Calendula officinalis)](/images/botany/calendula.jpg)

**Primary Use:** Skin regeneration, antimicrobial, and radiation damage mitigation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Scientific Evidence:** Randomized controlled trials have demonstrated Calendula's superior performance in reducing radiation-induced dermatitis compared to standard pharmaceutical creams. It is also clinically proven to reduce the surface area of chronic venous leg ulcers. See [Section 14.5: Botanical Studies](file:///c:/Users/Blair/Downloads/Homemaker%20Suite/app/public/content/14%20Health%20&%20First%20Aid/14.5%20Evidence-Based%20Botanical%20Studies.md).

### Identification
- **Flowers:** Bright orange or yellow, daisy-like (the medicinal part)
- **Leaves:** Oblong, slightly sticky
- **Growth:** Garden plant, reseeds easily

**Where it grows:** Gardens (very easy to cultivate).

### Medicinal Uses

**Calendula salve (cuts, burns, rashes):**
1. Fill jar with dried calendula petals
2. Cover with olive oil, let infuse 2 weeks
3. Strain, melt 1 oz beeswax per cup of oil
4. Pour into tins
5. **Use:** Apply to minor burns, diaper rash, eczema, slow-healing wounds

**Calendula wash (infected wounds):**
1. Steep 2 tbsp dried flowers in 1 cup boiling water for 15 minutes
2. Strain, let cool
3. Use as a rinse for cuts, scrapes, infected wounds
4. **Effect:** Antimicrobial, reduces inflammation

**Why it works:** Contains flavonoids and saponins (antimicrobial), stimulates collagen production.

## 5. Echinacea (Echinacea purpurea) - "Purple Coneflower"

![Purple Coneflower (Echinacea purpurea)](/images/botany/echinacea.jpg)

**Primary Use:** Immune support, reduces duration of colds/flu.

### Identification
- **Flower:** Purple-pink petals drooping around a spiky orange/brown cone
- **Leaves:** Rough, lance-shaped
- **Height:** 2-4 feet
- **Root:** Thick taproot (most medicinal)

**Where it grows:** Gardens, prairies (native to North America).

### Medicinal Uses

**Echinacea tincture (alcohol extraction):**
1. Chop fresh or dried root (root is strongest, but flowers/leaves also work)
2. Fill jar 1/3 full with plant material
3. Cover with 80+ proof alcohol (vodka, brandy)
4. Seal, shake daily for 4 weeks
5. Strain, store in dark dropper bottle
6. **Dosage:** 30 drops (1 dropperful) 3x daily at first sign of illness

**Echinacea tea (less potent than tincture):**
1. Simmer 1 tsp dried root in 1 cup water for 15 minutes
2. Add flowers/leaves, steep 5 more minutes
3. Strain, drink 3 cups daily during illness

**Effect:** Stimulates white blood cell production, reduces inflammation.

**Caution:** Don't use continuously (take breaks every 2 weeks). Avoid if you have autoimmune conditions.

## 6. Peppermint (Mentha × piperita)

![Peppermint (Mentha piperita)](/images/botany/peppermint.jpg)

**Primary Use:** Digestive issues, nausea, headaches.

### Identification
- **Leaves:** Opposite, toothed edges, strong mint smell when crushed
- **Stem:** Square (characteristic of mint family)
- **Growth:** Spreads aggressively (plant in a container)

**Where it grows:** Gardens, moist soil, partial shade.

### Medicinal Uses

**Peppermint tea (nausea, indigestion, gas):**
1. Steep 1 tbsp fresh leaves (or 1 tsp dried) in 1 cup boiling water
2. Cover and steep 10 minutes (traps volatile oils)
3. Strain, sip slowly
4. **Effect:** Relaxes digestive muscles, reduces cramping

**Peppermint steam (sinus congestion, headaches):**
1. Boil water, pour into a bowl
2. Add a handful of fresh peppermint
3. Lean over bowl, drape towel over head to trap steam
4. Breathe deeply for 5-10 minutes
5. **Effect:** Opens sinuses, relieves headache

**Peppermint oil (topical for headaches):**
1. Infuse peppermint in oil (same method as yarrow)
2. Apply 1-2 drops to temples, back of neck
3. **Effect:** Cooling sensation, reduces tension headaches

**Caution:** Don't give to infants (menthol can cause breathing issues).

## Making Tinctures (Alcohol Extraction)

**Why tinctures?** Alcohol extracts medicinal compounds that water can't, and preserves them for years.

### Basic Tincture Method (Folk Method)

**Supplies:**
- Clean glass jar with lid
- Dried or fresh herb
- High-proof alcohol (vodka 80+ proof, Everclear 190 proof is best)
- Cheesecloth or fine strainer
- Dark dropper bottles for storage

**Process:**
1. **Fill:** Fill jar 1/3 full with dried herb (or 1/2 full if using fresh)
2. **Cover:** Pour alcohol over herbs until covered by 2 inches
3. **Seal and label:** Write herb name and date on jar
4. **Macerate:** Shake daily for 4-6 weeks (2 weeks minimum)
5. **Strain:** Press through cheesecloth, bottle in dark glass
6. **Store:** Tinctures last 5+ years in a cool, dark place

**Dosage (general):** 30-60 drops (1-2 dropperfuls) 3x daily.

## Making Salves (Oil + Beeswax)

**Why salves?** Concentrated healing oils in a spreadable, shelf-stable form.

### Basic Salve Recipe

**Supplies:**
- 1 cup infused oil (yarrow, calendula, comfrey)
- 1 oz beeswax (pastilles or grated)
- Small tins or jars
- Double boiler (or pot + heat-safe bowl)

**Process:**
1. **Melt:** Heat oil and beeswax together in double boiler until wax dissolves
2. **Test:** Drop a small amount on a cold plate—if too soft, add more wax; too hard, add more oil
3. **Pour:** Pour into tins while still liquid
4. **Cool:** Let sit undisturbed for 2 hours until fully solidified
5. **Label:** Write contents and date

**Shelf life:** 1-2 years (longer if you add vitamin E oil as preservative).

## Safety and Ethics

**Identification:** Be 100% certain before using any plant. Cross-reference with multiple sources.

**Allergies:** Always test a small amount first (skin patch test for topicals, tiny dose for internal).

**Pregnancy/Nursing:** Avoid yarrow, comfrey, and most strong herbs unless guided by a professional.

**Not a replacement:** Herbal medicine is for minor ailments and emergencies. Serious conditions need professional care when available.

**Sustainable harvesting:** Never take more than 1/3 of a wild plant population. Grow your own when possible.
