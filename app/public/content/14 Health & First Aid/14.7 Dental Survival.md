# 14.7 Dental Survival & Oral Health

> [!WARNING]
> A dental infection (abscess) can spread to the bloodstream or brain, becoming life-threatening. In a long-term survival scenario, oral hygiene is not about aesthetics—it is about avoiding sepsis.

---

## 1. Prevention (The Only Real Cure)
Without modern fillings or root canals, keeping your teeth intact is priority one.

### Improvised Tools
- **The Chew Stick:** Find a twig from a non-toxic tree (Birch, Willow, or Dogwood). Fray the end by chewing it or pounding it with a rock. The fibers act as natural bristles.
- **Floss:** Use high-test (thin) fishing line or strong sewing thread. Sterilize before use.
- **Toothpaste:** 
    - **Baking Soda:** Excellent abrasive.
    - **Salt:** Antimicrobial.
    - **Activated Charcoal:** Absorbs toxins and bacteria.
    - **Method:** Create a paste with a little water or coconut oil.

---

## 2. Managing Dental Pain
Dental pain is distracting and debilitating.

### Clove Oil (The Gold Standard)
- **Active Ingredient:** Eugenol (a natural anesthetic and antiseptic).
- **Use:** Soak a tiny piece of cotton in clove oil and pack it directly against the painful tooth or into the cavity.
- **Note:** It will numb the area for 1-3 hours.

### Willow Bark (Aspirin)
- Chew on the inner bark of a Willow tree (Salix species).
- Contains salicin, which reduces inflammation and pain.

---

## 3. Emergency Repairs

### Lost Filling or Broken Tooth
If a tooth is sensitive to air/cold:
- **Temporary Plug:** Use a mix of **zinc oxide powder and clove oil** (temp dental cement) if available.
- **Improvised Plug:** Use sugarless gum or a piece of candle wax to seal the hole and protect the nerve.

### Re-planting a Knocked Out Tooth
If a tooth is knocked out cleanly (not broken):
- **Handle by the crown**, never the root.
- **Rinse gently** with saline or milk (do not scrub).
- **Re-insert immediately** into the socket if possible.
- **Transport in milk** or inside your own cheek (next to the gum) if it won't stay in. Success drops significantly after 30 minutes.

---

## 4. The Dangerous Abscess
An abscess is an infection under the tooth or in the gum.

### Signs of Danger:
- Severe, throbbing pain.
- Swelling in the gum (appears like a "pimple" or boil).
- **Red Flag:** Swelling that moves into the neck or under the tongue (affects breathing).
- Fever and chills.

### Treatment:
- **Hot Salt Water Rinses:** 1 tsp salt in warm water. Rinse every hour to draw out the infection.
- **Natural Antibiotics:** Use high doses of Garlic or Echinacea tincture.
- **Drainage:** If a visible "head" forms on the gum, it may be lanced with a **sterile** needle to relieve pressure, but do not dig into the tooth.

---

## 5. Extractions (Post-Collapse Only)
Extracting a tooth is a dangerous, final resort. It creates an open wound in the jaw that is highly prone to infection.

1. **Only extract if:** The patient has a fever, the tooth is loose and rotted, and all other pain management has failed.
2. **Numbness:** Apply high-proof alcohol or clove oil.
3. **Elevate:** Use a sterile tool to carefully "rock" the tooth back and forth to break the ligaments. Never pull straight up.
4. **Post-op:** Have the patient bite down on a clean cloth for 2 hours. Rinse with salt water twice daily.
