# 14.4 Primitive Antimicrobials & Natural Antibiotics

In a long-term survival situation where conventional pharmacies are inaccessible, understanding how to identify and process nature's "pharmacy" is a critical skill. This guide focuses on potent, scientifically-backed (historically and in modern herbalism) antimicrobials that can be found in the wild.

> [!WARNING]
> Natural antimicrobials are not a substitute for professional medical care when available. Improper identification of plants or fungi can be fatal. Always perform a "contact test" for allergies before applying or consuming new substances.

---

## 1. Usnea (Old Man's Beard)

![Usnea (Old Man's Beard)](/images/botany/usnea.jpg)
![Usnea Stretch Test](/images/botany/usnea_stretch.jpg)

Usnea is a lichen (a symbiotic relationship between algae and fungus) that grows on trees. It contains **Usnic Acid**, a potent antimicrobial effective against Gram-positive bacteria like *Staphylococcus* and *Streptococcus*.

### Discovery & Identification
- **The Stretch Test**: This is the definitive test. Gently pull a strand of Usnea apart. It should have a green/gray outer sheath and a distinct **white, elastic inner core** that stretches like a rubber band before breaking. No white cord? It’s not Usnea.
- **Habitat**: Found worldwide in clean-air environments, draped over conifer and hardwood branches.

### Processing for Survival
- **Wound Powder**: Dry the Usnea and grind it into a fine powder. Pack it directly into shallow, infected wounds.
- **Dual Extraction Tincture**: Usnic acid is not very soluble in water. To get the full spectrum of medicine, soak clean Usnea in 100-proof alcohol (Vodka) for 2 months. 
- **Internal Use**: Used historically for respiratory infections and urinary tract issues.

## 2. Pine Resin (Nature's Liquid Bandage)

![Pine Resin](/images/botany/pine_resin.jpg)

Coniferous trees (Pine, Spruce, Fir) produce resin as their own immune response to seal wounds. It is naturally antiseptic and hydrophobic.

### Application
- **Direct Seal**: In an emergency, fresh, sticky resin can be applied directly to a clean cut. It acts as a waterproof barrier while the antiseptic terpenes inhibit bacterial growth.
- **The "Drawing Salve"**: Melt resin with fat (lard or tallow) and a bit of charcoal or beeswax. This salve is legendary for "drawing out" splinters, boils, and deep-seated infections.

## 3. The Berberine Plants (Natural Yellow Antibiotics)

![Berberine Yellow Root (Oregon Grape)](/images/botany/oregon_grape_root.jpg)

Berberine is a bright yellow alkaloid found in the roots and inner bark of several survival plants.

### Sources
- **Oregon Grape / Barberry**: Found in many temperate forests.
- **Goldenseal**: A high-value forest floor herb.
- **Coptis (Goldthread)**: Found in boggy northern woods.

### Identifying the Medicine
- Scratch the bark or cut a root. If it is **neon yellow**, it likely contains berberine.
- **Use Case**: Potent for digestive infections (Giardia, bacterial diarrhea) and as a topical wash for infected eyes or skin.

## 4. Fungal Styptics & Cautery
Fungi are the immune system of the forest. Some are physical tools as much as medicine.

### Amadou (Horse Hoof Fungus)

![Amadou (Horse Hoof Fungus)](/images/botany/amadou.jpg)

- **Identification**: Looks like a horse's hoof growing on dead birch.
- **Styptic Layer**: The soft, suede-like layer (trama) inside the fungus is incredibly absorbent and contains chemicals that promote clotting. Press it against a bleeding wound.

### Birch Polypore

![Birch Polypore](/images/botany/birch_polypore.jpg)

- **The Bandage Fungus**: Slices of this fungus can be used as a sterile, antimicrobial bandage. It contains betulinic acid, which is mildly antibiotic and anti-inflammatory.

## 5. Grandma's Alchemy: Fermented Remedies
Simple kitchen "ferments" that create powerful antimicrobial environments.

### Fermented Garlic Honey
Garlic contains allicin; honey is osmotic and antibacterial. Together, they create a shelf-stable "survival penicillin."
- **How-to**: Fill a jar with peeled garlic cloves. Cover with raw honey. Flip the jar daily for 2 weeks as it bubbles.
- **Use**: Eat one clove at the first sign of a sore throat or infection.

### Fire Cider
A vinegar-based tonic designed to "blast" the system with circulation and antimicrobials.
- **Ingredients**: Apple Cider Vinegar, Horseradish, Ginger, Garlic, Onion, Cayenne.
- **Steeping**: Let sit for 4 weeks before straining.
