# 14.10 Psychological First Aid & Triage

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In a crisis, your mind is your most valuable survival tool—and the most fragile. This guide covers how to prioritize patients during a mass event (Medical Triage) and how to manage the psychological fallout of survival (Psychological First Aid).

---

## 1. Medical Triage: The SALT System
When injuries exceed resources, triage is the process of deciding who to treat first. The **SALT System** is the modern standard.

### Step 1: Sort
- **Global Sort:** Shout: "If you can hear me, move to [designated area]." Those who move are **Minor (Green)**.
- **Wave:** Ask those remaining to wave. Those who wave/move are **Delayed (Yellow)**.
- **Still:** Those who do not move or wave must be assessed first. They are **Immediate (Red)** or **Deceased (Black)**.

### Step 2: Assess & Life-Saving Interventions
For the "Still" group, provide immediate help (30 seconds max per person):
1. **Control Major Bleeding:** Apply tourniquet or pressure.
2. **Open Airway:** If not breathing, open airway. If they start breathing, they are **Immediate (Red)**.
3. **Approve:** If they still don't breathe after opening airway, they are **Deceased (Black)**. Move to the next person.

| Tag Color | Meaning | Priority |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RED (Immediate)** | Life-threatening but treatable | High |
| **YELLOW (Delayed)** | Serious but not life-threatening yet | Medium |
| **GREEN (Minor)** | "Walking wounded" | Low |
| **BLACK (Deceased)** | Dead or injuries incompatible with life | None |

## 2. Psychological First Aid (PFA)
PFA is not therapy; it is active support to reduce immediate stress and promote long-term coping.

### Use the "RAPID" Model:
- **R - Rapport:** Introduce yourself, provide basic needs (water, blanket), and listen without judgment.
- **A - Assessment:** Identify the most pressing psychological needs (e.g., loss of a child, fear of fire).
- **P - Prioritization:** Address the most severe reactions first.
- **I - Intervention:** Use calming techniques. Encourage "Square Breathing" (4s inhale, 4s hold, 4s exhale, 4s hold).
- **D - Disposition:** Plan the next steps. Do not leave the person isolated. Give them a simple, purposeful task.

---

## 3. Homestead Burnout & Cabin Fever
In long-term isolation (e.g., a harsh winter), mental health decay can lead to group conflict or suicide.

### Prevention:
- **Purposeful Routine:** Maintain a strict schedule for work, meals, and rest.
- **Micro-Celebrations:** Mark small victories and holidays. Mental morale requires ritual.
- **Conflict Resolution:** Address grievances immediately. resentment is toxic in small groups.

---

## 4. Managing Shock
Shock is the body's cardiovascular response to severe trauma or fear.

### Physical Signs:
- Pale, cold, clammy skin.
- Rapid, weak pulse.
- Rapid, shallow breathing.
- Confusion or anxiety.

### Treatment:
- **Elevate Feet:** 6-12 inches (unless head/neck injury).
- **Keep Warm:** Use blankets or body heat.
- **Keep Calm:** Talk to the person constantly. Panic consumes oxygen and energy the body needs for repair.
- **Do not give food or drink** if they might need surgery or have abdominal wounds.
