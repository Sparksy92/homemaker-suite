# 21.5 Seed Saving for Survival

If you buy seeds every spring, you are not a farmer; you are a consumer. To survive, you must save seed.

## 1. The Golden Rule: Open Pollinated (OP) vs Hybrid (F1)
*   **Open Pollinated / Heirloom**: These seeds breed true. The baby looks like the parent. **SAVE THESE.**
*   **Hybrid (F1)**: These are crosses. The baby will be a random mutation or sterile. **DO NOT SAVE THESE.**

## 2. Isolation Distances
Plants are promiscuous. If you grow Sweet Corn next to Dent Corn, they will cross-pollinate and ruin both.
*   **Beans/Peas**: Self-pollinating. Low risk. 10-20 ft distance.
*   **Tomatoes**: Mostly self. 10-20 ft distance.
*   **Corn**: Wind pollinated. High risk. Needs **1/2 mile** isolation or "Time Isolation" (plant 3 weeks apart).
*   **Squash**: Insect pollinated. High risk. 1/2 mile distance.

## 3. How to Save Specific Seeds

### Wet Seeds (Tomatoes, Cucumbers, Melons)
1.  **Fermentation**: Scoop seeds + jelly/pulp into a jar. Add a splash of water.
2.  **Rot**: Let it sit for 3 days until mold forms. This kills seed-borne diseases and removes the germination inhibitor.
3.  **Wash**: Rinse in a strainer. Good seeds sink; bad seeds float.
4.  **Dry**: On a coffee filter or screen for 2 weeks.

### Dry Seeds (Beans, Peas, Corn, Wheat)
1.  **Mature**: Let the pod/ear dry ON THE PLANT until brown and crispy.
2.  **Shell**: Remove from pod.
3.  **Dry**: Air dry for another 2 weeks indoors until rock hard. (Thumbnail test: If you can dent it, it's not dry yet).

### Biennials (Carrots, Beets, Onions, Cale)
These don't produce seed until Year 2.
1.  **Overwinter**: Keep the root in the ground (mulched) or in a root cellar.
2.  **Spring**: Replant the root. It will send up a flower stalk.
3.  **Harvest**: Collect seeds in late summer of Year 2.
