# 9.3 Autumn: Harvest (September - November)

Autumn is the season of transition and preparation. Your main objective is storing the root crops, securing winter fuel, and weatherproofing the homestead shell before the ground freezes.

## The Vibe
Golden horizontal light, frost warnings, curing vegetables, woodsmoke, and hunkering down.

---

## 🌾 The Pantry & Storage
Securing bulk food reserves and transitioning root crops into long-term winter storage.

*   **Root Cellaring & Curing**:
    *   **Squash & Pumpkins**: Cure in a warm, dry room (~80°F) for 10 days to harden skins, then store in a cool pantry (~55°F).
    *   **Carrots & Beets**: Pack in boxes of damp sand or sawdust to maintain humidity and prevent shriveling.
    *   **Potatoes**: Store in dark, well-ventilated bins at 40°F. Never store potatoes next to onions; onions release gases that cause potatoes to sprout.
    *   Read [Root Cellaring Guide](#/library?folder=4%20Preservation&file=4.5%20Root%20Cellaring.md).
*   **Foraging Acorns & Walnuts**: Harvest fallen black walnuts and oak acorns. Acorns must be shelled and leached in multiple changes of boiling water to remove bitter tannic acid before grinding into flour. Read [Tree Knowledge](#/library?folder=12%20Foraging%20%26%20Wildcrafting&file=12.2%20Tree%20Knowledge.md) and [Wild Harvest & Processing](#/library?folder=12%20Foraging%20%26%20Wildcrafting&file=12.4%20Wild%20Harvest%20%26%20Processing.md). Check poisonous look-alikes [Poisonous Look-alikes](#/library?folder=12%20Foraging%20%26%20Wildcrafting&file=12.5%20Poisonous%20Look-alikes.md).
*   **Protein Preservation**: Salt and smoke fish or small game harvested in autumn. Refer to [Salting and Smoking Guide](#/library?folder=13%20Meat%20%26%20Protein%20Security&file=13.2%20Salting%20and%20Smoking.md).

---

## 🌱 The Garden
Putting the soil to bed and planning for spring.

*   **Garlic Planting**: Plant garlic cloves 2 inches deep and 6 inches apart in October. Cover with 4 inches of straw mulch. Garlic roots grow in autumn, go dormant in winter, and shoot up in early spring.
*   **Winterizing Beds**: Pull dead tomato vines and beans. Cover exposed garden soil with 3 inches of shredded leaves or compost to prevent winter erosion and feed earthworms. Read [Soil Health Manual](#/library?folder=5%20Gardening&file=5.2%20Soil%20Health.md).
*   **Tool Storage**: Clean soil from shovels, hoes, and pruners. Wipe metal blades with an oiled rag to prevent rust, and sand wooden handles to avoid splinters. Read [Tool Maintenance](#/library?folder=16%20Tools%20%26%20Workshop&file=16.1%20Tool%20Maintenance.md).

---

## 🏡 Home & Infrastructure
Weatherproofing the shelter envelope and checking backup heating resources.

*   **Thermal Envelope Checks**: Inspect door sweeps and seal windows with weatherstripping. Insulate exposed outdoor pipes and pump house heads. Read [Weatherproofing Manual](#/library?folder=17%20Shelter%20%26%20Weatherproofing&file=17.2%20Weatherproofing.md).
*   **Woodstove & Flue Cleaning**: Clean the chimney flue using a wire brush to remove creosote accumulation. Inspect firebricks and check door gaskets for airtight seals. Read [Off-Grid Heating](#/library?folder=18%20Energy%20%26%20Lighting&file=18.3%20Off-Grid%20Heating.md).
*   **Water Line Drainage**: Drain outdoor hoses and gravity lines to prevent frost expansion damage.

---

## 🪵 Rituals
*   **The Harvest Feast**: Gather with friends to consume the fresh harvests. Roast squash, bake bread, and open early fermented preserves.
*   **Putting the Ground to Bed**: A quiet walk around the quieted, mulched garden beds. Acknowledge the soil for its abundance and celebrate the upcoming period of rest.
