# 27.2 Wood Gasification: Run Your Truck on Sticks

**Concept:** Turning solid wood into a flammable gas (Hydrogen + Carbon Monoxide) to run internal combustion engines. This is not theory; this is how Europe survived WWII.

## 1. Cultural History: The Imbert Generator
During World War II, petrol was scarce in Europe.
*   **France & Germany:** Over 1 million vehicles were converted to run on *Holzgas* (Wood Gas).
*   **The Design:** The "Imbert" downdraft gasifier was the standard. It was reliable, relatively clean, and strictly mechanical.
*   **Nordic Countries:** Even today, Finland and Sweden maintain this knowledge as a national security contingency.

## 2. The Science
You are **not** just burning wood (combustion). You are **roasting** wood in a starved-oxygen environment (pyrolysis) to crack the chemical bonds.

### The Zones:
1.  **Drying Zone (Top):** Wood heats up, water evaporates.
2.  **Pyrolysis Zone:** Wood turns into charcoal, releasing tar and dirty gas.
3.  **Combustion Zone (Middle):** A small amount of air is injected (via nozzles called *tuyeres*). The charcoal burns at extreme heat (1200°C+). This heat cracks the tars.
4.  **Reduction Zone (Bottom):** The CO2 and H2O from combustion pass through the bed of glowing charcoal. The charcoal strips the oxygen, converting:
    *   CO2 -> **CO (Carbon Monoxide - Flammable)**
    *   H2O -> **H2 (Hydrogen - Flammable)**

## 3. The Build: The FEMA Gasifier (Emergency Style)
The "FEMA" design is simpler than the Imbert but dirtier (tar can ruin engines long-term). It is strictly for **stationary generators**, not cars.

**Components:**
1.  **The Reactor:** A metal trash can or drum containing the fire.
2.  **The Filter:** A second drum filled with wood chips or sawdust to catch ash/soot.
3.  **The Cooler:** A radiator (long pipe) to cool the gas (cooler gas = denser = more power).
4.  **The Carburetor Adapter:** A hose fed directly into the engine's air intake.

## 4. Operation
1.  **Fuel:** Use small, dry wood chips or chunks (dryness is critical).
2.  **Start:** Light the reactor. Use a blower to suck air through the system for 5-10 minutes.
3.  **Flare Test:** Light the gas coming out of the exhaust. If it sustains a blue/orange flame, it's good quality.
4.  **Run:** Connect to the engine. Adjust the air/gas mixture valve manually until the engine runs smooth.

## 5. DANGER: The Silent Killer
**Carbon Monoxide (CO)** is the primary fuel.
*   It is odorless, colorless, and deadly.
*   **NEVER** run a gasifier indoors or in a garage.
*   **Cultural Warning:** Many European drivers in WWII died not from crashes, but from leaks in the cab.
