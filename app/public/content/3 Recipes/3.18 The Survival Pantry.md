# 3.18 The Survival Pantry: Preserving Carbohydrates

Meat can be smoked. Vegetables can be dried. But what do you do with flour when you need it to last 50 years or travel 2,000 miles in a rucksack?

This module covers the "Iron Rations" of the flour world.

## 1. Hardtack (Ship's Biscuit)
The legendary "Sheet Iron." This is not food for enjoyment. It is a calorie brick designed to outlive you.
*   *Shelf Life*: 10-30+ Years (if kept dry).
*   *History*: Fueled the Roman Legions, the Royal Navy, and the Civil War armies.

### The Recipe
*   **Ratio**: 3 cups Flour : 1 cup Water : 2 tsp Salt.
*   **Method**:
    1.  Mix flour and salt. Add water slowly.
    2.  **Knead**: You must work this dough efficiently. It should be stiff, not sticky.
    3.  **Roll**: Roll fast and thin (1/2 inch).
    4.  **Dock**: Poke holes everywhere with a nail/fork. This prevents puffing. We want a brick, not a pillow.
    5.  **Cut**: 3x3 inch squares.
    6.  **Bake (The Drying)**:
        *   375°F (190°C) for 30 mins (cook via heat).
        *   Flip. 
        *   375°F for 30 mins.
        *   *Turn oven off*, crack door, leave inside for hours. We are desiccating it.
    7.  **Test**: It should be hard as rock and ring like ceramic when tapped.

### How to Eat It (Without Breaking Teeth)
**Do not bite Hardtack.**
1.  **Soak**: Soak in coffee, water, or soup for 10 minutes.
2.  **Fry**: Smash with a hammer/rifle butt. Fry the chunks in bacon grease ("Skillygalee").
3.  **Thicken**: Grind into powder to thicken stew.

## 2. Rusks (Zwieback)
"Twice-Baked." A lighter, more edible version of hardtack, but with shorter shelf life (months, not years) due to fat/yeast.
1.  Bake a normal loaf of bread.
2.  Slice it.
3.  Bake the slices at low heat (200°F) until bone dry and golden.
4.  *Use*: Dunk in coffee/tea. Popular in South Africa and Europe.

## 3. Wheat Paste (Survival Glue)
Flour is not just food. It is adhesive.
*   **Uses**: Sealing gaps in windows, crafting, makeshift binding.
*   **Recipe**:
    1.  Mix 1 part flour, 1 part sugar (optional, makes it stickier), 2 parts water.
    2.  Boil until thick and translucent.
    3.  Use immediately. Hardens like cement.

## 4. Thickening (Stretching Calories)
In survival, a thin "water soup" feels demoralizing. A thick "stew" feels like a meal, even if the calorie count is the same. The mouthfeel matters for morale.

### The Slurry (Cold Method)
*   **Technique**: Shake flour and *cold* water in a jar. Pour into boiling soup.
*   **Result**: Functional thickening. Tastes slightly raw if not cooked long enough.

### The Roux (Hot Method)
*   **Technique**: Cook Fat and Flour together in a pan (1:1 ratio) until bubbly and smelling like cookies. Whisk into liquid.
*   **Result**: Velvety texture, nutty flavor, no lumps. High calorie bump due to fat.

### Beurre Manié (Kneaded Butter)
*   **Technique**: Mash soft butter and flour together into a ball. Pinch off pieces and drop into simmering stew.
*   **Result**: Instant thickening at the end of cooking.

## 5. Storage of Grain vs. Flour
*   **Rule 1**: Store the Berry, not the Flour. Wheat berries last 30+ years. Flour lasts 6-12 months.
*   **Rule 2**: Enemies are Moisture, Oxygen, heat, and Rodents.
*   **Rule 3**: If you must store flour, seal it in Mylar with Oxygen Absorbers. Rotation is key. Eat what you store.
