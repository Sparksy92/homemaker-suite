# 3.17 The Primitive Baker: Cooking Without Convenience

In a survival situation, you have no temperature-controlled oven, no packets of instant yeast, and likely no cast iron pans. You have fire, rocks, and hunger.

This module covers the "Vast Knowledge" of turning flour into edible calories with zero infrastructure.

## 1. The Chemistry of Rising (Leavening)
Why is bread fluffy? Gas bubbles trapped in a net of gluten.

1.  **Biological (Wild Yeast / Sourdough)**:
    *   *Source*: The Air and the Grain.
    *   *Method*: Flour + Water + Time. (See Module 3.6).
    *   *Speed*: Slow (4-12 hours).
    *   *Flavor*: Sour. Best preservation qualities.
2.  **Chemical (Ash/salts)**:
    *   *Source*: Wood Ash (Potash). Crude precursor to baking soda.
    *   *Method*: Use "Pearl Ash" (refined ash water) with an acid (sour milk/vinegar) to create CO2.
    *   *Speed*: Instant.
3.  **Mechanical (Air/Steam)**:
    *   *Method*: Beating the batter violently to trap air (beaten biscuits) or using high heat to flash-expand water into steam (Popovers/unleavened flatbreads).
4.  **Unleavened**:
    *   *Method*: Just don't let it rise. Tortillas, Hardtack, Chapati. Most survival bread is unleavened.

## 2. The Universal Survival Dough
Memorize this ratio. It works for everything from Ash Cakes to Dumplings.

*   **2 Parts Flour**
*   **1 Part Water** (approx.)
*   **Pinch of Salt** (if you have it - vital for gluten structure)
*   **Fat** (Luxury item - adds tenderness/calories)

*Instructions*: Mix until it forms a ball. Knead until smooth (activates gluten). Let rest 30 mins (relaxes gluten so you can stretch it).

## 3. Primitive Cooking Methods

### Method A: Ash Cakes (No Tools)
The oldest cooking method in human history.
1.  Build a hardwood fire. Let it burn down to a thick bed of white coals.
2.  Sweep a spot clean in the coals.
3.  Form dough into a thick puck (1/2 inch).
4.  **Throw it directly onto the coals.**
5.  *Wait*. The crust will sear instantly, protecting the inside.
6.  Flip when rigid.
7.  Brush off ash before eating. (A little ash acts as an antacid).

### Method B: The Hot Rock (Griddle)
1.  Find a flat river stone (DRY ONLY - wet stones explode).
2.  Place in/near fire for hour.
3.  Grease lightly (suet/fat).
4.  Stretch dough thin. Slap onto rock.
5.  Cook until bubbly and charred. (Tortilla/Chapati style).

### Method C: Stick Bread (Twist)
1.  Find a green stick (thumb thickness, sweet wood like Maple/Birch - avoid bitter/toxic).
2.  Peel the bark off the end. Heat the stick over fire to sterilize/dry.
3.  Roll dough into a long "snake".
4.  Coil the snake around the stick. Pinch ends to secure.
5.  Rotate slowly over coals (like a marshmallow).
6.  Done when it sounds hollow when tapped.

### Method D: Boiling (Dumplings)
The most fuel-efficient method. No heat is wasted radiating into the air; it all goes into the water.
1.  If you have a stew/soup boiling:
2.  Drop spoonfuls of wet dough (or balls of stiff dough) into the liquid.
3.  Cover tightly.
4.  Steam/Boil for 10-15 minutes.
5.  *Benefit*: The dumplings absorb the fat/flavor of the stew.

### Method E: Frying (Bannock)
Requires a vessel (skillet/flat rock) and Fat. Fat is high-value energy.
1.  Melt fat in skillet.
2.  Press dough into a single large cake (the size of the pan).
3.  Fry slowly. When bottom is golden, flip.
4.  *Historical Note*: "Bannock" was the staple of voyageurs and mountain men.

## 4. Specific Recipes

### 1. Johnny Cakes (Cornmeal)
*   **Ingredients**: 2 cups Cornmeal, Boiling Water, salt.
*   **Method**: Scald the cornmeal with boiling water (this partially cooks/hydrates it). Let cool. Form patties. Fry in bacon grease.
*   **Why**: Cornmeal has no gluten. It won't hold together as a "loaf". Frying binds it.

### 2. The "Dampers" (Australian Bush Bread)
*   **Ingredients**: Flour, Water, Pinch of Ash (leavening).
*   **Method**: Mix loosely. Bury in the hot cooling ashes of the campfire for 30-40 minutes. Dust off and eat.

### 3. Famine Bread (Bark Extender)
*   **Ingredients**: 50% Wheat Flour, 50% Dried Pine Cambium Powder.
*   **Method**: Mix. Add water to make stiff dough. Cook as Ash Cake.
*   **Note**: Tastes "woody". Keeps you alive.
