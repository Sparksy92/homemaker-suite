# 12.5 Poisonous Look-alikes

> [!DANGER]
> **LETHAL IDENTIFICATION ERRORS:** Foraging is a high-stakes survival skill. Some plants contain toxins (like cicutoxin or coniine) that cause respiratory failure and cardiac arrest with no known antidote. **Never eat a plant unless you have identified it using at least three distinct physical characteristics.**

---

## 1. The Deadly Parrots: Hemlock vs. Queen Anne's Lace

This is the most dangerous "beginner" mistake in foraging.

### Queen Anne’s Lace (Wild Carrot - *Daucus carota*) - **EDIBLE**
- **Stem:** Hairy/fuzzy. "The Queen has hairy legs."
- **Smell:** Smells distinctly like a carrot when crushed.
- **Root:** A white, carrot-shaped taproot.
- **Flower:** Flat-topped white cluster, often with a **single tiny purple flower** in the dead center.
- **Bracts:** Three-forked bracts (tiny leaves) directly under the flower head.

### Poison Hemlock (*Conium maculatum*) - **LETHAL**
- **Stem:** Hairless, smooth, with **purple blotches** or spots.
- **Smell:** Foul, musty, often described as "mouse-like."
- **Flower:** Small clusters of white flowers that are more rounded/umbrella-like than the flat carrot top.
- **Toxicity:** All parts are extremely poisonous. Ingesting a small amount leads to paralysis and death.

---

## 2. Nightshade vs. Wild Berries

### Deadly Nightshade (*Atropa belladonna*) - **LETHAL**
- **Berries:** Shiny, black, solitary berries (usually not in clusters).
- **Flower:** Dull purple, bell-shaped.
- **Leaves:** Oval, matte green.
- **Warning:** Ingesting 2-5 berries can kill a child.

### Wild Blueberries (*Vaccinium species*) - **EDIBLE**
- **Berries:** Dark blue with a dusty "bloom" (the white powder that rubs off).
- **Flower:** Tiny, white, bell-shaped clusters.
- **The Crown:** Blueberries have a distinctive **five-pointed crown** (calyx) on the bottom of the berry. If it's missing, don't eat it.

---

## 3. Poinsettia & Other Common Toxins

While the Poinsettia is rarely lethal, it is often confused with edible Euphorbias.

### Poison Ivy/Oak (*Toxicodendron species*)
- **Rule:** "Leaves of three, let it be."
- **Identification:** Three leaflets, the center one has a longer stalk.
- **Reaction:** Urushiol oil causes severe blisters. Never burn it; inhaling the smoke can be fatal to the lungs.

---

## 4. The "Test for Unknowns" (Universal Edibility Test)
If you are starving and forced to eat an unknown plant, use this protocol. **This does not work for mushrooms.**

1. **Separation:** Test only one part (root, leaf, stem) at a time.
2. **Contact:** Rub the crushed plant on your inner wrist for 15 minutes. Wait 8 hours for a reaction.
3. **Cooking:** If no reaction, boil the plant part.
4. **Lip Test:** Touch a small part of the cooked plant to your outer lip for 3 minutes.
5. **Tongue Test:** Place on tongue for 15 minutes. Do not swallow.
6. **Gum Test:** Rub on gums for 3 minutes.
7. **Small Bite:** Swallow a single teaspoon. Wait 8 hours. If no illness, the plant is likely safe in small quantities.

---

## 5. Emergency Response to Poisoning

> [!IMPORTANT]
> If someone ingests a suspected toxin:
> 1. **Do not induce vomiting** unless instructed by a professional (some toxins burn the throat twice).
> 2. **Activated Charcoal:** Ingest high doses immediately to absorb toxins in the gut.
> 3. **Hydration:** If they are conscious and not vomiting, give water to help flush the system.
> 4. **Identify the plant:** Take a sample of the plant to show medical personnel.
