# 12.6 Utility & Fiber Plants

Plants provide more than just calories and medicine. In a survival scenario, they provide the materials for cordage, hygiene, and fire.

---

## 1. Cordage Plants (Natural String)

The ability to make high-strength string is a "force multiplier" in survival.

### Stinging Nettle (*Urtica dioica*)
- **Part used:** The fibrous outer skin of the stalk.
- **Process:**
    1. Harvest large, mature stalks in late summer/fall.
    2. Remove leaves (wear gloves!).
    3. Crush the stalk flat with a smooth stone.
    4. Peel away the outer fibers from the woody core.
    5. Dry the fibers, then twist into cordage using the "reverse-twist" method.

### Milkweed (*Asclepias species*)
- **Part used:** Stem fibers.
- **Process:** Similar to nettle. Harvest after the plant has dried in the field (buff-colored stalks). The fibers are long, silk-like, and incredibly strong.

---

## 2. The Soap Plants

Hygiene prevents disease. Nature provides surfactants to break down oil and dirt.

### Yucca (*Yucca species*)
- **Part used:** The root.
- **Preparation:** Dig the root, peel the outer skin, and chop into small pieces.
- **Use:** Agitate in water to create a soapy lather. Excellent for laundry and hair.

### Soapwort (*Saponaria officinalis*)
- **Part used:** Leaves and roots.
- **Preparation:** Boil the plant parts in water to extract saponins.
- **Use:** Use the resulting liquid as a gentle liquid soap.

---

## 3. Tinder & Lighting

### Mulien (*Verbascum thapsus*)
- **The "Survival Torch":** Dip a dried, tall seed stalk in melted pine resin or fat. It will burned for 15-30 minutes as a bright, mobile torch.
- **Hand Drill:** The dried, pithy stalks make excellent focal points for friction fire.

### Birch Bark (*Betula species*)
- **Fire Starter:** Contains betulinic acid (oily/flammable). Even when wet, thin strips of birch bark will ignite with a single spark.

---

## 4. Reverse-Twist Cordage Method

1. **Step 1:** Take a bundle of fibers, twist the top into a "V" shape.
2. **Step 2:** Hold the V at the apex. Twist the **top** strand **away** from you until it is tight.
3. **Step 3:** Bring that twisted strand **over** the bottom strand (toward you).
4. **Step 4:** Repeat. Twist away, bring over.
5. **Adding fibers:** To extend your rope, never add at the same spot. Stagger your additions. Simply lay new fibers into the twist as you work.

---

## 5. Ethical Harvesting of Utility Plants
Unlike food, utility plants are often used in bulk.
- **Don't over-harvest one area.** Spread your collection.
- **Harvest at the end of the season.** Once the plant has gone to seed, the stalks are often more fibrous and the plant has already completed its life cycle.
