# 12.4 Wild Harvest & Processing

Finding wild food is only half the battle. Many natural ingredients require specific processing steps to remove toxins, improve flavor, or unlock nutritional value. 

---

## 1. Acorns (Oak Trees)
**The Challenge:** High tannin content makes them bitter and toxic to kidneys if eaten raw.
**The Solution:** Leaching.

### Processing Steps:
1.  **Selection:** Only use acorns that are heavy and free of small holes (weevil larvae).
2.  **Shelling:** Crack the outer shell. Remove the thin brown skin (testa) if possible.
3.  **Grinding:** Pulse in a blender or crush between stones into a coarse meal.
4.  **Cold Water Leaching (Best for Flour):**
    *   Place meal in a fine mesh bag or jar.
    *   Submerge in cold water. Change water 2-3 times a day until the water runs clear and the meal no longer tastes bitter (takes 3-7 days).
    *   **Result:** Maintains starch binding properties for baking.
5.  **Hot Water Leaching (Faster):**
    *   Boil acorns in a pot. When water turns dark brown, dump it.
    *   Immediately pour **boiling** water over them (cold water locks in tannins).
    *   Repeat until water is clear (takes 3-5 changes).
    *   **Result:** Good for immediate snacking or "acorn coffee".

## 2. Staghorn Sumac (Rhus typhina)
**The Challenge:** Distinguishing from Poison Sumac.
**The Solution:** Look for the "fuzz."

### Identification:
*   **Staghorn Sumac (Safe):** Red, fuzzy, cone-shaped berry clusters pointing **UP**. Stems are hairy (like a stag's antlers).
*   **Poison Sumac (Toxic):** White or gray berries hanging **DOWN**. Smooth stems. Grows in swamps.

### Processing (Sumac-ade):
1.  Harvest red clusters in late summer (before heavy rain washes away the acid).
2.  Rub berries in a bowl of cold water (hot water releases bitter tannins).
3.  Strain through a coffee filter or fine cloth (to remove the tiny irritating hairs).
4.  Sweeten to taste. High in Vitamin C.

## 3. Pine & Birch Bark
**The Challenge:** Harvesting without killing the tree.
**The Solution:** Ethical harvesting.

### Ethical Harvesting (The 10% Rule):
*   Never "girdle" a tree (cutting a ring around the trunk).
*   Only harvest vertical strips from one side of the tree.
*   Ideally, harvest from downed trees or branches.

### Processing Pine Bark Flour:
1.  Peel outer scaly bark.
2.  Peel the white/green inner layer (Cambium).
3.  Dry until brittle.
4.  Grind into a fine powder. Use as a thickener or mix 20% into wheat flour.

## 4. Rose Hips (Wild Rose)
**The Challenge:** "Itchy-back" seeds.
**The Solution:** Removal or filtering.

### Processing:
1.  Harvest in late fall (after frost makes them sweet).
2.  Slice in half and scrape out the seeds and the fine hairs (which irritate the digestive tract).
3.  **Alternative:** Boil whole hips for tea, then strain through a very fine cloth (to catch the hairs).

## 5. Birch Tapping
**The Challenge:** Short season, high volume.
**The Solution:** Proper timing.

### Differences from Maple:
*   **Timing:** Birch taps later than maple (when night temps stay above freezing).
*   **Volume:** Birch produces much more sap (2-3 gallons/day) but lower sugar (1%).
*   **Storage:** Birch sap spoils quickly (2 days). Boil immediately or freeze.
*   **Syrup:** Boiler beware—Birch syrup burns much easier than maple! Finish it on a kitchen stove, not an open fire.
