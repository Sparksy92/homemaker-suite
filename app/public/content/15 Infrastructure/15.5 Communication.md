# 15.5 Communication: The Unseen Lifeline

**Concept:** If you cannot communicate, you are isolated. In an emergency, information is as valuable as potable water.

## 1. The Family Comm Plan (PACE)
Don't rely on one method. Use the PACE plan.
*   **P (Primary):** Cell Phones / Text. (Works until towers jam/fail).
*   **A (Alternate):** Internet apps (Signal/Whatsapp). (Works if wifi is up but cell towers are overloaded).
*   **C (Contingency):** Out-of-area contact. (Local lines jam first; call Aunt Susan in the next state as a central hub).
*   **E (Emergency):** Physical meetup points or Radio.

---

## 2. Low-Tech Signals
Batteries die. Physics doesn't.
*   **Whistles:** Carry a loud (100db+) safety whistle. 3 blasts = "I need help." Universal signal.
*   **Visual:** Bright cloth or signal mirror for SAR (Search and Rescue).
*   **Chalk/Marker:** Leaving signs on a specific landmark (the "dead drop") to tell family where you went.

---

## 3. Radio Basics (The Grid-Down Option)
*   **AM/FM/NOAA:** *Passive* listening. Crucial for gathering intel on weather and government alerts. Hand-crank models are standard.
*   **FRS/GMRS (Walkie Talkies):**
    *   *FRS:* No license needed. Good for short range (neighborhood/campsite).
    *   *GMRS:* Requires $35 license (no test). Higher power, capable of hitting repeaters for city-wide coverage.
*   **Ham Radio (Amateur Radio):** The gold standard. Requires study and testing. Capable of communicating globally without infrastructure. Start with a simple handheld (HT) like a Baofeng UV-5R *just to listen* until you are licensed.
