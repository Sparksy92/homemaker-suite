# 2.10 The Miller's Craft: From Field to Flour

In the modern world, flour is a white powder that comes in a paper sack. In a survival scenario, flour is a hard, durable berry that must be conquered before it can be eaten.

This module covers the physics and biology of turning "seeds" into "food."

## 1. The Anatomy of a Grain
To understand milling, you must understand the enemy. A wheat berry (and most seeds) has three parts:

1.  **The Bran (14%)**: The hard outer shell. High fiber, B vitamins. It protects the seed. It also acts as "shrapnel" in your dough, cutting gluten strands and making bread dense.
2.  **The Germ (3%)**: The embryo (the baby plant). High in fat and nutrients. **This is the problem.** Fat goes rancid when exposed to oxygen. This is why whole wheat flour spoils in months, while white flour lasts for years.
3.  **The Endosperm (83%)**: The food supply for the baby plant. Pure starch and protein (gluten). This is "White Flour."

**The Survival Trade-off**:
*   **Whole Grain**: Maximum nutrition, short shelf life (must grind daily/weekly).
*   **Sifted (Bolted)**: Lower nutrition, infinite shelf life, luxury texture.

## 2. The Mechanics of Milling
You cannot digest whole raw grains efficiently. You need to increase surface area.

### Impact vs. Attrition
*   **Impact (Pounding)**: Hitting the grain (Mortar & Pestle). Produces varying particle sizes (dust + chunks). Good for "cracking" grain for porridge. Bad for bread flour.
*   **Attrition (Burr)**: Rubbing the grain between two surfaces. Produces uniform flour. This is the goal.

### Primitive Mills
1.  **The Saddle Quern**: The Neolithic standard. A large flat rock (bed) and a smaller rounded rock (rubber). You push the rubber back and forth.
    *   *Pros*: Simple. Indestructible.
    *   *Cons*: Back-breaking labor. Stone grit in food (dental issues).
2.  **The Rotary Quern**: Two circular stones. The top one spins.
    *   *Pros*: Continuous output. Much faster.
    *   *Cons*: Heavy engineering required.
3.  **The Coffee Mill**: If you scavenge a manual burr coffee grinder, *guard it with your life*. It is the most efficient portable mill available.

## 3. The Milling Process
Do not try to turn a berry into powder in one pass. You will overheat the flour (destroying nutrients) or jam the mill.

1.  **The Crack**: Open the mill wide. Run the grain through to break it into coarse chunks (grits).
2.  **The Grind**: Tighten the mill. Run the grits through to make coarse meal.
3.  **The Finish**: Tighten fully. Run the meal through to make flour.

## 4. Wild Grains & Famine Flours
When the wheat runs out, the land provides substitutes. But they are not "plug and play."

### Acorns (The Oak Nut)
Acorns are a caloric goldmine (fat + starch), but they are full of **Tannins** (bitter, kidney-damaging astringents). You must leach them.
*   **White Oak**: Low tannin.
*   **Red Oak**: High tannin (needs more leaching), but stores longer.

**The Process (Cold Leach - Preserves Starch/Binding):**
1.  **Shell**: Smash open with a rock. Remove nutmeat.
2.  **Grind**: Pound into a coarse meal *before* leaching (increases surface area).
3.  **Soak**: Put meal in a jar of cold water or a pillowcase in a stream.
4.  **Change**: Change water every day until it is no longer bitter (3-7 days).
5.  **Dry**: Dry the mash in the sun or by a fire. Grind into flour.
    *   *Note*: Acorn flour has NO gluten. Mix 50/50 with wheat or use for flat pancakes.

### Cattails (Typha)
"The Supermarket of the Swamp." The Rhizome (underground root) is a rope of starch.
1.  **Harvest**: Dig up roots in Fall/Winter (maximum starch).
2.  **Clean**: Wash thoroughly.
3.  **Extract**:
    *   *Wet Method*: Peel, break up fibers in a bucket of water. The starch settles to the bottom (white paste). Pour off water, dry the paste.
    *   *Dry Method*: Dry the roots bone-hard. Pound the hell out of them. Sift the fibers out.

### Inner Bark (Cambium)
Famine food. Pine, Birch, Slippery Elm.
1.  **Harvest**: Cut outer bark. The Cambium is the thin, soft, white/green living layer between bark and wood.
2.  **Process**: Dry strips until brittle. Pound into powder.
3.  **Use**: Extender. Mix with other flours. High calorie, but hard to digest.

### Corn (Nixtamalization)
**CRITICAL SURVIVAL KNOWLEDGE.**
If you survive only on corn, you will die of **Pellagra** (Niacin deficiency). The nutrients in corn are chemically locked.
*   **The Solution**: Alkaline cooking.
1.  **The Ash**: Collect clean white hardwood ash.
2.  **The Boil**: Boil dried corn with a scoop of ash (or slaked lime) for 1 hour. The skins will loosen. The water will turn yellow/orange.
3.  **The Soak**: Let sit overnight.
4.  **The Wash**: Rub the kernels to remove the skins (pericarp).
5.  **The Result**: "Hominy" (Nixtamal). Now nutritious. Grind this wet to make Masa for tortillas.
