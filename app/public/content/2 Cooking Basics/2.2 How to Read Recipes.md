# 2.2 How to Read Recipes: Deciphering the Code

## The Map is Not the Territory
A recipe is a guide, not a law (except in baking). You must use your senses. If the recipe says "cook for 10 minutes until brown" but your onions are black at 8 minutes, *stop cooking*.

## Anatomy of a Recipe

### 1. The Headnote
The intro text. Read this! It often contains critical flavor notes, storage tips, or "why" we do things a certain way.

### 2. The Ingredient List
*   **The Comma Rule:**
    *   *"1 cup nuts, chopped"* = Measure 1 cup of whole nuts, *then* chop them.
    *   *"1 cup chopped nuts"* = Chop nuts until you have enough to fill 1 cup.
*   **Room Temp:** If butter or eggs are listed, assume room temperature unless "cold" or "ice" is specified. Cold eggs can break a warm batter.
*   **"Divided":** Means you will use part of the ingredient now (e.g., in the marinade) and part later (e.g., in the sauce). Don't dump it all in at once.

### 3. The Method
Read the *entire* method before you turn on the stove. You do not want to find out you need to "marinate overnight" when dinner is in 30 minutes.

### 4. Clues to Look For
*   **Visual Cues:** "Golden brown," "opaque," "coated on the back of a spoon." (Trust these over time cues).
*   **Auditory Cues:** "Sizzling," "popping," "sound dampens."
*   **Olfactory Cues:** "Fragrant," "nutty smell."

## Mise en Place (Everything in its Place)
The professional secret.
1.  Read recipe.
2.  Gather all ingredients.
3.  Prep all ingredients (measure, chop, slice) into bowls.
4.  *Then* turn on the heat.
Cooking is stress-free when you aren't trying to chop garlic while the onions are burning.

## Scaling
*   **Doubling:** Usually safe for soups, stews, and braises.
*   **Baking:** Hazardous to simple math. Doubling a cake recipe might require adjusting leaveners or baking time/pan size. Proceed with caution.
*   **Spices:** Do not strictly double spicy elements (cayenne, chili). 2x the volume does not always equal 2x the heat; sometimes it feels like 4x. Add to taste.
