# 2.3 Heat & Doneness: Mastering the Flame

## The Thermodynamics of Cooking
Cooking is the transfer of energy to food to denature proteins, gelatinize starches, and caramelize sugars.

## Heat Levels
*   **High Heat (Searing/Boiling):** For browning meat (Maillard reaction) or boiling water. Things burn quickly here. Constant attention required.
*   **Medium-High (Sautéing):** The workhorse zone. Vegetables sizzle but don't instantly char.
*   **Medium (Frying/Simmering):** Gentle bubbling. Good for cooking chicken through without burning the outside.
*   **Low (Sweating/Stewing):** Softening vegetables without browning. Keeping soups warm.
*   **Off (Carryover):** Food continues to cook after you remove it from heat.

## Carryover Cooking
Big pieces of meat hold heat.
*   A steak pulled at 135°F will rise to 140°F or 145°F on the cutting board.
*   **Rule:** Pull meat 5-10 degrees *before* your target temperature. Resting is mandatory to redistribute juices.

## Indicators of Doneness

### Meat (Touch Test)
*   **Raw:** Squishy, like the fleshy part of your palm/thumb muscle when hand is relaxed.
*   **Rare:** Like thumb to index finger.
*   **Medium:** Like thumb to middle finger.
*   **Well:** Like thumb to pinky (firm).
*   *Better:* Use a thermometer. (See 0.5 Food Safety).

### Vegetables
*   **Blanched:** Bright color, still crunchy. (Boiled 1-2 mins).
*   **Al Dente:** "To the tooth." Tender but with a slight bite in the center. (Pasta/Veg).
*   **Tender:** Knife slides in with zero resistance. (Potatoes/Carrots).
*   **Caramelized:** Natural sugars have turned brown and sweet. Soft and jammy. (Onions).

### Baked Goods
*   **Cake:** Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean (no wet batter).
*   **Bread:** Sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom. Internal temp 190°F-200°F.

## Pan Management
*   **Don't Crowding:** If you put too much cold food in a pan, the temperature drops, and the food *steams* instead of searing. You get gray, soggy meat. Cook in batches if needed.
*   **Preheat:** Always put food into a hot pan (usually with oil). "Cold oil in hot pan, food happens."
