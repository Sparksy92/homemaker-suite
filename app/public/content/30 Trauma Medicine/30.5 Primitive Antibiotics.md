# 30.5 Primitive Antibiotics: The Last Resort

**WARNING:** This is dangerous. Inaccurately cultured mold can be toxic. Usage of this knowledge is for scenarios where professional medical care has ceased to exist entirely.

## 1. Cultural History: The Old Ways
Long before Fleming "discovered" penicillin in 1928, cultures used antibiotic molds.
*   **Ancient Egypt:** Imhotep (2600 BC) prescribed moldy bread for infected wounds.
*   **Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM):** Used fermented soybean curds to treat skin infections.
*   **Serbia/Greece:** Used moldy cheese/bread packed into wounds.
*   **Lesson:** They didn't know *why* it worked (Penicillium fungi fighting bacteria), but they knew *that* it worked.

## 2. Penicillin (The Blue Mold)
You cannot just scrape mold off a sandwich (that might be black mold = death). You must culture it.

**Identification:**
*   *Penicillium* is the **blue-green** mold that grows on citrus fruits (oranges/lemons) and bread.
*   It looks like white lace at the edges, turning blue-green in the center.

**The "Surface Culture" Method (Orville May, 1940s):**
1.  **The Host:** Moldy bread/citrus is just the starter. You need a liquid medium to grow enough medicine.
2.  **The Broth:**
    *   Water (boiled/sterilized)
    *   Sugar (lactose is best, or cane sugar)
    *   Corn Steep Liquor (or potato starch water) - provides Nitrogen.
3.  **Inoculation:** Scrape the blue spores from your starter fruit into the sterilized broth bottle.
4.  **Fermentation:** Keep at 75°F (24°C) for 7-10 days.
5.  **Harvest:** The liquid (broth) now contains the Penicillin excreted by the mold. The mold mat on top is waste.
6.  **Usage:** The yellow liquid ("mold juice") was used topically on wounds or soaked into dressings. Injecting home-brew broth is **lethal** due to debris/proteins (anaphylaxis). **Topical use only.**

## 3. Sulfa Drugs (The Synthetic Route)
If you have a chemistry lab but no biology lab.
*   **Prontosil (1935):** The first synthetic antibacterial. Derived from coal tar dyes.
*   **Difficulty:** Requires Sulfuric Acid, Aniline, and Ammonia. Extremely difficult to scavenge, but chemistry textbooks (pre-1950) contain the synthesis pathways.

## 4. The "Wild" Antibiotics (Botanical)
Easier to source, safer to use, but less potent than mold.
*   **Garlic (Allicin):**
    *   *History:* Roman legionaries ate it daily for health.
    *   *Usage:* Must be crushed to activate. Powerful against Staph.
*   **Usnea (Old Man's Beard):**
    *   *History:* Indigenous North American medicine.
    *   *ID:* Hair-like lichen hanging from trees. Stretching it reveals a white elastic core (the "test").
    *   *Usage:* Tincture in alcohol. Contains Usnic Acid (gram-positive antibiotic).
*   **Honey:**
    *   *History:* Mentioned in the Bible and Quran as healing.
    *   *Usage:* Pack deep wounds with raw honey. It is hygroscopic (sucks water out of bacteria) and produces low-level peroxide.

**The Reality Check:**
Scavenging leftover veterinary antibiotics (Fish Mox, animal feed stores) is safer than growing mold. But when those run out, the blue mold on the orange is your only friend.
