# 30.3 Field Suturing: Techniques

**Warning**: Suturing is invasive. If you seal in infection, you kill the patient.
**Rule**: If the wound is >6 hours old, **DO NOT SUTURE**. Pack it with wet-to-dry gauze and let it heal by secondary intention (bottom-up).

## Equipment
*   **Driver**: Needle holder (looks like locking pliers).
*   **Pickups**: Forceps (tweezers) with teeth to hold skin.
*   **Suture**:
    *   *Nylon (Ethilon)*: Non-absorbable. Strong. Best for Skin.
    *   *Vicryl/Gut*: Absorbable. Weak. Best for internal/mouth.

## 1. The Simple Interrupted Stitch
The "bread and butter" stitch. If one breaks, the others hold.
1.  **Bite**: Enter skin at 90° (perpendicular), 5mm from the wound edge.
2.  **Turn**: Rotate wrist to drive needle through the arc.
3.  **Exit**: Come out inside the wound, then re-enter the other side (inside-out) to match the depth.
4.  **Knot**: Square knotes only. Surgeons Knot (Double throw first, then single throws).
    *   *Tie it loosely!* Swelling will occur. If tight, it kills the tissue (necrosis).

## 2. Vertical Mattress Stitch
Use for skin that wants to curl inward (inversion). This stitch forces edges up and out (eversion), which heals best.
*   **Far-Far**: Enter 8mm back, exit 8mm back on other side.
*   **Near-Near**: Reverse needle. Enter 2mm from edge, exit 2mm from edge on original side.
*   **Tie**: Tie the knot on the original side.

## 3. Corner Stitch
For triangular flaps.
*   Do NOT put a needle through the tip of the flap (it will die).
*   Pass needle through the distinct skin, catch the *subdermal* layer of the flap tip underneath, and exit the other distinct skin side.

## 4. Removal Timing
*   **Face**: 4-5 Days (Scarring risk).
*   **Scalp**: 7 Days.
*   **Torso/Limbs**: 10-14 Days.
*   **Joints**: 14-21 Days.
