# 18.2 Solar & Passive Energy

**Reality Check:** Full solar panel systems are expensive and require technical knowledge. This section covers **low-tech solar applications** anyone can build with minimal investment—solar cookers, dehydrators, passive heating, and simple water heating.

**No batteries, no inverters, no electrician needed.**

---

## Solar Cooker (Cardboard Box Method)

**Concept:** Sunlight heats a black pot inside an insulated, reflective box. Temps reach 250-350°F—enough to boil, bake, and slow-cook.

### Materials
- 2 cardboard boxes (one 3-4" smaller than the other on all sides)
- Aluminum foil or mylar
- Black paint (or black pot)
- Glass or clear plastic sheet
- Newspaper or straw (insulation)
- Gl

ue or tape

### Construction

#### Step 1: Create the Insulated Chamber
1. Place smaller box inside larger box
2. Fill the gap between boxes with crumpled newspaper or straw (3-4" of insulation on all sides)
3. This creates an insulated oven cavity

#### Step 2: Line with Reflective Material
1. Cover inner walls of the small box with aluminum foil (shiny side in)
2. Smooth it as flat as possible (wrinkles reduce efficiency)
3. **Purpose:** Reflects sunlight onto the cooking pot from all angles

#### Step 3: Create Reflective Panels (Oven Door)
1. Cut flaps on the top edges of the larger box
2. Fold outward, cover with aluminum foil
3. These act as "wings" that funnel extra sunlight into the box

#### Step 4: Add the Glass Top
1. Cover the opening with glass or clear plastic
2. Seal edges with tape
3. **Purpose:** Greenhouse effect (light enters, heat can't escape)

#### Step 5: Paint Your Pot Black
- Use a black metal pot (absorbs maximum heat)
- If pot isn't black, paint it with non-toxic black paint

### How to Use
1. Place food in black pot, cover with lid
2. Set pot inside the solar cooker
3. Angle the cooker toward the sun
4. Adjust reflector flaps to maximize sunlight entry
5. Let cook (2-4 hours for most dishes—slower than a conventional oven)

**Best for:** Stews, rice, beans, baking (anything that benefits from slow, even cooking).

**Temperature:** 250-300°F on a sunny day (lower in winter or cloudy days).

## Solar Dehydrator (Food Preservation)

**Purpose:** Dry fruits, vegetables, herbs, and meat without electricity.

### Design: Box-Style Dehydrator

#### Materials
- Wooden frame or cardboard box
- Screen or mesh (food-grade)
- Clear plastic or glass (top panel)
- Black-painted metal sheet (heat absorber)
- Vents (top and bottom)

#### Construction

**Step 1: Build the Frame**
- Construct a box (2' x 3' x 1' tall is a good size)
- Wooden frame covered with screens, or use a cardboard box with screen shelves

**Step 2: Create Airflow**
1. Cut vents at the bottom (cool air enters)
2. Cut vents at the top (hot, moist air exits)
3. This creates a natural convection flow

**Step 3: Install Drying Racks**
- Stretch food-safe mesh or screen across the interior (multiple layers if possible)
- Space racks 3-4" apart

**Step 4: Add Heat Absorber**
- Place black-painted metal sheet at the bottom (absorbs sunlight, heats the air)

**Step 5: Cover with Transparent Top**
- Glass or clear plastic on top (lets sun in, traps heat)

### How to Use
1. Slice fruits/vegetables thin (1/4")
2. Arrange on screens (don't overlap)
3. Place dehydrator in full sun
4. Dry for 1-3 days (depending on food and weather)
5. **Test:** Food should be leathery, no moisture when squeezed

**Temperature inside:** 110-140°F (ideal for dehydrating).

**Best for:** Tomatoes, apples, herbs, jerky.

## Passive Solar Heating (House Warmth)

**Concept:** Use sunlight to heat your home during the day, store heat for nighttime.

### Strategy 1: Thermal Mass (Heat Battery)

**What is thermal mass?** Heavy, dense materials that absorb heat slowly and release it slowly.

**Examples:**
- **Water barrels:** 55-gallon drums painted black
- **Stone or brick wall:** Interior wall that gets direct sun
- **Concrete floors** Dark-colored, exposed to sunlight

**How to use:**
1. Place thermal mass in a sunny spot (south-facing window in Northern Hemisphere)
2. Sunlight heats the mass during the day
3. At night, the mass radiates warmth into the room

**Effectiveness:**
- One 55-gallon drum of water can release heat for 8-12 hours
- 10 drums = significant heating capacity

---

### Strategy 2: Trombe Wall (Advanced)

**A passive solar heating wall.**

#### How It Works
- Thick masonry wall on the south side of the building
- Painted black (absorbs maximum sunlight)
- Glass panel 2-6" in front of the wall
- Vents at top and bottom

**Operation:**
1. Sun heats the black wall through the glass
2. Air in the gap between glass and wall heats up
3. Hot air rises, exits through top vents into the room
4. Cool air is drawn in through bottom vents (convection loop)
5. The wall itself stores heat, radiates it inward at night

**DIY version:**
- Stack dark bricks against a south-facing window
- Seal window with plastic (creates the air gap)
- Cut small vents at top and bottom

**Effect:** Can provide 30-50% of heating needs in a well-insulated space.

---

### Strategy 3: Window Insulation (See Section 17.2)

**Review:** Insulate windows at night, open during day to capture solar heat.

## Solar Water Heating (Warm Showers)

### Batch Heater (Simplest Method)

**Materials:**
- Black container (5-gallon bucket, old radiator, coiled black hose)
- Insulated box or platform
- Clear plastic or glass cover

**Construction:**
1. Paint container black (if not already black)
2. Fill with water
3. Place in insulated box with glass/plastic top
4. Set in full sun

**Result:** Water reaches 100-140°F in 3-6 hours (enough for a warm shower or washing dishes).

**Upgrade:** Add a spigot at the bottom for easy dispensing.

---

### Active Solar Water Heater (More Complex)

**Concept:** Water circulates through a solar collector, heats up, returns to storage tank.

**Materials:**
- Black-painted metal tubes or radiator (collector)
- Insulated storage tank
- Hoses

**How It Works:**
1. Cold water flows from tank to collector
2. Sun heats water in the collector
3. Hot water rises back to tank (thermosiphon effect—no pump needed)

**This requires plumbing skills—start with the batch heater if you're new to DIY.**

## Sun Oven (Alternative to Box Cooker)

**Parabolic reflector:** Curved mirror focuses sunlight onto a single point (the cooking pot).

**Materials:**
- Large cardboard or poster board
- Aluminum foil
- Wire frame

**Shape:** Cut a parabolic curve, cover with foil, mount pot at the focal point.

**Advantage:** Reaches higher temps (350-400°F).

**Disadvantage:** Must constantly adjust to follow the sun (labor-intensive).

## Battery Maintenance (Without Electricity)

**If you have car batteries or deep-cycle batteries, preserve them without a charger:**

### Solar Trickle Charger (Small Panel)
- 5-10 watt solar panel (portable, inexpensive)
- Connects directly to battery terminals
- Keeps battery topped off (prevents sulfation and death)

**Cost:** $20-40 for a small panel.

**Payoff:** Extends battery life indefinitely.

## Weather Limitations

**Solar equipment only works in direct sunlight.**

- **Cloudy days:** Minimal performance (20-30% efficiency)
- **Winter:** Lower sun angle = less heat (still works, just slower)
- **Shade:** Useless (must have clear view of the sun)

**Solution:** Have backup methods (fire cooking, wood stove, etc.).

## Solar Dehydrator Performance

**Ideal conditions:**
- **Temp:** 90-100°F ambient, 110-140°F inside dehydrator
- **Humidity:** Low (dry air speeds drying)
- **Sun:** Full, unobstructed

**Drying times:**
- **Herbs:** 6-12 hours
- **Tomato slices:** 1-2 days
- **Apple slices:** 1-2 days
- **Jerky:** 1-3 days (depending on thickness)

## DIY Solar Panel (For Small Devices)

**This is advanced—requires electrical knowledge.**

**Materials:**
- Small solar cells (3V, 5V—available online)
- Solder and soldering iron
- Wire
- Plexiglass or glass (protection)

**What you can power:**
- LED lights (very low power)
- Charge AA/AAA batteries
- Small radios

**This won't power a refrigerator, but it can keep communication devices alive.**

## The Cost-Benefit Analysis

**Solar box cooker:**
- **Cost:** $20-30 (DIY), $100-200 (commercial)
- **Payoff:** Free cooking energy for 5-10+ years

**Solar dehydrator:**
- **Cost:** $30-50 (DIY), $150-300 (commercial)
- **Payoff:** Preserve 50-100 lbs of food per season (prevents waste)

**Thermal mass water heater:**
- **Cost:** $50-100 (batch heater)
- **Payoff:** Hot showers without fuel costs

**All three projects: ~$100 total if you build yourself.**
