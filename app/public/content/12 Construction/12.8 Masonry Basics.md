# 12.8 Masonry Basics (Stone & Brick)

Permanent structures require stone or brick. Unlike wood, masonry does not rot, burn, or decay easily.

## 1. The Mortar Mix
Modern cement is too hard for old brick/stone. For longevity and repairability, use **Lime Mortar**.

### Recipe: NHL (Natural Hydraulic Lime)
*   **Mix**: 1 part NHL (3.5 or 5) : 2.5 parts Sharp Sand.
*   **Water**: Just enough to make it workable (peanut butter consistency).

### Recipe: Portland Cement (Modern Strength)
*   **Mix**: 1 part Portland Cement : 4 parts Sand.
*   **Note**: Very strong but brittle. Can crack soft stones.

## 2. Stone Foundations
A dry-stone foundation (no mortar) relies on gravity and friction.
1.  **Trench**: Dig below the frost line (usually 3-4 feet).
2.  **Footing**: Large, flat stones at the bottom.
3.  **Battering**: Slope the wall inward slightly as it goes up (1" per foot).
4.  **Through Stones**: Every 2-3 feet horizontally, place a long stone that reaches through the entire width of the wall to tie it together.

## 3. Bricklaying Patterns (Bonds)
*   **Running Bond**: Standard offset. Weakest for walls.
*   **English Bond**: Alternating courses of headers (end visible) and stretchers (side visible). Very strong.
*   **Flemish Bond**: Alternating header/stretcher in the SAME row.

## 4. Making Bricks (Survival)
1.  **Clay Selection**: Dig until you find sticky, plastic soil. Test by making a "snake" and bending it into a ring without cracking.
2.  **Tempering**: Mix with 20% sand to reduce shrinkage.
3.  **Molding**: Press into wooden molds dusted with sand.
4.  **Drying**: Air dry for 2-4 weeks (crucial!).
5.  **Firing**: Must verify 1000°C (Orange heat) for 24 hours in a clamp kiln or pit fire.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Slaking Lime**: If you burn limestone (calcium carbonate) to make quicklime, you MUST hydration it (slake it) carefully. It releases massive heat and can boil water/cause burns.
