# Building a Root Cellar

A root cellar uses the earth's stable temperature (approx 50-55°F) to prevent produce from freezing in winter and spoiling in summer.

## Design Principles
1.  **Earth Sheltering**: You need at least 1-2 feet of dirt overhead, or deep excavation, to reach stable temperatures.
2.  **Ventilation**: Critically important. You need an **Inlet** (low, cool air in) and an **Outlet** (high, ethylne gas/warm air out).
3.  **Humidity**: Must be high (90-95%) for root crops. A dirt or gravel floor is best. Concrete floors need standing water added.

## Build Types

### 1. The Trash Can Cellar (Easiest)
*   **Dig**: A hole slightly larger than a metal trash can.
*   **Insert**: Place can in hole. Rim should be 4 inches above ground.
*   **Insulate**: Pack straw around the can. Place lid on. Place hay bale on top of lid.
*   **Capacity**: Good for 1-2 families' worth of potatoes/carrots.

### 2. The Hillside Excavation (Intermediate)
*   **Dig**: Dig *into* a North-facing hill.
*   **Frame**: Build timber retaining walls and roof.
*   **Cover**: Backfill dirt over the roof.
*   **Door**: Must be insulated. Double-door airlocks are best.

### 3. Basement Corner (Advanced Retrofit)
*   **Location**: North-east corner of basement (coolest).
*   **Insulate**: Build stud walls insulated with rigid foam (not fiberglass - it molds).
*   **Vents**: Run PVC pipes out the rim joist window. One pipe goes to floor level (intake), one at ceiling level (exhaust).

## Shelf Construction
*   Use rot-resistant wood (Cedar, Locust) or plastic.
*   Slatted shelves allow airflow around crates.
