# Building a Log Cabin from Scratch

Building a log cabin is the quintessential homesteading project. It represents shelter, security, and a deep connection to the forest.

## 1. Site Selection
Before you cut a single tree, you must choose the right spot.
*   **Drainage**: Choose high ground. Water must flow away from your foundation.
*   **Sun Exposure**: South-facing orientation maximizes solar heat gain in winter.
*   **Resources**: proximity to water, timber, and stone.

## 2. Felling and Preparing Logs
*   **Selection**: Straight trees with minimal taper (Pine, Spruce, Cedar). 8-10 inches diameter.
*   **Felling**: Winter felling is best (sap is down).
*   **Peeling**: Remove bark immediately to prevent rot and insects. Use a drawknife.
*   **Seasoning**: Let logs dry for at least 6 months to reduce shrinkage, or build green and account for settling.

## 3. Foundation
A solid foundation keeps your logs off the ground.
*   **Stone Pillars**: Traditional and effective. Place large flat stones at corners and every 6-8 feet.
*   **Gravel Trench**: Dig below frost line, fill with gravel for drainage.

## 4. The Notch
The notch locks the corners together.
*   **Saddle Notch**: Simplest for round logs. A semi-circular cut on the bottom of the upper log to fit the log below.
*   **Dovetail**: Stronger, shedding water away from the joint, but requires more skill.

## 5. Raising the Walls
1.  **Sill Logs**: The first two logs are the most critical. Ensure they are perfectly level and square.
2.  **Floor Joists**: Notch these into your sill logs.
3.  **Stacking**: Alternate logs, scribing and notching as you go up. Alternate tip-to-butt to keep the wall level.

## 6. Roofing
*   **Gable Ends**: Log gables or framed gables.
*   **Ridge Pole**: The central beam supporting the roof.
*   **Materials**: Metal (durable), Shakes (traditional), or Sod (insulating).

## 7. Chinking
Fills the gaps between logs.
*   **Traditional**: Moss and clay/mud.
*   **Modern**: Synthetic chinking over backer rod. Flexible to move with the wood.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A log cabin settles! Leave 3-4 inches of space above windows and doors (settling space) packed with insulation, or your glass will shatter as the logs shrink.
