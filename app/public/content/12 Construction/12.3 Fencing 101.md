# Fencing 101: Good Fences Make Good Homesteads

Fencing has two jobs: Keep your animals **IN**, and keep predators **OUT**.

## Types of Fencing

### 1. Woven Wire ("Field Fence")
*   **Best For**: Goats, Sheep, Pigs, Dogs.
*   **Structure**: A grid of wires knotted together.
*   **Installation**:
    *   **Corner Posts**: Must be massive (6-8 inch diameter) and braced (H-Brace). They take all the tension.
    *   **Tension**: Wire must be stretched with a vehicle or come-along until "tight as a guitar string."

### 2. Barbed Wire
*   **Best For**: Cattle only.
*   **Pros**: Cheap, easy to repair.
*   **Cons**: Injures horses/goats. Doesn't stop predators.

### 3. Electric
*   **Best For**: Rotational grazing, psychological barrier.
*   **Structure**: High tensile wire or poly-tape carrying a 10,000v pulse.
*   **Grounding**: The most important part. You need 3 ground rods spaced 10ft apart. If the animal touches the wire and the ground is dry/poor, no shock happens.

### 4. Poultry Netting (Electric)
*   **Best For**: Chickens.
*   **Pros**: Movable. Move the flock to fresh grass every week.

## Post Setting
*   **Wood**: Cedar/Locust last 20+ years. Pine rots in 5.
*   **T-Posts**: Steel posts for line posts. Easy to drive in.
*   **Depth**: Corner posts need to be 3-4 feet deep (below frost line).

> [!TIP]
> "Water gap": If crossing a creek, don't stretch fence across it. Hang a separate "flap" of fence that can swing up during floods, or the debris will tear your whole fence line down.
