# Primitive Water Filtration: Rock, Sand, Charcoal

Modern filters are great, but what if you don't have one? Nature provides the materials.

## The Theory
We need to remove particles in stages, from big to microscopic.
1.  **Gravel**: Catches leaves and bugs.
2.  **Sand**: Catches dirt and silt.
3.  **Charcoal**: Removes chemicals, odors, and some bacteria (adsorption).

## The Build (Tripod or Bottle)
You need a container. A plastic bottle with the bottom cut off, a birch bark cone, or a pant leg tied at the bottom.

### Layering (Top to Bottom)
Pack these layers tight. Water flows down through them.
1.  **Top Layer (3 inches)**: **Grass/Gravel**. This prevents the sand from floating up when you pour water in.
2.  **Middle Layer (3 inches)**: **Sand**. The finer the better.
3.  **Bottom Layer (3 inches)**: **Crushed Charcoal**. Cold fire coals crushed into powder/chunks. This is the most important layer.
4.  **Outlet**: Poke a small hole in the cap/bottom. You want a **slow drip**, not a gush. Slow water = Clean water.

## IMPORTANT WARNING
 This filter makes water **Clear**, but not necessarily **Sterile**.
*   It removes mud, algae, and bad tastes.
*   It does **NOT** kill viruses.
*   **ALWAYS BOIL** the water after it comes out of the filter for 1 minute.
