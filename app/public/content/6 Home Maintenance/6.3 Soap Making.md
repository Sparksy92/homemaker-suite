# Soap Making: Chemistry in the Kitchen

True soap is the result of a chemical reaction called **Saponification**: Fat + Lye + Water = Soap + Glycerin.

> [!WARNING]
> **Lye (Sodium Hydroxide) is caustic.** It causes chemical burns. Always wear gloves and eye protection. Vinegar neutralizes lye.

## The Equipment
*   Stainless steel or enamel pot (NO aluminum - it reacts with lye).
*   Stick blender (immersion blender).
*   Digital scale (precision is mandatory).
*   Silicon molds.

## The Basic Recipe (Bastille Soap)
A gentle, mostly olive oil bar.
*   **Oils**:
    *   Olive Oil: 500g
    *   Coconut Oil: 150g (for hardness/lather)
    *   Castor Oil: 30g (for bubbles)
*   **Lye Solution**:
    *   Sodium Hydroxide (Lye): 95g
    *   Distilled Water: 200g

## The Process (Cold Process)
1.  **Lye Water**: Pour Lye *into* Water (never water into lye - eruptions happen). Stir in a well-ventilated area. Let cool to ~100°F.
2.  **Melt Oils**: Melt solid oils, add liquid oils. Cool to ~100°F.
3.  **Emulsify**: Pour lye water into oils. Use stick blender in short bursts until "Trace" is achieved (mixture looks like thin pudding).
4.  **Pour**: Pour into mold.
5.  **Saponify**: Cover with a towel. Let sit 24-48 hours. It will heat up (gel phase).
6.  **Cut & Cure**: Unmold and cut into bars. Let sit on a rack for 4-6 weeks to harden and let water evaporate.

## Superfatting
We usually calculate recipes to have 5% "extra" oil that doesn't react with the lye. This ensures there is no free lye left in the bar and makes the soap moisturizing.
