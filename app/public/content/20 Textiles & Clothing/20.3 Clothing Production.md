# 20.3 Clothing Production: The Anti-Tailor

**Concept:** You don't need a degree in fashion design. You need to cover your body. Prioritize **function** over fit, and **simplicity** over style.

## 1. Pattern-Free Sewing
Historically, cloth was too valuable to cut into complex curves. "Zero Waste" patterns use rectangles and triangles.

### The T-Tunic (The Universal Shirt)
Just one big T-shape.
1.  **Body:** A rectangle of fabric (Shoulder to knees long, shoulder-width + 4 inches wide).
2.  **Neck:** Cut a slit in the center for your head.
3.  **Sleeves:** Two rectangles sewn to the sides.
4.  **Sew:** Stitch up the sides and under the arms.
5.  **Belt it:** The belt gives it shape. Without a belt, it's a sack. With a belt, it's a tunic.

### Drawstring Pants (The Universal Trousers)
1.  **Legs:** Two long rectangles (waist to ankle).
2.  **Crotch:** Just sew the legs together halfway up? No. You need a **Gusset**.
3.  **The Gusset:** A diamond-shaped piece of fabric sewn between the legs. It adds room to walk/sit without ripping the seam.
4.  **Waist:** Fold the top over 2 inches, sew a channel, run a rope through it.

## 2. Fabric Sources (Scavenging)
When the fabric store closes, where do you get cloth?

*   **Bed Sheets:** High quality, large yardage. 1 King sheet = 2 tunics.
*   **Curtains:** often heavy, durable material (canvas/velvet). Good for winter coats.
*   **Blankets:** Wool blankets = instant winter cloaks or coats.
*   **Canvas Drop Cloths:** Painters' drop cloths are cheap, heavy cotton. Perfect for work pants/aprons.

---

## 3. Fasteners (No Zippers)
Zippers fail. Velcro clogs with mud. Go primitive.

*   **Toggles:** A stick or piece of antler toggled through a loop of rope. Indestructible.
*   **Drawstrings:** The ultimate adjustable waistband.
*   **Wrap & Belt:** Like a bathrobe or kimono. No fasteners needed, just a belt.

---

## 4. Footwear (The Hardest Part)
Making shoes that don't cripple you is difficult.

*   **The Moccasin:** Simple leather bag for your foot.
    *   *Trace your foot* on leather. Add 2 inches all around.
    *   *Punch holes* around the edge.
    *   *Thread a drawstring* through the holes and pull tight.
*   **Tire Sandals (Huaraches):**
    *   *Sole:* Cut the tread off an old car tire.
    *   *Straps:* Use nylon webbing or leather.
    *   *Pros:* Last for 10,000 miles.

---

## 5. The Sewing Awl (Speed Stitcher)
*   If you are sewing canvas or leather, a standard needle will break.
*   **Get a Sewing Awl.** It punches a hole and feeds a lockstitch in one motion.
*   **Usage:** Tents, tarps, shoes, packs, heavy coats.

## 6. Durability Hacks
*   **Flat Felled Seams:** Interlock the fabric layers before sewing (like the seam on your jeans). 3x stronger than a normal seam.
*   **Double Knees/Elbows:** Sew a second layer of fabric on before you construct the garment.
