# 20.1 Clothing Repair

**The Grid-Down Reality:** That rip in your jeans? You can't replace them. That button? Can't buy a new shirt. **Your clothes must last years, not months.**

**This section covers field repairs that extend clothing life indefinitely.**

---

## The Essential Sewing Kit

**Minimum supplies to repair anything:**

1. **Needles** (assorted sizes—thick for canvas, thin for fabric)
2. **Thread** (strong cotton or polyester—buy bulk spools)
3. **Scissors** (sharp, dedicated to fabric only)
4. **Thimble** (protects your finger when pushing needle through thick fabric)
5. **Buttons** (assorted sizes—salvage from old clothes)
6. **Safety pins** (temporary fixes, hold patches in place)

**Optional but useful:**
- Patches of denim, canvas, and cotton fabric
- Leather scraps (for heavy-duty repairs)
- Waxed thread or dental floss (for extreme durability)

**Total cost:** < $20 for a kit that repairs 100+ garments.

---

## Hand Stitching Fundamentals

### The Running Stitch (Fastest)

**Use for:** Hems, seams (low-stress areas).

**How:**
1. Knot the end of thread
2. Push needle in and out in a straight line
3. Small, even stitches (1/4" apart)

**Strength:** Moderate.

---

### The Backstitch (Strongest)

**Use for:** Seams, high-stress areas (crotch, underarms).

**How:**
1. Take one stitch forward
2. Bring needle back to the start of previous stitch (creates overlap)
3. Repeat

**Result:** As strong as a sewing machine stitch.

---

### The Whipstitch (Edge Finishing)

**Use for:** Attaching patches, binding edges.

**How:**
1. Bring needle from back to front, wrapping over the edge
2. Repeat in a spiral pattern

---

## Patching Holes

### Small Holes (<1 inch)

**Option 1: Simple Patch (Inside)**
1. Cut a patch 1" larger than the hole on all sides
2. Place patch on the inside of the garment
3. Pin in place
4. Whipstitch or running stitch around the edges of the hole

**Option 2: Darning (Weaving)**
1. Thread needle, don't knot the end
2. Weave thread back and forth across the hole (horizontal rows)
3. Then weave perpendicular (vertical), going over-under the horizontal threads
4. Creates a woven "patch" of thread

**Best for:** Socks, elbows, knees (areas that flex).

---

### Large Holes or Tears (>1 inch)

**Reinforced Patch:**
1. Cut patch 2" larger than the tear
2. Fold raw edges under (prevents fraying)
3. Pin patch over the hole (outside of garment for visibility)
4. Backstitch around all edges
5. Optional: Add decorative stitching (makes it a "feature," not a flaw)

**For jeans/canvas:** Use denim or heavy canvas for patches.

---

## Reinforcing High-Stress Areas

**Prevent holes before they happen.**

### Crotch Reinforcement
1. Before pants rip, sew a patch on the INSIDE of the crotch seam
2. Use denim or canvas
3. This doubles fabric thickness, extends life by years

### Elbow/Knee Patches
**Classic look, practical function:**
1. Cut oval patches (canvas, leather, or contrast fabric)
2. Sew on OUTSIDE of elbows/knees (decorative + functional)
3. Backstitch all around edges

---

## Button Replacement

### Sewing on a Flat Button (Shirt/Pants)
1. Thread needle, double the thread, knot the ends together
2. Push needle from inside of fabric (knot will be hidden)
3. Thread through buttonhole, back down through opposite hole
4. Repeat 4-6 times (creates strong attachment)
5. **Critical:** Leave slight slack (button needs to move)
6. Wrap thread around the threads beneath the button (creates a "shank")
7. Push needle to back, knot off

---

### Shank Button (Coat/Heavy Fabric)
- These have a loop on the back instead of holes
- Sew through the loop, same process as above

---

## Zipper Repair (Temporary Fix)

**Problem:** Zipper won't stay closed.

**Fix #1: Pliers Adjustment**
- Squeeze the zipper pull tighter with pliers
- This narrows the gap, grips teeth better

**Fix #2: Safety Pin**
- If zipper still won't stay up, safety pin it shut from the inside

**Permanent fix:** Replace zipper (advanced skill, easier to button-convert).

---

## Elastic Replacement (Waistbands)

**When elastic dies (stretches out), replace it.**

### Process
1. Find the opening in the waistband (small gap in the stitching)
2.If none, cut a small slit on the inside
3. Attach safety pin to new elastic, thread through the waistband
4. **Don't lose the other end!** (pin it outside the waistband)
5. Once threaded all the way through, overlap ends of elastic by 1"
6. Sew together with backstitch
7. Close the opening in the waistband

---

## Hemming (Shortening Pants/Sleeves)

1. Try on garment, mark desired length with pins or chalk
2. Add 1" below the mark (seam allowance)
3. Cut off excess fabric
4. Fold raw edge under 1/2", iron or finger-press
5. Fold again (creates a finished edge)
6. Pin in place
7. Running stitch or backstitch all around

---

## Emergency Field Repairs (No Needle/Thread)

### Safety Pins
- Pinned seams hold surprisingly well
- Use multiple pins for strength

### Duct Tape (Inside Patch)
- Apply to inside of fabric
- Lasts days to weeks (temporary)

### Staples (Heavy Fabric Only)
- Staple a patch or seam together
- Use on canvas, denim (not delicate fabrics)

---

## Salvaging Fabric from Unusable Garments

**Don't throw away worn-out clothes—harvest them for parts.**

### What to Save
- **Zippers** (expensive to buy, free to salvage)
- **Buttons** (store in jars, sorted by size)
- **Fabric** (cut into patches, squares for quilts, rags)
- **Elastic** (from waistbands, if still good)
- **Drawstrings** (reuse in gym shorts, bags)

**One worn-out shirt = patches for 5 other shirts.**

---

## Thread Conservation

**In long-term survival, thread is finite.**

### Make It Last
- Use only as much as needed (no 3-foot lengths for a 2" seam)
- Salvage thread from unusable clothes (unpick seams, wind onto spools)
- **Strongest homemade thread:** Dental floss (waxed is best)

---

## The Visible Mending Movement

**Don't hide repairs—celebrate them.**

**Techniques:**
- **Sashiko (Japanese):** Decorative running stitches in patterns
- **Contrast patches:** Red patch on blue jeans, denim on khakis
- **Embroidery over darns:** Turn a repair into artwork

**Why:** Repairs become a source of pride, not shame. Your clothes tell a story.

---

## Leather Repair

**For boots, belts, jackets:**

### Small Tears
- Stitch with waxed thread or dental floss
- Use a leather needle (triangular point—punches through easier)

### Large Tears
- Patch with scrap leather
- Pre-punch holes with an awl or nail
- Lace through with strong cordage or leather strips

---

## Winter Clothing Hacks

### Layering Worn Wool
- Place two thin, holey wool sweaters together (holes don't align)
- Wear as one garment (double thickness = warm)

### Stuffing for Insulation
- Stuff jacket lining with cattail fluff, milkweed down, or dry leaves
- Creates instant insulation (remove for washing)

---

## The Bottom Line

**Clothing is a consumable resource in survival.**

- The average shirt lasts 2-3 years with normal wear
- With aggressive repairs, it can last 10-15 years

**Start practicing now:**
- Sew a button back on
- Patch a pair of jeans
- Darn a sock

**When stores close, you'll already know how to keep your family clothed.**
