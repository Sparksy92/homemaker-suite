# 17.2 Weatherproofing

**Premise:** You have a structure (house, cabin, or long-term shelter), but it's leaking heat, letting in drafts, or failing to keep out rain. Modern materials (caulk, foam, plastic sheeting) may not be available.

This section covers **field-expedient weatherproofing** using natural and scavenged materials.

---

## The Thermal Hierarchy: Where Heat Escapes

**Target these in order:**

1. **Roof** (25-30% of heat loss—rain/snow entry point)
2. **Windows** (15-25% of heat loss—drafts and radiation)
3. **Walls** (10-15% of heat loss—air infiltration)
4. **Floor** (10-15% of heat loss—ground contact)
5. **Doors** (5-10% of heat loss—gaps and drafts)

---

## Roof Repair (Without Modern Materials)

### Problem 1: Leaking Shingles/Tiles

**Temporary Patch:**
- **Pine pitch/tar:** Heat pine resin until liquid, brush over leak
- **Birch bark:** Peel

 large sheets, nail over damaged area (overlapping like shingles)
- **Mud plaster:** Mix clay-rich mud with straw, pack into cracks (works for flat roofs)

**Long-Term Fix:**
- **Sod roof:** Layer of soil + grass over waterproof membrane (birch bark, canvas, plastic)
  - 4-6 inches of soil provides insulation + weatherproofing
  - Grass roots hold soil in place

### Problem 2: Collapsed/Missing Section

**Emergency Tarp:**
1. Scavenge plastic sheeting, tarps, or canvas
2. Drape over damaged section, extending 2 feet beyond the leak
3. Weight down edges with rocks or nail to roof

**Natural Thatching:**
1. Layer evergreen boughs (pine, spruce, cedar) in overlapping rows
2. Start at the bottom, work upward (like shingling)
3. Thickness: 12-18 inches minimum
4. Secure with branches or cordage

## Window Insulation (Extreme Cold)

### Method 1: Plastic Sheeting (If Available)
**Indoor installation:**
1. Staple clear plastic sheeting to the interior window frame
2. Leave 1" air gap between plastic and glass (air = insulation)
3. Seal edges with tape or caulk
4. **Effect:** Creates double-pane effect, reduces heat loss by 30-50%

### Method 2: Fabric/Blanket (No Plastic)
1. Hang heavy blankets or quilts over windows at night (remove during day for light)
2. Secure with nails or tension rods
3. **Effect:** Blocks drafts, reduces radiation heat loss

### Method 3: Cloth + Mud (Permanent Seal)
**If you're willing to sacrifice light:**
1. Stuff rags or cloth into gaps around window frame
2. Cover with mud plaster (clay + straw + water)
3. **Result:** Fully sealed window (no light, but no heat loss)

### Method 4: Paper Inserts (Historical)
**Common in 19th-century homesteads:**
1. Cut cardboard or thick paper to window size
2. Insert between storm window and interior window
3. Remove in spring

## Draft Sealing (Walls and Doors)

### Finding Drafts
**Method 1: Smoke test**
- Light a candle or incense, walk around room
- Smoke movement = draft location

**Method 2: Hand test**
- On a windy day, run your hand along baseboards, door frames, and sockets
- Feel for cold air

### Sealing Methods

#### Natural Caulk (Mud + Fiber)
**Recipe:**
1. Mix clay-heavy soil with water (thick paste consistency)
2. Add chopped straw, grass, or animal hair (15-20% by volume)
3. Pack into cracks with a stick or fingers
4. Smooth with wet hand

**Dries hard, lasts months** (reapply seasonally).

#### Rope/Cloth Stuffing
1. Twist rags or rope into "snakes"
2. Push into gaps around doors and windows
3. For permanent sealing, cover with mud plaster

#### Door Draft Snake
1. Fill a long sock or cloth tube with rice, sand, or dry beans
2. Place at base of door
3. **Instant draft blocker**

## Heat Retention Strategies

### Thermal Mass (Passive Heating)
**Concept:** Materials that absorb heat during the day and release it at night.

**Implementation:**
1. **Rocks:** Stack dark-colored rocks near a sunny window or fire
   - They absorb heat when warm, radiate it when air cools
2. **Water barrels:** Fill 55-gallon drums with water, paint black
   - Place in sun during day, they release heat at night
3. **Mud/Adobe walls:** Thick earthen walls (12-18") absorb and store heat

### Room Subdivision (Reduce Heated Space)
**In extreme cold, heat only essential rooms:**
1. Close off unused rooms (bedrooms, storage)
2. Hang blankets in doorways to seal them
3. Focus heating efforts on 1-2 core rooms (kitchen, sleeping area)

**Effect:** Heating 500 sq ft vs. 2000 sq ft = 75% less fuel.

## Floor Insulation (Ground Heat Loss)

### Problem: Cold Floor = Cold Room
**Ground sucks heat faster than air.** Even if walls are insulated, a cold floor drains warmth.

### Solutions

#### Rug/Carpet Layers
- Lay rugs, blankets, or hay directly on the floor
- Multiple layers = better insulation

#### Raised Floor
1. Lay wooden pallets or 2x4 framework on floor
2. Fill gaps with straw, leaves, or crumpled newspaper
3. Cover with plywood or planks
4. **Effect:** Creates air gap + insulation layer

#### Earthen Floor Insulation
**If you have a dirt floor:**
1. Dig out 6-12 inches
2. Fill with dry sand or gravel (drainage layer)
3. Top with clay-straw mix (tamped hard)
4. **Optional:** Cover with flagstones or wood planks

## Window Replacement (When Glass is Broken)

### Temporary Solutions

**Option 1: Plastic + Frame**
1. Cut heavy-duty plastic sheeting to size
2. Staple to exterior window frame
3. Reinforce with wooden slats nailed over edges

**Option 2: Plywood Board**
- Cut plywood to window size
- Nail over opening
- **Sacrifices light, but stops wind/rain**

**Option 3: Translucent Animal Membrane (Historical)**
- Scrape and stretch animal bladders, intestines, or parchment
- Tack to frame
- **Lets in light, blocks wind** (used in medieval Europe)

## Rain Gutter Management (Prevents Foundation Rot)

### Problem: Water Pooling Near Foundation
**Rain runoff erodes foundation, rots wooden structures.**

### DIY Gutter System
**Materials:** Halved PVC pipe, bamboo, or carved wooden troughs

**Installation:**
1. Attach trough along roof edge (slight downward slope)
2. Direct runoff away from structure (10+ feet)
3. Use rocks or splash blocks to diffuse water at discharge point

**No gutters?**
- Dig a shallow trench around the structure (6" deep, 6" wide)
- Line with rocks
- Directs water away from foundation

---

## Makeshift Vapor Barrier (Prevents Moisture Buildup)

### Why It Matters
**Warm, moist air hits cold walls → condensation → mold, rot.**

### Solution: Interior Vapor Barrier
1. Staple plastic sheeting or tarp to the warm side of walls
2. Seal seams with tape or mud
3. **Effect:** Prevents moisture from soaking into walls

**If no plastic:**
- Paint walls with oil-based paint or linseed oil (creates moisture barrier)
- Hang canvas or oilcloth

## Insulation Materials (In Order of Effectiveness)

| Material | R-Value* | Availability | Flammability | Notes |
|----------|----------|--------------|--------------|-------|
| **Straw bales** | High | Farms | High | Excellent, but fire risk |
| **Dry leaves** | Medium | Woods | High | Compresses, needs replacing |
| **Wool/animal fur** | High | Farms | Low | Best if you have access |
| **Sawdust** | Medium | Mills | Medium | Free, but messy |
| **Newspaper (crumpled)** | Medium | Urban salvage | High | Readily available |
| **Mud + straw (adobe)** | Low-Med | Natural | None | Permanent, thermal mass |

*R-value = thermal resistance (higher = better insulation)

## The 30-Day Weatherproofing Plan

**If you have one month before winter hits:**

### Week 1: Roof
- Patch all leaks
- Add extra layer (tarp, bark, thatch)

### Week 2: Windows
- Seal gaps with caulk/mud
- Add plastic or fabric insulation

### Week 3: Walls + Doors
- Find and seal drafts
- Add door draft snakes

### Week 4: Floor + Heat Retention
- Insulate floor
- Set up thermal mass (rocks, water)
- Subdivide space if needed

---

## Testing Effectiveness

**Before and after comparison:**
1. **Temperature test:** Measure indoor temp at sunrise (coldest time)
   - Improvement of 5-10°F = successful weatherproofing
2. **Draft test:** Light candles in multiple locations
   - Flames should be steady (not flickering)
3. **Fuel test:** Track how much firewood you use per day
   - Good weatherproofing = 30-50% less fuel

## Emergency Insulation (When You Have Nothing)

**Stuff walls with whatever you can find:**
- Dry grass/hay
- Crumpled paper
- Cardboard (flattened boxes)
- Clothing you're not wearing
- Pine needles
- Cattail fluff

**Even poor insulation beats no insulation.**

---

## The Bottom Line

**Weatherproofing = force multiplier for survival.**

- A leaky shelter with a fire = you're burning firewood to heat the outdoors
- A sealed shelter with a fire = you're warm with 1/3 the fuel

**Invest the time to weatherproof. Your future self (and your firewood pile) will thank you.**
