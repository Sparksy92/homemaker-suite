# 17.3 Long Term Shelter: Beyond the Debris Hut

**Concept:** A debris hut keeps you alive for a week. A cabin keeps you alive for a decade. Long-term shelter requires managing moisture, insulation, and durability.

## 1. The Log Cabin (Traditional)
*   **Pros:** Bulletproof, lasts 100+ years, uses on-site materials.
*   **Cons:** Extremely labor intensive, requires large trees.
*   **Key Principle:** The "Sill Log" (bottom log) must be off the ground. Place it on a stone foundation to prevent rot.
*   **Chinking:** The gaps between logs. Fill with a mix of clay, sand, and straw (like cob).

## 2. Timber Framing (Efficient)
Instead of stacking whole logs, build a skeleton.
*   **Pros:** Uses fewer trees than a log cabin. Allows for insulation (straw bale, wattle & daub) between the posts.
*   **Joinery:** Mortise and Tenon. No nails required. Wood pegs hold the frame together.
*   **Infill:**
    *   *Wattle & Daub:* Weave sticks (wattle) and plaster with mud/straw (daub).
    *   *Slip-Straw:* Pack loose straw coated in clay slip into forms. High insulation value.

## 3. Earthbag Construction (The Bunker)
*   **Method:** Fill polypropylene feed bags with damp soil/clay. Stack them like bricks. Barbed wire between layers locks them together.
*   **Pros:** Bulletproof, fireproof, massive thermal mass (stays cool in summer, holds heat in winter).
*   **Cons:** Labor intensive filling bags.
*   **Shape:** Domes are strongest, but round houses are hard to roof with conventional materials.

## 4. The Dugout / Sod House (The Prairie Solution)
*   **Concept:** Use the earth as insulation.
*   **Method:** Dig into a south-facing hillside. The earth provides 3 walls. Build a roof over the front.
*   **Warning:** Drainage is critical. If you don't engineer water diversion (swales uphill), you are building a swimming pool, not a house.

## 5. Site Selection (Permanent)
For a permanent home, the criteria change from emergency shelter.
*   **Solar Gain:** South-facing exposure (Northern Hemisphere) is non-negotiable for passive heating.
*   **Water Access:** You can carry water for a week. You cannot carry it forever. Build near (but not *in*) the floodplain.
*   **Resources:** Build where the materials are. hauling logs a mile is miserable.
