# 8.2 Dietary Restrictions & Allergies

In a survival or homesteading scenario, dietary restrictions aren't just inconveniences—they can be life-threatening issues that affect calorie intake and morale. Managing them requires foresight and adaptation.

---

## Lactose Intolerance

Lactose intolerance is the inability to digest lactose, the sugar found in milk. In a self-reliant setting where dairy animals (goats, cows) are common assets, this can be a challenge.

### The Mechanism
It's not an allergy; it's an enzyme deficiency (lactase). Unlike an allergy, small amounts might be tolerated, and processed dairy is often safe.

### Living with Dairy Animals
If you have a cow or goat but can't drink the milk:
1.  **Fermentation is Key:** Bacteria eat lactose during fermentation.
    *   **Yogurt:** 24-hour fermented yogurt has almost no lactose left.
    *   **Kefir:** Similar to yogurt but drinkable; the grains consume the lactose.
    *   **Hard Cheese:** Cheddar, Parmesan, and Swiss have virtually zero lactose. The whey (liquid) holds the lactose, and that is drained off.
2.  **Ghee (Clarified Butter):** Boiling butter and skimming the solids removes the lactose and casein, leaving pure fat. It is shelf-stable and safe for most intolerant people.
3.  **Goat Milk:** Contains slightly less lactose than cow's milk and is easier to digest for some, but not all.

### Non-Dairy Alternatives
If you cannot tolerate even fermented dairy, you need substitutes for cooking and nutrition.
*   **Oat Milk:** Easy to make. Soak oats in water, blend, strain. Good for baking.
*   **Nut Milks:** Hazelnut or walnut milk (if foraging). High fat content, good for coffee/tea.
*   **Oil/Lard:** Use olive oil, bacon grease, or lard in place of butter for frying.

## Gluten & Wheat Issues

Wheat is a staple prep item, but Celiac disease or gluten sensitivity requires strict avoidance.

### Safe Starches
*   **Corn:** Polenta, grits, cornbread.
*   **Potatoes:** High yield, easy to grow.
*   **Rice:** Excellent storage life.
*   **Acorns:** (Foraged) Leached acorn flour is naturally gluten-free and nutty.
*   **Buckwheat:** Despite the name, it's not wheat. It's a seed related to rhubarb. Grows fast in poor soil.

### Cross-Contamination
If someone has Celiac, you cannot share:
*   Cast iron skillets (porous, holds gluten)
*   Wooden spoons/cutting boards
*   Toasters or grinders

## Managing Severe Allergies (Anaphylaxis)

If someone has a life-threatening allergy (peanuts, bee stings, shellfish) and you are off-grid:

1.  **Avoidance is the Only Cure:** There is no EpiPen tree. 
2.  **Benadryl (Diphenhydramine):** Stockpile heavily. It won't stop throat closing like Epinephrine, but it helps with systemic reactions.
3.  **Identify "Hidden" Sources:**
    *   *Lupin* flour (common in gluten-free goods) triggers peanut allergies.
    *   *Medicinal plants:* Echinacea (ragweed family), Willow bark (aspirin allergy).
