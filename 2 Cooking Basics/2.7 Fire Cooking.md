# 2.7 Fire Cooking

## The Foundation: Understanding Your Heat Source

Unlike a stove with a dial, fire cooking requires reading and managing your heat source. Success comes from understanding the difference between **flame** (high, direct heat) and **coals** (sustained, even heat).

### The Coal Bed - Your True Cooking Surface

**Key Principle:** You don't cook over flames—you cook over coals.

- **Active flames** = 700-1000°F, uneven, burns food
- **Red hot coals** = 400-600°F, steady, perfect for most cooking
- **Gray ash-covered coals** = 300-400°F, gentle heat for simmering

**Building a proper coal bed:**
1. Start your fire 30-45 minutes before cooking
2. Let hardwood (oak, hickory, maple) burn down to coals
3. Rake coals into a concentrated bed
4. Add wood to the side to maintain fresh coals

## Dutch Oven Mastery

The Dutch oven is the workhorse of fire cooking. Success is all about **coal placement**.

### The 3-to-1 Rule (Baking)
For breads, biscuits, cobblers (things that need top browning):
- **Top:** 2/3 of your coals
- **Bottom:** 1/3 of your coals

**Example for 350°F in a 12" oven:**
- Top: 16 coals
- Bottom: 8 coals
- **Total: 24 coals**

### The 1-to-1 Rule (Roasting/Stewing)
For meats, stews, beans (things that need bottom heat):
- **Top:** 1/2 of your coals
- **Bottom:** 1/2 of your coals

**Example for 325°F in a 12" oven:**
- Top: 10 coals
- Bottom: 11 coals
- **Total: 21 coals**

### Temperature by Coal Count (12" Dutch Oven)
| Temp | Top | Bottom | Total |
|------|-----|--------|-------|
| 325°F | 15 | 6 | 21 |
| 350°F | 16 | 8 | 24 |
| 375°F | 17 | 10 | 27 |
| 400°F | 18 | 12 | 30 |

**Important Notes:**
- Rotate the oven 1/4 turn every 10 minutes
- Rotate the lid 1/4 turn (opposite direction) every 10 minutes
- Prevents hot spots and ensures even cooking

## Open Fire Temperature Zones

When cooking directly over coals (no oven), use the **hand test**:

| Hold hand 6" above coals | Seconds you can hold | Temperature | Use for |
|--------------------------|---------------------|-------------|---------|
| 1-2 seconds | High heat | 450-500°F | Searing steaks |
| 3-4 seconds | Medium-high | 375-450°F | Frying, sautéing |
| 5-6 seconds | Medium | 325-375°F | Grilling vegetables |
| 7-8 seconds | Low | 250-325°F | Simmering sauces |

## The Keyhole Fire Layout

The most versatile fire design for cooking:

```
[Active Fire]  →  [Coal Bed]
     |               |
   Wood pile      Cooking zone
```

**Setup:**
1. One side has the active fire (fresh wood burning)
2. As it burns, rake hot coals to the adjacent flat area
3. Cook over the coal bed
4. Feed the fire as needed to maintain coal supply

**Advantages:**
- Continuous coal supply
- Multiple heat zones
- Can add wood without disturbing cooking

## Cast Iron Care in the Field

**Seasoning on the fire:**
1. Coat inside with lard or bacon fat
2. Place upside down over hot coals for 1 hour
3. Let cool completely
4. Repeat 2-3 times for new pans

**Cleaning:**
- Scrape while still warm
- Wipe with oil
- Heat gently to lock in seasoning
- **Never use soap** (unless you're reseasoning)

## Emergency Cooking Without Cookware

### Rock Frying
1. Find a flat, smooth stone (avoid river rocks—they can explode)
2. Heat in coals for 30 minutes
3. Oil surface
4. Cook eggs, flatbreads, or thin meat directly on the stone

### Plank Cooking
1. Soak a hardwood plank (cedar, oak) for 1 hour
2. Pin fish or meat to the plank with wooden skewers
3. Prop plank at 45° angle next to (not over) the fire
4. Cook slowly, rotating as needed

## Safety Protocols

- **Gloves:** Leather welding gloves for handling hot metal
- **Pot lifter:** Carry a specialized Dutch oven lid lifter (or use pliers)
- **Shovel:** For moving coals and burying fires
- **Water bucket:** Always within arm's reach when fire is active
- **Perimeter:** Clear 10 feet of dry grass/leaves around fire

**Extinguishing:**
1. Let fire burn down to ash if possible (saves water)
2. Spread coals out
3. Drown with water
4. Stir and repeat
5. Touch test—should be cool to the touch before leaving

---

## The 48-Hour Rule

**If you're cooking for sustained periods (multi-day bug-out, camping):**
- Maintain a constant small coal bed rather than restarting fires
- Bank coals overnight under ash (they'll last 8-12 hours)
- In the morning, add tinder and blow gently—instant restart

This saves matches, lighters, and energy.
