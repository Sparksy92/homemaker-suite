# 2.4 Flavor Balancing: How to Fix a Dish

## The 5 Tastes
Your tongue detects five things. A great dish hits most or all of them.
1.  **Salty:** Enhances all other flavors.
2.  **Sweet:** Balances acid and heat.
3.  **Sour (Acid):** Cuts fat, brightens dull flavors.
4.  **Bitter:** Adds complexity (coffee, dark chocolate, kale).
5.  **Umami:** Savory depth (mushrooms, meat, soy sauce, parmesan).

## How to Taste & Adjust
Dip a spoon. Taste. Ask: "What is missing?"

### Problem: "It tastes flat / boring."
*   **Solution 1:** Add **Salt**. (Most common).
*   **Solution 2:** Add **Acid**. A squeeze of lemon or splash of vinegar "wakes up" soups and sauces.

### Problem: "It's too salty."
*   **Solution:** Dilute. Add more water, broth, or unsalted ingredients (potatoes/rice).
*   *Myth:* Putting a potato in does *not* suck the salt out; it just absorbs some salty liquid (along with everything else). Adding volume is the only true fix.

### Problem: "It's too spicy/hot."
*   **Solution:** Add **Fat** or **Sweetness**. Dairy (yogurt, cream) neutralizes capsaicin. Sugar/Honey masks the heat.
*   **Acid** can also help cut the burn.

### Problem: "It's too rich/heavy."
*   **Solution:** Add **Acid**. Vinegar in a fatty pork dish, lime on a taco, pickles with a burger. The acid "cuts" the grease on the palate.

### Problem: "It's too sour/acidic."
*   **Solution:** Add **Sweet** or **Fat**. Caramelized onions, honey, or a pat of butter will round off the sharp edges of tomato sauce.

## Texture (The 6th Element)
Flavor isn't just taste; it's mouthfeel.
*   **Contrast:** Creamy soup needs crunchy croutons. Soft pasta needs a chunky sauce. Salad needs nuts.
*   **Variety:** Every bite should be slightly different.

## The Secret Weapons (Umami Bombs)
If a vegetarian or budget dish lacks "meatiness," add:
*   Soy Sauce / Tamari
*   Tomato Paste (browned)
*   Mushrooms
*   Parmesan Rind (simmered in soup)
*   Fish Sauce (in small amounts, it doesn't taste fishy, just savory)
*   Nutritional Yeast
