# 14.2 Wound Care

**Critical Premise:** Modern medicine prioritizes antibiotics, sutures, and sterile supplies. In their absence, you rely on principles that predate modern medicine—but still work.

---

## The 4 Pillars of Wound Care

1. **Stop the bleeding**
2. **Clean the wound**
3. **Prevent infection**
4. **Promote healing**

---

## Step 1: Stop the Bleeding

### For Minor Cuts/Scrapes
**Direct pressure is your first tool.**

1. Apply clean cloth or bandage directly to wound
2. Press firmly for 5-10 minutes (don't peek—lifting pressure restarts bleeding)
3. Elevate above heart level if possible
4. Once bleeding stops, move to Step 2

**If bleeding continues:**
- Add more cloth on top (don't remove soaked layers—disturbs clotting)
- Apply pressure for another 10 minutes
- Use **yarrow** (fresh leaf, crushed and applied) to accelerate clotting

### For Severe Bleeding (Arterial)
**Arterial blood = bright red, spurting with heartbeat. This is life-threatening.**

1. **Direct pressure first** (same as above, but maintain pressure for 15+ minutes)
2. **Pressure points** (if direct pressure fails):
   - **Arm bleeding:** Press brachial artery (inside of upper arm, midway between shoulder and elbow)
   - **Leg bleeding:** Press femoral artery (groin crease, where leg meets torso)
3. **Tourniquet (last resort only):**
   - Use 2-3" wide band (belt, cloth strip—never rope or wire)
   - Place 2-3" above wound (between wound and heart)
   - Tighten until bleeding stops
   - Note the time (tourniquets can only stay on for ~2 hours before tissue damage)
   - **Do not remove until professional help arrives**

**Key principle:** Most bleeding stops with direct pressure. Tourniquets are for "bleeding to death" scenarios only.

---

## Step 2: Clean the Wound

**Goal:** Remove dirt, debris, and bacteria without damaging tissue.

### With Running Water
**Best case scenario:**

1. Rinse wound under clean running water for 5 minutes (lukewarm, not hot)
2. Use gentle pressure (imagine rinsing lettuce, not scrubbing a floor)
3. If debris remains, use tweezers (sterilized in boiling water or flame) to remove visible particles
4. Rinse again

**Rule:** If you can see dirt, remove it. Embedded debris causes infection.

### Without Running Water

**Option 1: Saline solution (homemade)**
- Mix 1 tsp salt in 1 cup boiled (then cooled) water
- Pour over wound to irrigate
- **Why:** Matches body's salinity, less irritating than plain water

**Option 2: Herbal antiseptic wash**
- Calendula or yarrow tea (strong, cooled)
- Pour over wound
- **Why:** Antimicrobial properties

**Option 3: Honey (yes, really)**
- If nothing else is available, rinse with cleanest water you have
- Apply raw honey directly to wound (see Step 3)

**Never use:** Peroxide or alcohol on open wounds (kills healthy tissue, impairs healing). Only use these to sterilize tools.

---

## Step 3: Prevent Infection

**Infection is the real killer.** A clean wound heals; an infected one can cause sepsis.

### Natural Antiseptics (When Modern Supplies Are Unavailable)

#### 1. Raw Honey (The Ancient Antibiotic)
**Why it works:** Honey is hygroscopic (draws moisture out, bacteria can't survive), low pH (acidic = antimicrobial), contains enzymes that produce hydrogen peroxide.

**How to use:**
1. Apply a thick layer (1/8") directly to clean wound
2. Cover with clean bandage
3. Change bandage daily, reapply honey
4. **Best honey:** Raw, unfiltered (Manuka honey is strongest, but any raw honey works)

**Evidence:** Used in hospitals for diabetic ulcers and burns. Proven to outperform some antibiotics.

---

#### 2. Garlic (Nature's Broad-Spectrum Antibiotic)
**Why it works:** Allicin (released when garlic is crushed) is antimicrobial, antifungal, antiviral.

**How to use:**
1. Crush 2-3 cloves into a paste
2. Mix with a small amount of oil or honey (straight garlic burns skin)
3. Apply to wound, cover with bandage
4. Change every 12 hours

**Caution:** Garlic can irritate skin—dilute well, test on small area first.

---

#### 3. Yarrow or Calendula (Gentle + Effective)
**How to use:**
1. Make a strong tea (2 tbsp dried herb per cup of water)
2. Let cool
3. Soak a clean cloth in tea, apply as a compress
4. Change 2-3 times daily

**Effect:** Antimicrobial, promotes tissue growth.

---

#### 4. Salt Water (Simple, Effective)
**How to use:**
1. Dissolve 1 tsp salt in 1 cup warm boiled water
2. Soak wound for 10 minutes, 2-3 times daily
3. **Effect:** Draws out infection, keeps wound clean

**Use for:** Shallow wounds, punctures, infected areas.

---

### Bandaging

**Goal:** Protect wound from contamination while allowing it to breathe (some air circulation promotes healing).

**Steps:**
1. Cover wound with a clean cloth (boiled and dried, or from a sealed package)
2. Secure with tape or ties (not too tight—restricts blood flow)
3. Change daily or whenever bandage gets wet/dirty

**When to leave unbandaged:**
- Once a scab forms (scabs are nature's bandage)
- If bandage material is questionable (dirty bandage = worse than no bandage)

## Step 4: Promote Healing

### Nutrition for Wound Healing
**Your body needs raw materials to repair tissue:**

| Nutrient | Why It Matters | Food Sources |
|----------|----------------|--------------|
| **Protein** | Rebuilds tissue | Meat, eggs, beans, nuts |
| **Vitamin C** | Collagen production | Citrus, pine needle tea, rose hips |
| **Zinc** | Immune function, tissue repair | Meat, pumpkin seeds |
| **Vitamin A** | Skin cell growth | Liver, carrots, dandelion greens |

**Minimum:** 1.5x normal protein intake while healing a wound.

---

### Wound Closure (When You Can't Stitch)

**Small, clean cuts (<1" long, not deep):**
- Use **butterfly bandages** or improvise with tape:
  1. Cut thin strips of tape
  2. Pull edges of wound together
  3. Apply tape perpendicular to cut (holds edges closed)
  4. Change tape when it loosens

**Larger wounds:**
- **Historically:** Flour paste, egg whites, or pine resin were used to "glue" wounds shut
- **Modern improvisation:** Superglue (cyanoacrylate) is used in emergencies (originally developed as a wound adhesive)
  - Apply sparingly to wound edges (not inside), pinch closed
  - **Caution:** Only for clean cuts, not punctures or infected wounds

**When sutures are critical:** Deep gashes, facial wounds, or wounds that won't close on their own require professional stitching when possible.

## Signs of Infection (When to Escalate Care)

**Watch for these red flags:**

| Sign | What It Means | Action |
|------|---------------|--------|
| **Red streaks from wound** | Spreading infection (lymphangitis) | Severe—seek help immediately |
| **Increasing redness/warmth** | Infection starting | Increase cleaning, apply antiseptic |
| **Pus (yellow/green)** | Bacterial infection | Clean more frequently, use honey/garlic |
| **Foul smell** | Necrotic tissue | Debride (remove dead tissue—advanced skill) |
| **Fever, chills** | Systemic infection | Antibiotics needed—seek professional help |
| **Numbness/tingling** | Nerve damage or compartment syndrome | Medical emergency |

**Golden rule:** If it's getting worse instead of better after 48 hours, escalate treatment.

## Wound Types and Specific Care

### Puncture Wounds (Nails, Thorns, Bites)
**Danger:** Deep, narrow = hard to clean, prone to infection (especially tetanus).

**Care:**
1. **Don't seal shut** (traps bacteria inside)
2. Soak in warm salt water 3x daily (draws out infection)
3. Apply honey, leave uncovered or lightly covered
4. Watch closely for infection

---

### Burns

**1st degree (red, no blisters):**
- Cool with cold water for 10 minutes
- Apply aloe vera or calendula salve
- No bandage needed

**2nd degree (blisters):**
- Cool with cold water
- **Don't pop blisters** (natural barrier against infection)
- Apply honey or calendula salve
- Cover lightly with non-stick bandage
- Change daily

**3rd degree (white/charred skin):**
- Cool gently
- Cover with clean, moist cloth
- **Seek professional help** (high infection risk, fluid loss)

**Never use:** Butter, oils, ice (makes burns worse).

---

### Animal Bites
**Danger:** Puncture + bacteria from animal's mouth = high infection risk. Rabies risk with wild animals.

**Care:**
1. Wash immediately with soap and water for 15 minutes (even if it restarts bleeding)
2. Rinse with saline or antiseptic wash
3. Apply honey or garlic paste
4. **Do not close** (let drain)
5. **Seek medical help** for rabies evaluation if from wild animal

---

## Field Sterilization (Making Tools Safe)

**Boiling:**
- 10 minutes at a rolling boil (kills bacteria, viruses)
- Use for bandages, cloths, tweezers

**Flame:**
- Hold metal tool (knife, needle) in flame until red hot
- Let cool before use
- **Use for:** Tweezers, knife blades, needles

**Alcohol:**
- 70% isopropyl alcohol (lower concentrations less effective)
- Soak for 5 minutes, air dry
- **Use for:** Thermometers, scissors, skin prep

## The Wound Care Kit (Grid-Down Essentials)

**If you can only have 10 items:**

1. **Clean cloth** (boiled, sealed in plastic)
2. **Salt** (for saline wash)
3. **Raw honey** (antiseptic, shelf-stable for years)
4. **Yarrow** (dried or fresh, for bleeding)
5. **Calendula salve** (antimicrobial, healing)
6. **Garlic** (fresh or powder, antibiotic)
7. **Tweezers** (splinters, debris removal)
8. **Scissors** (cutting bandages, cloth)
9. **Tape or strips of cloth** (securing bandages)
10. **Needle and thread** (silk or fishing line if needed for sutures—sterilize first)

**Total cost:** <$30  
**Capabilities:** Treat 90% of wounds you'll encounter.

---

## The Bottom Line

**Modern wound care = sterile, complex.**  
**Field wound care = clean, simple, vigilant.**

**Your advantages:**
- Time (you can clean and redress wounds multiple times daily)
- Observation (you'll notice infections early)
- Nature's pharmacy (honey, herbs, salt are proven antiseptics)

**Your mission:** Stop bleeding, keep it clean, watch for infection. The body does the rest.
