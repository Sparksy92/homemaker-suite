# 18.3 Off-Grid Heating: Surviving the Freeze

**Concept:** In modern homes, the furnace is the heart. When it stops, the house dies. In a survival home, the *hearth* is the heart.

## 1. Passive Solar (The Free Heat)
Before you burn fuel, use the sun.
*   **Thermal Mass:** Stone, concrete, or water barrels placed in direct sunlight inside the house. They absorb heat during the day and release it at night.
*   **Greenhouse Effect:** South-facing windows let heat in. Insulated curtains at night keep it there.

## 2. Wood Stoves (The Workhorse)
*   **Cast Iron vs. Steel:** Cast iron holds heat longer; steel heats up faster.
*   **The Flue:** A clean chimney is non-negotiable. Creosote buildup causes chimney fires (which burn at 2000°F and destroy houses).
*   **Efficiency:** Modern EPA stoves re-burn gases, getting 30% more heat from the same log.
*   **Pot Belly Stoves:** Inefficient (30-40%). Most heat goes up the chimney. Good for a workshop, bad for a home.

## 3. Rocket Mass Heaters (The Efficiency King)
*   **Concept:** A high-temperature, fast burn that channels exhaust horizontally through a massive bench (cob/stone) before exiting.
*   **Efficiency:** Uses 80-90% less wood than a standard stove.
*   **Safety:** Exhaust is almost cool steam. No creosote.
*   **Drawback:** Heavy. Cannot be installed on a wooden floor (needs a slab).

## 4. Masonry Heaters (The European Method)
*   **Concept:** Like a rocket heater, but built upright. A massive stone fireplace with twisting channels inside.
*   **Usage:** Burn one hot fire for 2 hours. The stone radiates heat for the next 24 hours.

## 5. Micro-Zone Heating
*   **Don't heat the house.** Heat the person.
*   **The "Tent-in-a-Room":** If fuel is critically low, set up a camping tent inside the living room. Your body heat will warm the small volume.
*   **Hot Water Bottles:** A stone or water bottle heated by the fire and placed in a sleeping bag stays warm for hours.
