# 1.3 Budget Grocery Framework: Eating Better for Less

## The Strategy: Reverse Meal Planning
Most people plan meals first ("I want lasagna"), then buy ingredients. This is expensive because you buy at full price.
**The Budget Way:**
1.  **Check Sales:** What is the "loss leader" this week? (e.g., Chicken thighs are $0.99/lb).
2.  **Check Pantry:** What do I already have? (e.g., Pasta, canned tomatoes).
3.  **Plan Meal:** "I will make Chicken Cacciatore."

## The Price Book
Stop trusting sale signs. Create a "Price Book" (mental or physical) of the absolute bottom price for your staples.
*   *Example:* Ground Beef.
    *   Regular: $5.99/lb
    *   Sale: $3.99/lb
    *   Stock-Up Price: $2.99/lb
*   **Action:** When it hits $2.99, buy 10 lbs and freeze it. When it is $5.99, buy none.

## Unit Price is King
Always look at the small print on the shelf tag: **Price Per Ounce/Unit**.
*   A "Sale" on 12oz cheese might still be more expensive per ounce than the regular price 2lb block.
*   **Bulk isn't always cheaper:** Sometimes buying 3 small bags is cheaper than 1 big one. Do the math.

## The Buying Hierarchy
Where to shop for maximum savings:
1.  **Salvage Grocers / Outlets:** "Dent and scratch," near-expiry goods. (Savings: 50-80%)
2.  **Ethnic Markets:** Asian/Latino markets often have significantly cheaper produce, spices, and rice than big-box stores. (Savings: 30-50%)
3.  **Discount Chains (Aldi/Lidl/WinCo):** Low overhead, limited selection. (Savings: 20-30%)
4.  **Bulk Stores (Costco/Sam's):** Good for select items (dairy, rotisserie chicken, gas), can be trap for impulse buys.
5.  **Conventional Supermarkets:** Shop ONLY the perimeter (produce/meat) and loss leaders.
6.  **Convenience Stores/Gas Stations:** Never. (Markup: 200%+)

## 10 Cheap Superfoods
Build your diet around these high-nutrition, low-cost pillars:
1.  **Eggs:** The perfect protein.
2.  **Oats:** Heart healthy, filling.
3.  **Lentils:** Fast cooking, iron-rich.
4.  **Cabbage:** Vitamin C, lasts weeks in fridge.
5.  **Bananas:** Potassium, cheapest fruit.
6.  **Frozen Spinach:** Nutrient dense, zero waste.
7.  **Potatoes:** Vitamin C, potassium, satiety.
8.  **Whole Carrots:** Cheap, versatile, durable.
9.  **Peanut Butter:** High calorie energy.
10. **Sardines:** Omega-3s, calcium, shelf stable.

## Reducing Waste
The most expensive food is the food you throw away.
*   **"Must Go" Dinner:** Once a week, cook a meal using only perishables that are about to expire. Soup, stir-fry, and frittatas are perfect for this.
