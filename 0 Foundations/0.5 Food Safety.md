# 0.5 Food Safety: The First Responsibility

## The Danger Zone
Bacteria grow most rapidly between **40°F (4°C) and 140°F (60°C)**.
*   **Rule:** Do not leave perishable food in the Danger Zone for more than **2 hours** (1 hour if the ambient temperature is above 90°F).
*   **Action:** Cool leftovers rapidly. Do not put a giant pot of hot chili directly in the fridge (it heats up the fridge). Portion it into smaller, shallow containers to cool quickly.

## Minimum Internal Temperatures
Use your instant-read thermometer. Color is not a reliable indicator of doneness.
*   **Poultry (Chicken, Turkey, Duck):** 165°F (74°C)
*   **Ground Meats (Beef, Pork, Lamb):** 160°F (71°C)
*   **Whole Cuts (Beef, Pork, Lamb):** 145°F (63°C) + 3 minute rest
*   **Fish:** 145°F (63°C) or until opaque and separates easily
*   **Leftovers:** Reheat to 165°F (74°C)

## Cross-Contamination
The spread of pathogens from raw food to ready-to-eat food.
1.  **Boards:** ideally, use separate cutting boards for raw meat (plastic, bleachable) and produce (wood). If using one, prep produce *first*, then meat.
2.  **Hands:** Wash hands for 20 seconds with hot soapy water after touching raw meat, eggs, or soil.
3.  **Utensils:** Never put cooked meat back on the same plate that held raw meat without washing it first.

## Storage Safety (The Fridge Hierarchy)
Organize the fridge to prevent raw juices from dripping onto other foods.
*   **Top Shelf:** Ready-to-eat foods (leftovers, drinks, yogurt).
*   **Middle Shelf:** Raw vegetables and fruits (if not in drawer).
*   **Bottom Shelf:** Raw whole meats (fish, steaks, chops).
*   **Very Bottom:** Raw ground meats and poultry (highest risk of dripping and highest bacterial load).

## Botulism & Preservation
*   **The Risk:** *Clostridium botulinum* thrives in anaerobic (oxygen-free) environments, like canned goods and garlic-in-oil. It is odorless, tasteless, and deadly.
*   **The Fix:**
    *   **Acid:** High acid environments (pH < 4.6) prevent growth. This is why water bath canning is safe for pickles/fruits.
    *   **Heat:** Low acid foods (beans, corn, meat) MUST be pressure canned at 240°F+ to kill spores. Boiling water (212°F) is NOT hot enough.
    *   **Oil:** Store homemade garlic-in-oil or herb oils in the fridge and use within 4 days. Never store at room temperature.

## When in Doubt, Throw It Out
*   Smell is a good indicator, but not perfect.
*   If a can is bulging, leaking, or spurts liquid when opened: **DISCARD**. Do not taste test.
*   If mold is visible on soft food (bread, soft cheese, yogurt, jams): **DISCARD** the whole thing. Roots extend deeper than you can see.
*   (Exception: dense hard cheese or firm veggies like carrots—you can cut 1 inch around the mold and save the rest).
